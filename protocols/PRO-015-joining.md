# PRO-015 — Joining Numinia

> **Summary:** How a person who will work with Numen Games comes in: the
> agreement, the access, the welcome and the first piece of work.
> **Epistemic:** What has to happen before a new person can work here?
> **Pragmatic:** Bring someone in without forgetting an access, a signature
> or a welcome, and leave the list that lets them go cleanly later.
> **Audience:** Oracles · Agents

**Binds:** whoever brings a person into Numinia to work, paid or not.

---

## 1. Purpose and trigger

A person who comes in without a signed agreement, or with access nobody
wrote down, is a risk the day they leave. A person who comes in with access
and no welcome is a worker, not a citizen. This protocol does both halves
once, in order.

It starts when the Oracle agrees with a person that they will work with
Numen Games — as staff, as a freelancer or as a volunteer. The **Oracle**
who agreed it owns every step; an agent may prepare the documents and the
list, never grant access on its own.

---

## 2. Preconditions

- The Oracle's decision that the person joins, and in what form: staff,
  freelancer or volunteer.
- A place outside this repository for the person's records: contract,
  identity and bank details are personal data and never enter the archive
  (`STD-035`).
- The first piece of work the person will do, named in one sentence.

---

## 3. Procedure

1. **Sign the agreement.** Contract or service agreement, and the
   confidentiality clause, signed by both sides before any access.
2. **Collect what the law and payment need.** Identity, tax and bank
   details; for a freelancer, billing details. Keep them where the
   preconditions say, never in the tree.
3. **Open the access list.** One list per person, kept with their
   records: every system they are given (the GitHub organisation, the
   community server, shared drives, any provider), the date and who
   granted it. Leaving (`PRO-024`) revokes from this list; an access not
   on it is one nobody will remember to close.
4. **Grant the least access that lets them start.** Only what the first
   piece of work needs; widen it later, on the list. Secrets are handed
   through the vault, never in chat or a file (`STD-022`).
5. **Welcome them.** A first conversation with the Oracle: what Numinia
   is, how the work is organised, how they prefer to work and be reached.
   Point them to the site's front door and to `AGENTS.md` if they will
   work in a repository.
6. **Place them.** Their guild — what they know — and, if they choose one,
   their faction (`CAN-004`). Their rank starts at Nomad; the system reads
   it from what they do, it is never assigned by hand.
7. **Give them the first piece of work,** small enough to finish in their
   first two weeks, with what done looks like written down.
8. **Invite them to the rituals** (`PRO-019`): the weekly council and the
   coven. Attendance is an invitation, not a duty.
9. **Review at three months.** The Oracle and the person, together: what
   worked, what did not, what comes next. For a freelancer with an agreed
   path to staff, decide it here.

---

## 4. Verification

| Step | Evidence it completed |
|---|---|
| 1 | The signed agreement, dated before the first access on the list |
| 3–4 | The access list exists and every access on it has a date and a granter |
| 7 | The first piece of work, closed |
| 9 | The review held, with its date, in the person's records |

---

## 5. Escalation

Any access asked for before step 1 is signed waits. A person who needs
access to something reserved — money, a licence, the brand mark, a secret
beyond their work — needs the Oracle's word for that access, recorded on
the list. If the agreement cannot be signed as drafted, stop and bring it
to the Oracle; the person does not start on a promise.
