# The scene before the scene — for a national police force's training academy

*Draft for the first meeting. Written in English, the house's original; the
client receives the Spanish translation. Not yet sent: sending it is the
Oracle's approval, and the record will say when and by whom.*

## 1. Objectives

When it is over, a trainee arriving at a crime scene knows which pieces of
evidence are there, what is done with each one, and in which order the
protocol says it is done — and has already done it, several times, in a
place where getting it wrong cost nothing.

For classes of ten to twenty future officers at the academy, entering from
the academy's computers, from their own phones, or from headsets; with a
link and nothing to install. As one more training format beside the ones the
academy already uses for this purpose, for the academy to use freely during
the period agreed.

## 2. Why us

- **You can walk it today.** A ten-to-twenty-minute rehearsal of one crime
  scene is built and can be walked at the first meeting — from a phone, a
  computer or a headset.
- **We have done this before.** A large retailer's first day, rehearsed
  before the first day: lockers, contract, floor rules, till — the same
  method applied to a different procedure. Client unnamed until it agrees to
  be.
- **Who builds it.** Numen Games designs places where a procedure is learnt
  by walking it: narrative and game design, 3D production, browser-based
  delivery. The space is built on a browser engine, so the academy needs no
  software, no licence and no installation: a browser and a link.
- **What we need from you.** The protocol as the academy teaches it — the
  evidence types, the order, what counts as an error — and one instructor
  who can answer questions while we build.

## 3. How it teaches, and how it measures

One scene, three moments, ten to twenty minutes:

| Moment | What the trainee does | What is checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Arriving** | Secures the scene: perimeter, access, who enters | the order of the first acts |
| **Reading** | Finds the evidence: what is there, what each piece is | what is found, what is missed |
| **Collecting** | Handles each piece as the protocol says, in the protocol's order | the sequence, per piece; the errors |

A guide accompanies the trainee and says when something is done out of
order or missed; nothing is lost but a minute. The scene can be walked as
many times as the trainee wants.

**Level:** learning — **Indicator:** sequence errors per walk, each trainee's
first walk against their last. The space records every walk on its own; the
instructor reads each trainee's record and the class's.

**What each trainee leaves with:** their record of walks — the sequence
they followed each time, what they found, what they missed, how they
improved.

The next level, if the academy measures it: errors in the academy's own
practical exercises after the rehearsal, against classes that did not
rehearse.
