# OPP-2026-004 — a startup and investment event, 3,000+ attendees

> **Summary:** A large startup and investment event, over 3,000 attendees
> and 100 investors and partners. The house proposed an experiential side
> event in three tiers and two delivery models; the recommended one,
> Signature in co-production, at a launch price of 23,200 EUR + tax. Lost:
> the organiser judged its mainly institutional audience would not value
> it, and pointed at a Web3 community abroad as a better audience.
> **Epistemic:** Why was it lost, and what does it teach about whom the
> offer is for?
> **Pragmatic:** Closed. Read it before proposing to an institutional
> audience; a new approach is a new record that names this one.
> **Audience:** Sales · Oracles

## Need

Reconstructed from the proposal sent.

- **Objectives.** Connections that survive the event, intelligence on the
  ecosystem the organiser can show sponsors, and a side event unlike the
  others of its week.
- **Participants.** Over 3,000 attendees; over 100 investors and partners.
- **Conditions.** A side event within the main event's week, or around a
  startup contest before it; the organiser's venue and catering, or the
  house's.
- **What the responsible will see.** Sponsor renewal — "if the report
  helps renew one sponsor, the experience has paid for itself": a
  **results** level, not agreed with the client.

## Notes

- 2026-09-29. **Lost: not a fit — for this audience.** The organiser did
  not think a mainly institutional audience would appreciate the format;
  they thought a Web3 community, in Argentina, would receive it better.
  The door is open for next year. The lesson for the offer: **who the
  audience is** belongs in qualification — an institutional audience and a
  community audience are different buyers of the same format.
- The proposal introduced what the earlier two did not: three tiers of
  depth, two delivery models, payment by milestones and a launch discount
  in exchange for documenting the case. They go to the offer record.
- The original is a designed PDF, kept outside the archive: it names the
  client and a third-party artist, and the client was not told the house
  works in the open. By sector for good, as every lost record.
- The client's own explanation is in the house's e-mail, outside the
  archive.
