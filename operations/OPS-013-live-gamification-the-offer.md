# OPS-013 — Live gamification — the offer

> **Summary:** What Numen Games sells to an event: a system of Roles (how
> each attendee takes part) and Territories (where), run live by a crew of
> cartographers and game masters, that turns an event's conversations into
> connections, intelligence and deliverables the organiser keeps.
> **Epistemic:** The one offer record every event proposal starts from.
> **Pragmatic:** Read it before opening an opportunity for an event;
> change it when a client asks something it does not answer.
> **Audience:** Agents · Oracles · Clients

---

## 1. Context

Events gather people and rarely reveal the ecosystem: conversations do not
survive the Monday after, and sponsors pay for visibility when they want
leads. The house has sold this by designed decks — a generic one and one
proposal per event, each iteration with its own world. This record is what
they have in common.

---

## 2. The record

### What it is

| | |
|---|---|
| **The service** | A participation system for an event: roles chosen at the entrance, territories on the floor with one mechanic each, a crew that runs it, deliverables after |
| **In the client's voice** | Each event gets its own world — a voyage, a Renaissance workshop, a forge and a bazaar — over the same structure |
| **Legal category** | *To confirm with the house's terms*: live service at an event |

### The structure every event shares

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Roles** | Six; they say how you take part, not who you are. Chosen at the entrance, worn on the credential |
| **Territories** | Five; each is a place on the floor with a flag, a cartographer's desk, an interaction island and a characteristic object, and one mechanic that yields one thing |
| **The crew** | Cartographers facilitate each territory and gather the contributions; game masters direct the whole |
| **Before** | Narrative design (master document, system flow), production kit, a digital space |
| **After** | Four deliverables: an intelligence report, a map of the event, and two pieces made from what attendees produced |

### How the crew scales

| Attendees | Cartographers | Game masters | Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 150–200 | 3 | 2 | a digital-economy association's forum |
| 1,000+ | 5 | 2 | a technology community's conference |

### What it costs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | On quote. The two proposals so far: 2,000 EUR for 150–200 attendees (about 10 EUR each); 10,000 EUR for 1,000+, against an estimated value of 21,000 EUR |
| **Floor** | *Not set.* The Oracle sets it |
| **Tax** | Shown separately in every proposal |

---

## 3. Open questions

What the proposals so far do not answer, and a client may ask:

- **How the result is judged.** The proposal standard asks for a level and
  an indicator; no event proposal has named one.
- **What the deliverables are licensed for**, and who owns the works made
  by attendees.
- **When it is invoiced**, and until when a proposal holds.
- **Earlier cases** the house may tell with the client unnamed.
