# OPS-018 — The house's card for tenders

> **Summary:** What Numen Games S.L. holds when a public body asks a
> bidder to prove itself: who it is, how big, what it has done, which
> registers and certificates it has, and what it does not have yet. Every
> tender found is read against this card, criterion by criterion; what
> fails here fails there.
> **Epistemic:** What can the house prove to a contracting authority today,
> and which tenders does that leave within reach?
> **Pragmatic:** Read it beside the notice before writing a tender's
> criteria table; update it the day a certificate, a register or a year's
> accounts changes.
> **Audience:** Sales · Oracles

---

## 1. Context

Spanish public procurement asks a bidder for three things before it reads
the offer: that it may contract (capacity, no prohibition), that it can pay
its way (economic solvency) and that it can do the work (technical
solvency) — and, in the simplified procedures, that it is in the bidders'
register (LCSP arts. 65, 87, 90, 159). A tender is lost before it is read
when one of these fails. This card is the house's side of that check,
written once, so no tender is weighed from memory.

Spanish tenders first; the European platform (TED) comes when the house
bids abroad.

## 3. What the card leaves within reach

Read from the law, against the card:

- **Minor contracts, under 15,000 €** — no notice and no solvency: the
  authority asks and awards directly (LCSP art. 118). The house is in reach
  of every one whose object fits; they are won by being known to the
  technician before the need is written.
- **Abridged simplified procedure, under 60,000 €, not intellectual
  services** — no solvency to prove; ROLECE or its application filed before
  offers close; price and formula criteria only (LCSP art. 159.6). In reach
  once the ROLECE application is filed.
- **Simplified procedure, under 35,000 €** — no solvency is asked unless the
  terms say so (RGLCAP art. 11.5). In reach with ROLECE.
- **Any services tender below the harmonised threshold** — no past works
  may be asked of a company under five years old; it proves its technical
  solvency by its team, its means or its quality measures instead (LCSP
  art. 90.4). This holds until 16 Feb 2029.
- **Where turnover is asked** — the authority may ask up to one and a half
  times the estimated value (LCSP art. 87.1.a). With a turnover under
  50,000 €, a tender whose terms ask the full multiple is in reach only up
  to about 33,000 € of estimated value; above that, only with a partner
  whose solvency is added (LCSP art. 75) or inside a joint venture (UTE,
  LCSP art. 69).
- **Out of reach today** — any tender asking ENS, an ISO certificate or a
  turnover above 50,000 € the house cannot add from a partner.

## 4. How a tender is read against it

1. Open the notice and, if needed, the administrative terms (the PCAP): the
   solvency clause and the award criteria.
2. Write one row per requirement in the record's criteria table: what the
   call asks, what this card says, yes, no or check.
3. The chance follows from the rows, by the register: any **no** is low (a
   partner could meet it) or none (nobody could, or the object does not
   fit); any **check** keeps it below high.
4. A **check** that decides the chance is a task with a date: the record's
   next action.

## 5. To get, in order

1. **ROLECE** — file the application now: it opens every simplified
   procedure, and the filed application is enough until it is granted.
2. **Tax and Social Security certificates** — request both; renew every six
   months.
3. **The filed accounts for FY2024 and FY2025** — turnover is proved from
   them.
4. **The certificate of the one past work** — from the client, with amount
   and date.
5. **Professional liability insurance** — some terms accept it instead of
   turnover (LCSP art. 87.1.b).
6. **A partner with solvency** for the tenders above 33,000 € — named per
   tender, never in this card.
