# OPS-012 — Training — the offer

> **Summary:** What Numen Games sells under the name Training: a place,
> built for one organisation, where its people rehearse a procedure before
> the day it counts — from a phone, a computer or a headset, with nothing
> to install. What it delivers, whom it is for, how learning is judged,
> what it costs and how a proposal for it is made.
> **Epistemic:** The one offer record every Training proposal starts from;
> what it says is what a client may hold the house to.
> **Pragmatic:** Read it before opening an opportunity for Training, and
> change it when a client asks something it does not answer.
> **Audience:** Agents · Oracles · Clients

---

## 1. Context

Every organisation has a folder it hands new people on the first day, and
every organisation lets the day itself do the teaching. Training puts the
walk before the day: a place where the corridor, the locker, the form and
the rule are already there, where getting it wrong costs a minute, and
where whoever walks it leaves a trace of what they did.

This record exists because the house has sold this by conversation, and a
conversation is not a thing to send. It is the offer the sales procedures
qualify against, propose from and learn into. The old site described
Training in promises — expert, empowering, collaborative — and no deliverable;
this record says what is delivered instead.

## 2. The record

### What it is

| | |
|---|---|
| **The service** | A browser-based 3D place, built from the client's own procedure, where their people rehearse it — entered from a phone, a computer or a headset, with a link and nothing to install |
| **What it is for** | Learning a procedure, a skill or a standard by walking it: a first day, a sequence that must be done in order, a rule that is expensive to learn on the job |
| **In the client's voice** | Their rooms, their forms, their names for things; the house's world stays out of sight |
| **Legal category** | *Training*, one of the three services the house's terms define |

### What is delivered

| Deliverable | Detail |
|---|---|
| The place | One or more rooms, each a step of the procedure; a guide who accompanies the learner; ten to fifteen minutes per room |
| The accesses | A link per group or per learner, valid for the period agreed |
| The evaluation | A final form at the end of the adventure; the answers reach the responsible by e-mail or in a spreadsheet. No per-learner login, live metrics or per-learner tracking: they do not exist |
| The files | The scene and its assets, under the licence written in the proposal |
| The measure | The indicator agreed at the level agreed, before and after |

### How learning is judged

The client chooses, before anything is built, at which level the result is
judged, and the proposal names the indicator:

| Level | The question it answers | Example of an indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction | Did they find it useful and relevant? | a short questionnaire at the exit |
| Learning | Did they learn it? | sequence errors in the rehearsal, first walk against last |
| Behaviour | Do they do it at work? | first-week incidents reported by their supervisor |
| Results | Did a figure of the organisation move? | time to full productivity of a new hire |

### Two cases

| Case | Procedure rehearsed | Learners | Judged at |
|---|---|---|---|
| A large retailer, the first day | Lockers, contract, floor rules, the till — the day before the day | new shop assistants, from their phones | behaviour: first-week incidents |
| A police force, the crime scene | Securing a scene and collecting evidence in the prescribed order | trainees at the academy, from computers and headsets | learning: sequence errors before and after |

Clients are named only with their written consent; until then a case is
told as above.
