# Opening is an act

> **Summary:** Every piece is born in the most closed regime that makes sense
> and is opened only by a deliberate act. What is opened cannot be closed
> again, and the name is never opened.
> **Epistemic:** Why openness here is a decision with a direction, not a
> default and not a mood — and why that is a kindness to whoever wants to use
> what we make.
> **Pragmatic:** Before giving anything away, know what you are giving and
> that you will not get it back. Before taking anything, know in thirty
> seconds what you may take.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** every Numen Games repository, and whoever publishes from one.

> This is not legal advice. It is the internal norm.

---

## Somebody wants to use something of ours

Picture them. A teacher who wants the scarab on a worksheet. A studio that
wants to run its own city on our system. A developer who found the viewer and
wants to fork it. A fan who wants to write a story set in the Ouroboros
district. Each of them has the same question, and they want it answered in
thirty seconds, without a lawyer: *what may I take, and what may I not?*

Everything in this canon exists so that the answer is always the same, always
written down, and never a surprise. That is what openness means here: not a
feeling about sharing, but a promise a stranger can rely on.

## Every piece is born closed and opened on purpose

**Every piece is born in the most closed regime that makes sense, and is
opened by deliberate acts, never by default.** Not because we are cautious
with what we make — most of it ends up given away — but because opening is a
promise, and a promise made by accident is the kind that gets broken.

What we make falls into five kinds, and each is held differently, for a
reason a stranger can follow.

The **catalogue** — the assets, the data, the design tokens — is given away
outright. It is how people arrive, not what we sell; the teacher takes the
scarab and owes us nothing, not even a credit.

The **edge** — the interfaces, the viewer, the tooling, the infrastructure —
is shared, because its worth grows with every person who adopts it. The
developer forks the viewer, improves it, and the improvement is as free as
the original.

The **core** — identity, progression, billing — is protected by copyleft:
free to whoever shares back, paid by whoever will not. The studio may run its
own city on our system; if it improves the system it gives the improvement
back, and if it would rather keep it, it pays for that.

The **world** — the lore, the narrative, the game — is given away like the
catalogue, because a world grows by being played, retold and remixed. The
fan writes the story, and it is hers.

Only the **name** is kept — the brand and its marks — because it is the one
thing nobody else can be. More on that below.

## What is reserved is said out loud

A file with no licence is all rights reserved by law — and to a reader it is
indistinguishable from an oversight. So here, what is kept is declared as
plainly as what is given: a statement that no right is granted, in the same
place and the same form as any other licence. The teacher who finds a file
with no notice should never have to guess whether we forgot or we meant it.
Where keeping matters most is exactly where one can least afford to rely on
someone inferring correctly.

## Opening only runs one way

A version published openly stays open, and anyone may carry on from there. A
waiver cannot be withdrawn. Think of it as a door that only opens outward:
reserved and unpublished, every option is still on the table; under copyleft
with a contributor agreement, we can still open further or license in
parallel; under copyleft without one, each contributor holds a veto over
that; published permissively, nothing practical remains; and once waived
outright, nothing at all.

This is why a piece meant to stay reserved is declared so **before** it is
published, never after. A document added to an already-open folder inherits
that folder's offer the moment it is pushed, and the offer cannot then be
taken back. It happened to us: the archive's own canon is open because it was
published under an open licence before any reservation existed, and no later
decision could reach back over it. We are glad it is open. We would rather
have chosen it.

## A licence on the shelf grants nothing

A repository carries its licence from the first commit, and while it stays
private that licence grants nothing to anyone. The grant is the act of making
the work publicly available under it — no release, no distribution step, no
permanent store required. The moment the repository is public, the promise
is made.

That is why the two acts that cannot be undone — turning a repository public,
and writing to a store that cannot be unwritten — are signed by an Oracle
rather than run by whoever is at the keyboard. Not distrust; arithmetic. An
act that cannot be reversed should cost at least one deliberate signature.

## The name is never opened

No free licence surrenders a trademark. Outside every one of them remain
**Numinia**, **Numen Games** and **Khepri** as identifiers of origin, together
with the logo, the isotype and the signature wordmarks.

The reason is the same as for everything above, seen from the other side. A
promise is only worth something if you can tell who made it. The teacher, the
studio, the developer and the fan all need to know that what says *Numen* is
ours and what does not is not — and so does everyone they show it to.

So: cite us, link to us, write about the project, say that your product uses
Numinia assets — yes, all of it, gladly. Use our marks as your own brand,
suggest we sponsor you, present a fork as the official one — no.

*The system can be copied; one cannot claim to be Numen.*
