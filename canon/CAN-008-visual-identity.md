# One identity, four registers

> **Summary:** Numen Games has one visual identity that dresses in whatever
> register a piece needs and stays recognisable across all of them, because
> the foundation underneath never changes.
> **Epistemic:** Why this looks the way it does — the dosage, the century it
> crosses, and what each register is for.
> **Pragmatic:** Choose a register, and know when a piece has stopped being
> ours. If it is decided here, it is not up for redesign in a piece.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** every piece that carries the Numen Games or Numinia mark.

---

## Where it comes from

The identity is the house's purpose made visible: *leveling up organisations
to build better relationships*, through games that make work better. Its cause
is digital humanism — **humans are not the problem** — and its closing is the
same as everything else here: leave things better than we found them. The
emotion it aims for is joy, trust and interest; the archetypes behind it are
the magician who heals, the caregiver and the explorer.

The four values of the house each translate into a design obligation.
**Cosmic harmony**: nothing exists as decoration — a resource that does not
encode something true about the content is removed. **Equable**: accessibility
is equity, not compliance. **Curiosity**, read through active inference:
minimise surprise where the user acts, and maximise exploration where they
discover. **Healthy environments**: the material lasts and weighs little.

And the three pillars: **craft**, art as the engine; **learn**, because humans
play to learn; **remix**, which is why this system is published openly for
anyone to copy and better.

## The mix: forty, forty, twenty

Three threads, in a fixed dosage, and they are not three aesthetics — they are
**three decades of the same story**.

**Solarpunk**, forty per cent, the year 2120: Mediterranean optimism,
technology in the service of life. It brings the light, the whole day mode,
and compositional serenity. Even the night here is its warm garden night —
brown blacks, sand under moonlight, never acid neon.

**Steampunk**, forty per cent, 1920: the machine. Bronze, mechanism, measure,
the Second Industrial Revolution. It brings the neutrals, the monospaced
letter, the mechanical grid and the instruments in the iconography.

**Cyberpunk**, twenty per cent, 2020: the signal, the flash that cuts through.
Dosed neon and the binary texture. At twenty per cent, cyber is accent and
never ambience.

**The mixing rule: light dominates, the machine structures, the signal
blinks.** The dose test is done squinting — does it look like a Mediterranean
garden with bronze machinery where a signal blinks? Correct. Like Blade
Runner? Too much cyber. Like a gardening catalogue? Not enough machine.

**The hundred-years line.** 1920, the material city burns and the machine
remains. 2020, the decade of invocation: the signal cuts through and the
Oracles call the city back. 2120, the full city, the garden. We always speak
in decades — the twenties of three centuries — and the system does not mix
eras: it traverses a two-century journey, with the scarab pushing the sun from
one to the next.

**The era is flavour, not skin.** A piece is never "set" in one decade: the
dosage holds always, and the era is emphasised only through its sanctioned
devices. The test: remove those, and the piece must still be unmistakably
ours.

The scarab — Khepri, creation, cycle, rebirth, and the only place the old name
survives — crosses the three threads and closes every piece. It closes; it
never opens.

## The governing principle

**Legibility before detail, one orchestrated moment per piece, and nothing
that imitates.** Every register is a way of saying the same thing, and the
moment a piece could be mistaken for another studio's, the register has been
used wrong.

## The four registers

The system speaks in four, ordered by importance, and every piece declares its
own **before it declares its medium**.

**The Umbral** is the general register, named for the border anyone can cross.
It is the base system: websites, presentations, documents, invoices,
interface, events, email. It sets the priors of whoever arrives from outside,
and it is the register that invoices.

**The Velo** is the register of depth. In the world it is the symbolic skin
that sustains the city, where knowledge orders itself upon being discovered;
in the system it is the atmosphere seen behind and through things — archive,
viewers, discovery surfaces. It makes visible how knowledge settles, as
literal layers. Its single rule: **the Velo adds no new colour, only
transparency.** Nothing enters the system through that door.

**Low-poly** is the world's 3D body: environments, props, characters. **The
mesh is honest as the pixel is honest** — the faceted geometry is shown, not
disguised. Silhouette first, facets after; detail that does not read is
removed; flat colour, never photographic texture.

**Píxel** is the world's 2D memory: the heritage of the graphic adventures
that taught that you learn by playing, cited and never copied. It is a
rendering register, not a decorative theme, and it has a discipline: **you
enter and leave it completely.** A scene is pixel or it is not; mixed
densities are forbidden. It serves game and lore moments, achievements,
narrative scenes — never corporate communication, where the pixel would be
costume rather than narrative. It lives in the night: a pixel scene inside a
light piece keeps its dark canvas, framed, and is never recoloured.

**The fusion rule.** Registers do not stack. A piece lives in one and may
**frame** content from another — a sprite inside a website, a viewer showing a
3D asset — and the boundary between them must be visible.

## Brand architecture

Numen Games is the organisation and this identity; Numinia is its own
narrative world; client projects are bespoke systems. **A project system never
replaces this identity**: it inherits palette, typography and grid, and adds
its own lexicon and iconography on top.

## The signature and the play

The signature is monochrome and solemn; the play is coloured and expressive.
Two registers of the same brand with a hard border between them: the play
lives in the world and the expressive — events, social, merchandising,
Numinia pieces — and the signature lives in the corporate: invoices,
proposals, legal headers. **A corporate piece with the brand in colour is a
register error, not a variant.**

The three glyphs — *space, people, connect* — are born from the wordmark's own
letters, and they tell Numen's service in three shapes: we design spaces, for
people, that connect. They are brand, not icons: a single ink per
composition, never recoloured by parts, never rotated.

The scarab may repeat in sequence over a field of colour — the scarab
multiplying is rebirth made pattern — and in the expressive register the
wordmark may compose colour over colour. Where the brand is a piece's only
identifier, the pair must stay legible.

## How it moves, how it speaks

Motion follows from curiosity: nothing moves where the user acts, content
reveals itself where they discover, **one orchestrated moment per piece**, and
with reduced motion everything simply appears. The typing is the flagship,
and it carries a double heritage — the 1920 typewriter and the 2020 terminal
at once, which is what makes it the most Numinian movement in the catalogue.

The voice is cultivated, plain and clear: precision with a touch of fantasy,
no jargon until it reaches the receiver.

Every piece declares its **level of language** and keeps it. The
**colloquial** for community, product and interface; the **literary**, which
is the lore's, for home, covers, events and campaigns; the **technical** for
documentation, proposals and contracts. A contract with lore in it is not
signed; a home page full of metrics convenes nobody.

The world's vocabulary is level II's own, and in level III it is cited rather
than narrated. Its names are written as they are, never translated, and no
relatives are invented for them. Alongside it, each decade has words that
smell of it — cenacle and bronze and correspondence for 1920, signal and
invocation and terminal for 2020, garden and harvest and morning light for
2120 — used as spices and not as the dish.

## What this canon does not define

Not one value: no hex, no size, no curve, no budget, no class name. Those are
the design values register and the kit, where a guard verifies them and a
change is traceable. How a piece is produced is a protocol; each surface has
its own blueprint; the licence is the licensing standard's.

Where this canon and the canon of brand and culture disagree, that one holds:
this is how the house looks, not what the house is.
