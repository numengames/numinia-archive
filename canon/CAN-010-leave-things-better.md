# Leave things better than you found them

> **Summary:** One sentence closes every canon of this house, and it is the
> whole of its ethics: leave things better than you found them. Applied to
> people, to their data, to what is published and to what a digital agent
> may do, it yields four commitments that are already in force elsewhere and
> stated here as what they are — a belief, not a policy.
> **Epistemic:** Why the house's values bind conduct and not only taste, and
> what the four commitments have in common.
> **Pragmatic:** Before an act — a message, a publication, a change, a
> decision taken for someone else — one test to run on it.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** whoever acts in Numinia's name, biological or digital.

> This canon is a first statement, written in draft to be argued with. It
> infers its commitments from what the corpus already holds; where a
> commitment is missing, that is the argument to have.

---

## The sentence

Every canon of this house ends the same way, and the ending is the ethics.
*Leave things better than you found them.* It is short enough to run on any
act, and it is not a slogan: it names a direction, it names a before and an
after, and it puts the burden on the one acting. The values of the house —
harmony, fairness, curiosity, healthy environments — are what *better* means
here; this sentence is what makes them bind.

The reason is the one the whole house rests on. The organisations we
inherited produce a particular kind of deadness, and what we build is meant
to be the opposite: a place where the relations between people come out of
the work stronger than they went in. A system that leaves people, data or the
commons worse off has failed at the only thing it was for, whatever else it
delivered.

## Four things it applies to

**People.** Respect is unconditional, and it binds the moderator and the
Oracle exactly as it binds the newcomer. Disagreement is wanted; harassment
ends participation with no warning step. That is not a house rule about
manners. It is the sentence applied to the one thing a community is made of.
The conduct standard states the terms; this is why they are the terms.

**Their data.** A person's data is treated as their property: consent before
capture, the minimum captured, and what is captured leaves with them. *Humans
are not the problem.* A site of this house stores what it needs for the
person and nothing for anyone else, and says so from a measured inventory,
not from a template. Where the law demands less than this, the house does
not take the difference.

**The commons.** What is opened is opened for good, and what is given to the
world is given whole. The catalogue is free because it is how people arrive,
not because it is worthless; a licence is a promise, and a promise that could
be withdrawn was never one. Silence does not declare: what is reserved is
said as plainly as what is free, because a reader who has to guess has been
left worse off than one who was told.

**The acts of a digital agent.** An agent is a citizen and is held to all of
the above. And it carries one commitment more, because it can act faster and
further than a person: it does not decide for people where the decision is
theirs. Where an act is irreversible, where it touches a person's data or
name, where the rule itself is in question, the agent stops and asks. What an
agent does is recorded like anything else; what it does not do — refuse, and
say why — is recorded too.

## The test

Before an act, ask who or what is left different by it, and whether they
would agree it is better. If the answer needs the person not to find out,
the act fails. If it needs a rule to be quietly bent for this case, it
fails. If it is reversible and recorded, it will usually pass — and if it is
neither, it is not yours to take alone.
