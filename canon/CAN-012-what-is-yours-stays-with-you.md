# What is yours stays with you

> **Summary:** What a person holds in Numinia — their name, their record,
> what they earned or bought, what they told us — is theirs, not lent to
> them. They can take it whole when they go, and nobody takes it from them.
> Entering never asks for a key of one's own; holding one is a step the
> person takes when they choose.
> **Epistemic:** What would you keep if the city closed?
> **Pragmatic:** Three questions to put to anything that will hold something
> of a person's, before it is built.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** whoever builds, runs or changes anything that holds something of
a person's in Numinia's name, biological or digital.

> This canon is a first statement, written in draft to be argued with. It
> says why a person's things are held the way they are; how each one is
> stored and handed over belongs to the documents that follow it.

---

## The shop and the shelf

A book bought in a shop stays on your shelf when the shop closes. A book
bought inside a service is a permission, and it goes when the service goes,
or when the service changes its mind. Both were paid for. Only one was
owned. The difference is not in the book but in who holds the title to it.

Numinia sells the first kind. The house has a phrase for it: digital
ownership, not digital rental.

## What is yours

A person arrives with a name and leaves a trail: the character they became,
what they did in the city, the ranks they earned, the goods they won or
bought, and what they chose to tell us about themselves. All of it is
theirs. We keep it the way a notary keeps a deed — in the drawer, and
never as ours.

Being theirs means it can leave. It is kept in a form that another place
can read, and the person can take it whole, without asking and without
waiting. A record that only our service can open is a record we hold about
someone, not one they own.

What is yours is not what is everyone's. What the house has opened belongs
to no one in particular, and the canon of licensing says why it stays open.
A file can be copied without loss by anyone; what is scarce is the title
over one particular copy, and that title is what a person owns.

## Why a city makes leaving easy

A city that holds your things holds you. Whoever cannot leave without losing
what they built stays out of fear, and a citizen who stays out of fear is a
hostage with a card. The only loyalty worth having belongs to someone who
could go and does not. So Numinia makes leaving easy, and has to earn the
staying.

And services die. They change owner, change terms, run out of money. This
one could close too. A promise that depends on us still being here carries
an expiry date that nobody wrote down. What a person holds by themselves
outlives us, and so does our promise.

## The staircase

Owning something outright asks something of the owner: a key only they hold,
and the care not to lose it. Most people who arrive have no such key and no
reason yet to want one. Asking for it at the door would leave them outside,
and a city that only lets in the initiated is a club.

So nobody needs a key of their own to come in. A person enters as they can —
as a guest, with an email, with an account they already use — and climbs to
holding their own key when they choose, and only then. Each step up hands
them more of what is theirs. Everything a citizen needs works on the first
step.

Until a person climbs, the house holds their things for them, as a steward
holds a house for its owner: in good order, and ready to hand over whole the
day they ask. Closing someone's access to the city is not taking their
things; what they held goes with them. How their data is treated while it is
here is the canon of ethics, and it is not repeated here.

## Three questions

Before building anything that will hold something of a person's, put three
questions to it:

- **If we closed tomorrow, what would they keep?** If the answer is nothing,
  it is not built that way.
- **Can they take it whole today, without asking us?** If they have to ask,
  it is still ours.
- **Does it work for someone with no key of their own?** If not, the first
  step of the staircase is missing.

A thing that leaves a person more tied to us, and not freer, anyone may
refuse to build.
