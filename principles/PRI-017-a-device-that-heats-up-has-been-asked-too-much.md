# A device that heats up has been asked too much

> **Summary:** The engine draws what is needed and no more, and chooses what to give a device by what that device can do, never by what it is called. When the device cannot bear the work, the work gives way before the device does.
> **Epistemic:** What do we owe the hardware people already have?
> **Pragmatic:** Before adding something that draws, ask what it costs the least capable device we say we serve, and what it gives up when that device cannot pay.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** whoever builds, runs or changes Numinia Engine, human or digital.

---

People come to the city on the device they own. A phone that burns the hand, drains in an hour or stutters until the picture is a slideshow has told its owner they are not welcome. The cost of beauty falls on the person least able to carry it, and we refuse to put it there.

So the engine does nothing when nothing changes, draws only what the player can reach, keeps a ceiling on how much it asks, and lowers its own quality before the device has to complain. The ladder is built from the top: we first learn what the best device can do, and then decide, rung by rung, what each lower one keeps. The lowest rung is not where the design begins; it is where it is last tested.

What a device may be given is read from what the device reports it can do. A name is a guess, and guesses age; a capability is a fact that can be asked. A feature with no way down for a weaker device is not finished, and one that is withheld from a stronger device because of its name is a mistake.
