# Copy, transform, combine

> **Summary:** We make new things by copying what already exists, transforming the copy and combining it with other things, and we say what we took. A piece is built to be taken, changed and joined by the next person, so nothing is made from nothing and nothing is kept from reuse without a reason.
> **Epistemic:** How is something new made here?
> **Pragmatic:** Before building, look for a piece to copy; when you build, make it easy to copy; when you copy, keep its licence and name its source.
> **Audience:** Everyone

**Binds:** whoever makes or changes anything in Numinia, human or digital.

---

Almost nothing worth having was made from nothing. A world starts from a template, a template from a world that worked, a document from the one that came before it. Copying is the honest start of making: it spares the maker the work that is already done and puts the effort where the new thing differs from the old.

Copying alone only repeats. The step that makes a thing ours is the transformation: changing the copy until it fits its purpose, and keeping what the change taught. Combining is the third step and the one that surprises, because two pieces that never met do something neither could do alone. The engine is built in pieces for this reason: whoever wants one switches it on, adapts it and joins it to others.

Taking carries a duty. What we copy keeps its licence and its author's name; what we may not copy, we do not copy. What we make we publish under the most open licence the house allows, so that the next person can take it without asking. A piece that cannot be copied, changed or joined, because it is closed, undocumented or tied to the one place it was made, is a debt on whoever comes after.
