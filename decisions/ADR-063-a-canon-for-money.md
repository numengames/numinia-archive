# ADR-063 — A canon for money, opened in draft

> **Summary:** The Oracle asked for money to have a canon of its own, and
> corrected the world on the way: a Token may represent wealth as well as a
> bond. This opens the canon and changes the sentence in the manual in the
> same cut, so the canon and the world never disagree for a day.
> **Epistemic:** Why a payment is written as a bond, and why the account is
> open.
> **Pragmatic:** The text on the table before the standard and the protocols
> that will carry the rules.
> **Audience:** Agents · Oracles

---

## 1. Context

Numinia is about to take payments again. The only charge the corpus
remembers is a season pass, retired with its site; the only mention of the
payment processor is the old architecture reference. No document says what a
payment here *is*. The work of designing the support page, the catalogue and
the cost register kept coming back to three beliefs that nobody had written:

| Belief | Where it surfaced |
|---|---|
| Every payment delivers a good; nothing is a donation | The Oracle, in session: everything carries VAT because there is always a digital good as the incentive |
| A Token can carry money and meaning at once | The Oracle's correction of the manual: *pueden representar las dos cosas* |
| The account is open, and one | The cost register: what Numinia has cost since its first day, for citizens, the gestoría, a lender, an auditor |

The manual says the opposite of the second: *No representan riqueza.
Representan vínculo.* The codex glossary quotes it.

The Oracle's words in session: money gets its own canon; the title is left to
the author (*el que quieras*); everything Numinia consumes is paid by Numen
Games, now and later; fiscal audits and private auditors are not to be
forgotten.

## 2. Decision

**`CAN-011` is created at `0.1.0`, *What has value also makes a bond*.** It
binds whoever sets a price, takes a payment or keeps the account in
Numinia's name.

It holds: a payment is neither a donation nor only a purchase, but a bond
that is worth money (the image of a club member's fee); every payment
delivers a good the payer can name before paying, with the whole price in
view; what is sold never closes what is open; a Token's worth does not erase
its history; whoever pays is remembered by the name they choose, by none if
they say nothing, and the amount is never shown; the account is written and
open, one account read by citizen, lender, auditor and tax authority alike,
closed from documents and never from estimates, with the people's time
counted and no person's pay published. It leaves three questions to put to
any charge.

**It is in draft and says so under its card**, as the ethics canon did: it is
a first statement to argue with, and the rules it justifies — prices, VAT,
the processor, how the month is closed, how long papers are kept — are not
written yet. They go to a standard and to protocols, which is where
`STD-031` sends tools and cadences.

**It reads in *The Summa*,** after the archive canon: it is that canon
applied to money — *if a charge is not written in the archive, it does not
exist*.

**The Token sentence changes**, in the Spanish and English manuals and in
the Spanish glossary that quotes it: *they may be exchanged*, and *they
represent a bond, and may represent wealth as well: what a Token is worth
never erases where it came from*. The next sentence — obtaining a Token is
always a form of recognition — stays.

Body: 670 words.

## 3. Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| Fold money into the ethics canon | The Oracle chose a canon of its own. Ethics says what an act owes people; this says what a payment is. Folded, the ethics would carry a price list's reasons |
| Write the standard first | The standard would plate rules whose reason is nowhere. The canon is short; the standard follows it |
| Name the processor, VAT rates and retention periods in the canon | `STD-031` STA-006 and STA-007: a canon names no tool and keeps no clock. They belong to the standard |
| Leave the manual as it is until later | For as long as it says *they do not represent wealth*, the canon and the world contradict each other. Same cut |
| Correct the English glossary too | It exists only on an open branch (`lore/codex-en`); it is corrected there or in the next cut after it lands |

## 4. Consequences

- **Obliges:** nothing new yet. It gives the future standard and protocols
  their reason and one place to cite it.
- **Costs:** the canon series counts ten documents; the rule index, the
  reading groups and the manual's Token passage change.
- **Follow-up:** the standard of charges and the account (what every charge
  must meet; one ledger with four views; what a lender, an auditor and the
  tax authority are shown; how long papers are kept), then the protocols
  *put something on sale* and *close the month*.
- **Reversal:** delete the canon; restore the Token sentence from history.

## 5. Status

Proposed, as a draft to be argued with. It takes effect — as a draft — when
the Oracle approves the pull request that carries it.
