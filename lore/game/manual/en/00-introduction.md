# **INTRODUCTION**

# **THE ECHOES OF A VIRTUAL CITY**

_«The sound of a stone rolling across a broken digital tile was the only thing that interrupted the silence. In the distance, a levitating sphere ‒silver and rough like a lost moon‒ cast the shadow of a board into the air. It had no end and no apparent centre. Each square flickered with a different symbol: some were ancient, others were yet to be invented._

_Rima moved forward slowly, resting her hand against the wall, which trembled faintly at her touch. She had been told that the textures in Numinia were anchored memories: a code of sensitive memory that only the initiated could read. It had taken her seven days to cross the corridors of mist that separated the Threshold from the Central Archive, and now she was there, where every game began and ended._

_‒Are you ready? ‒asked a voice without a throat._

_Rima did not answer. She knelt before the suspended board, drew an obsidian die from inside her cloak and held it between her fingers. A single number was engraved on every face: one. The origin. The first fall._

_The voice spoke again._

_‒You have, as yet, no rules ‒it said‒. Only intentions. You have no map. Only paths that do not exist yet. What do you hope to find?_

_‒A question worth answering ‒she replied._

_And she threw the die._

_What happened next was less a movement than an opening. The board broke apart into a thousand grids of light, spreading through the city like a conscious swarm. Towers without foundations began to grow downwards. Streets that had never existed rewrote the data of the old ones. A choir of spectral beings began to intone verses in forgotten dialects. Something ‒or someone‒ was awakening beneath the surface of the neighbourhoods that made up the four districts._

_Rima closed her eyes._

_She knew she was playing. That every step, every choice, every silence and every word would bring an echo, a reply, a distortion. That to play in Numinia was not to act as someone else, but to reveal what one is when the other dissolves._

_Because there, in that city written by will and upheld by the Veil, to play was to remember what adults had forgotten: that fantasy is not escape, but conjuration. That the game is not distraction, but destiny. And that every true story ‒like every great game‒ begins with a question thrown into the air, a die that spins and a world that waits._

_It was then that the gates of the city opened for her. Not with noise. Much less with light._

_But with an invitation._

_Rima took the first step._

_And Numinia, as always, began to write itself anew.»_

A roleplaying game is a shared experience of narrative creation. It is not simply a matter of taking on the role of a fictional character, but of embodying a point of view, a temperament, a story within a collective universe. In essence, a roleplaying game proposes a flexible structure in which each player plays a character, while a guiding figure ‒the Game Director, or GD‒ shapes the settings, the world and its challenges. Together, narrator and players build a story that is not written in advance, but emerges from decisions, actions and the chance of the dice.

The foundations of roleplaying rest on interaction, improvisation and shared storytelling. A world is established with internal rules, a set of logical norms that allow conflicts or uncertain situations to be resolved through dice rolls or similar mechanisms. And yet, despite this apparently structured mechanics, a roleplaying game is not a competition. There are no winners and no losers. What there is, is a symbolic experience, a journey that crosses archetypes, moral challenges, cooperation, chance and wit. Roleplaying is, then, a way of playing that is at once new and ancestral, complex, complete and essential.

It is common to think of play as an instrument: something that serves something else. People speak of its educational usefulness, its therapeutic power, its value in developing narrative, logical or social skills. And, above all, of its sense of fun. And it is true: play educates, it cultivates empathy, imagination, strategic thinking and collaboration. And it is fun, no doubt about it. But reducing it to that would be to mistake its essence.

Because, above all, the dynamic of play is not instrumental: it is functional. Its meaning does not lie in what it is useful for, but in the function it fulfils. The dynamics of play let us carry out an essential function: that of exploring ourselves through others. When we play, we do not simply act. We experience ourselves. We see in the other ‒the character, the setting, the fictional situation‒ an extension of what we could be, a living hypothesis of ourselves. And that function is complex, vital, humanly unavoidable.

Play is not only action: it is also, as Lacan said, reflection, recognition, and the symbolisation of the self in relation to the world.

Play is our first narrative, our first lesson and our first creative impulse.

Roleplaying, as an essential and complex dynamic of play, does not only entertain, nor does it only teach. It transforms us. Not only because it represents another world, but because it lets us rebuild our own from multiple perspectives that are new to us, from fantasy. To roleplay is, ultimately, a way of understanding and of living, of fulfilling ourselves. And that is why it is so powerful.

Freud held that the child, when playing, is building a poetry of its own, rearranging reality through fantasy and finding deep pleasure in it. And that, although at some point the adult stops playing, they find that lost pleasure in fantasy, building castles in the air and daydreams. It is true that those of us who love roleplaying games did not stop playing when we grew up and became adults, but this reflection teaches us how much the dynamic of play matters in our fulfilment, in how we recognise and face reality. Because play is not a mechanism of escape; it neither silences reality nor confronts it: it faces it. From the perspective of fantasy, that is.

So let us agree that the exercise of sitting at a table in front of a character sheet, full of characteristics, indicators and traits, and interacting with a Game Director who describes a world to us and guides us through it ‒a world (re)invented, (re)built for the sake of our impulses, of our demanding fantasy‒ answers, in the last instance, to an intimate need to be, to fulfil ourselves essentially, which, given the pleasure it brings, we may come to understand as play.

But if all we wanted were to have fun, we would turn to far simpler pastimes: sitting on the sofa wrapped in a blanket to watch a sitcom or an entertainment show; opening our phone to devour short, random videos on some fashionable app… These activities feed dopamine and instant reward generously, but they are purely instrumental: they lack all fulfilment, all function. The dynamic of play, on the other hand, proposes commitments that must be taken on; it demands a kind of effort. And roleplaying, especially, holds a long list of commitments, rules and proposals. No one in their right mind would take on such an effort only to have fun.

But that is what play is: a complex structure, a proposal overflowing with rules, the acceptance of a good number of commitments.

For without rules there is no game; without the game there is no soul; and without a soul… there is no Numinia.

## **Numinia: playing to inhabit a symbol**

Numinia is not simply a setting, nor a backdrop of the usual kind. It is not just a fantasy world waiting to be travelled, nor a closed tale the players are condemned to repeat. Numinia is, above all, a proposal. A gesture in the form of a game. A city built not with bricks or code, but with stories, decisions and bonds.

As a roleplaying game, Numinia is an open and symbolic architecture, a fabric of meanings in which each character is a thread that affects the whole. What is played here is not only a campaign or an adventure, but the resonance of what one decides to be when facing the unknown. Because in Numinia, the world answers to the way you look at it. The city reacts, folds and reveals itself through the will of those who inhabit it. Each player, in creating a character, is accepting a way of inhabiting time, of naming things and of facing the essential dilemmas of the soul and of society.

This is not a game of power or accumulation. It is a game of revelation. Here, to play is to make meaning. Each attribute of the character stands for a way of understanding the world. Each competence is an open door to resolution, to chaos, to art or to the void. The positions one chooses are not trades or classes, but ways of speaking with the Veil that covers the city: the symbolic skin of a universe that exists halfway between imagination and memory.

Numinia proposes a journey in which the board is made of language and gaze, in which the dice do not dictate fate, but illuminate it. The roll does not resolve an action; it states it, strengthens it or transforms it. Because here there is no success without a story, and no story without transformation.

This game is born from the wish to join the ancient with what has not yet been said. It recovers mythologies, symbolisms, philosophical and scientific structures and weaves them together with technology, information theory, digital culture and the art of storytelling. Numinia is a place where the player becomes author, interpreter, demiurge.

And, like every true act of play, what unfolds in Numinia is a possibility of life. A way of thinking, of feeling and of building community. An open-gated city where every story told is a truth added to the ones before.

Because Numinia is not played.

It is inhabited.

And, in inhabiting it, it is transformed.
