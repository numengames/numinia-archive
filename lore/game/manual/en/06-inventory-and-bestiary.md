# **CHAPTER 6**

# **INVENTORY AND BESTIARY**
« _The archivist descended in silence along the spine of the Duv-Harel Promontory, where the mists fold beneath the weight of unspoken memories. Under his hood of black velvet, the sensors vibrated gently with the breath of the volcanic winds. Each step upon the wet sand of the shore of the Ocean of Lúnacar resounded with echoes of ancient invocations, uttered by beings who no longer knew whether they were fable or fossil._

_He sought neither glory nor map. He sought meaning._

_In his satchel lay the fragments: the vitreous bone of a volute dragon, the broken casing of a stored voice, the still-living seed of a weapon that had not chosen its bearer. Each object was an unformulated question. Each relic, a piece of the torn Veil._

_The ancients said that knowledge was the best defence. But the archivist knew that, in Numinia, knowledge could also be the prey._

_And, as he copied into his terminal, with trembling hands, the runes that floated above the corpse of a creature rocking gently between the foam and the muddied sand, he understood a truth that only true hunters knew: that objects were vestiges of decisions._

_And that beasts were those very decisions, returning to claim their debt._ »

In every roleplaying game, the Inventory and the Bestiary constitute two pillars of the system: one represents what is carried, the other what is faced. The first speaks of use, memory and choice; the second, of risk, confrontation and otherness.

But, in Numinia, these elements are more than mechanics: they are mirrors of the soul of the world.

In Numinia, the Inventory and the Bestiary are not separate systems, but two faces of the same circuit of meaning: what the player chooses to take in, and what the world forces them to confront. One is burden; the other, collision. Both are reflections. Both are story.

Where the Inventory answers to the will of the character, the Bestiary answers to the will of the world. Where one offers agency, the other poses resistance. But both are inscribed within the same principle: that to play, in Numinia, is to take on a narrative responsibility for desire and danger.

There is no neutral object: every Relic contains a story. There is no gratuitous creature: every being embodies a dissonance. Thus, what one carries and what one faces become signs of a single dialectic: that of the player with their symbolic surroundings.

That is why this chapter is not a catalogue, but a map of relationships between form, memory and conflict. Each entry, be it weapon or beast, relic or aberration, is a node in the living network of Numinia. Understanding them helps not only to survive in the city and its periphery: it helps to read the world.

Because in this city, what you carry and what attacks you are part of the same story. Yours.

**Narrative echoes**

The Inventory answers the question: _What do we choose to take with us from the world?_

The Bestiary asks: _What part of the world resists being integrated?_

Both, at heart, chart desire and fear. They are the two hemispheres of the game: what we take in and what we reject.

What we choose and what chooses us. And it is not always the case that one answers to the object and the other to the creature.

## **Fragment 1: The Bestiary**

The creatures of Numinia are more than altered biology or hostile algorithms. Each one is born of an unresolved tension in the world: urban traumas, narrative errors, arrested symbols, archetypes that mutated.

Combat against them is always a symbolic confrontation: to face them means reading them, understanding what they represent, what part of the city ‒or of the player‒ they seek to reveal or to block.

Some beasts attack not through force, but through semantic fracture. And defeating them means, very often, understanding more than wounding.

Each beast in Numinia possesses a set of general characteristics, as well as a series of attributes that will help us resolve confrontations with the players. Other details such as its habitat, its way of life or its origin will complete the outline of its NPC sheet, which the GD can later adapt to the context of an adventure and to a more specific creature.

### **Formrenders**

The Formrenders are ancient humanoid presences that arose from the depths of the Akasha and were drawn towards Numinia during the earliest times of the Dark Age. Their nature maintains a profound affinity with the earth and with telluric energies, to the point that their relationship with the ground, the stone and the currents that flow beneath them determines a good part of their existence. With the technological evolution of the city, they took refuge in the subterranean regions of the periphery, where they remain as hostile vestiges of an earlier order.

**Origin and nature**

The Formrenders arrived in Numinia during a period of chaos, when the ancient city had gone up in flames and its reflection, projected into the Akasha, was still weakened. The subsequent reconstruction and progressive technification of Numinia represented for them a rupture of the natural cycles and of the primordial wisdom they revered. Unable to integrate into the new city, they withdrew towards the depths and established small communities among caverns, ruins and rock formations of the periphery.

Their prolonged bond with these places ended up transforming them. The Formrenders do not merely inhabit the earth: they maintain a symbiotic relationship with it and seem to perceive Numinia through its vibrations, fractures and telluric currents, as though the ground were an extension of their own senses.

**Physiology and psychology**

The Formrenders present a humanoid silhouette, slender and sinewy, slightly hunched and adapted to rapid movement through tunnels and uneven terrain. Their skin, dark and hardened, has a mineral, almost stony texture, upon which horny plates, scars and small crystalline deposits appear. Their hands are large and gnarled, ending in long black claws that they use both to dig and to cling to rock.

The face retains vague canine features, though deeply deformed by their adaptation to the subsoil. Especially striking are their enormous opaque eyes, like whitish lenses, which take up a good part of the face and lend them an expression that is difficult to interpret. The mouth, narrow and drawn back, lets small, irregular teeth show through. Some individuals partially cover their head and body with pieces of leather, mineral remains, straps, plates recovered from ancient ruins and objects whose significance seems ritual rather than protective.

Their perception depends above all on the vibrations transmitted through the ground. They can sense movements and disturbances at a considerable distance, a sensitivity that their shamans interpret as a form of communication with the deep currents of Numinia.

Their thinking is instinctive, ritual and profoundly mistrustful of technology. They retain the capacity for reasoning and organisation, but their communication is based mainly on guttural sounds, clicks, gestures and signs traced upon stone or sand. For them, the world is not explained: it is listened to, observed and interpreted.

**Way of life**

The Formrenders organise themselves into small clans settled in networks of caverns and galleries. Their eldest members and their shamans interpret omens through vibrations, fractures in the rocks and patterns formed spontaneously in the sand. They also guard stones, glyphs and fragments of ancient ruins that they consider repositories of a memory older than present-day Numinia.

Their culture barely distinguishes between territory, memory and cult. An ancestral cavern, a mineral formation or a marked stone may be at once refuge, archive and sanctuary.

**Relationship with Numinia**

The Formrenders regard the city as a structure that has done violence to the primordial order upon which it was raised. Their hostility is directed above all against technological infrastructures and against those factions they hold responsible for extending them.

For this reason, their incursions rarely respond solely to the need to obtain resources. They destroy conduits, devices, networks and structures even when they gain no immediate benefit, as though each attack were meant to tear a layer of artifice from Numinia and return to the surface that which lies buried beneath.

**Confrontation tactics**

The Formrenders avoid open spaces and make use of tunnels, changes in level, ruins and areas of poor visibility. They generally attack in groups, bursting out from unexpected positions and withdrawing before the adversary can regroup. Their claws and rudimentary tools are dangerous, but their true advantage lies in their ability to perceive vibrations, move across difficult terrain and coordinate through almost imperceptible signals.

They do not conceive of confrontation as a head-on exchange. They surround, isolate, harass and alter the terrain, seeking to break their adversaries' coordination. When they have time, they prepare traps and exploit cracks, secondary galleries and unstable surfaces to turn the space itself into part of the threat.

**Habitat**

Small communities of Formrenders are scattered across the periphery of Numinia, although there is no reliable census of their population. They are particularly common in the Sea of Sílices and in the Neuma Subvale, where caverns and subterranean galleries provide them with suitable refuges.

They prefer places where they can maintain close contact with the ground and where the vibrations of the surface reach the depths. From these lairs they launch swift incursions against colonies, routes and peripheral installations before disappearing underground once more.

**Narrative function**

The Formrenders represent the shadow of Numinia's progress: that which was displaced, buried or abandoned so that the present city could rise. Their confrontation with technology is not merely material, but symbolic. In them persists the suspicion that every advance demands leaving something behind, and that what is buried does not necessarily disappear.

They embody the resentment of the natural towards the manufactured, the fear that progress may destroy what it sets out to improve, and the violence of a memory incapable of expressing itself through arguments, yet stubborn in its will to go on existing.

**Fixed Anchors**

<u>Telluric bond.</u> The Formrender sustains its presence and orientation through contact with the ground and the currents that run through it. It perceives vibrations, movements and disturbances through the stone, the sand or the soil over which it moves. This relationship shows itself constantly in its behaviour: it touches surfaces, tilts its head at certain vibrations and seems to react to movements it cannot yet see. Separating it, isolating it or in any way interfering with that telluric continuity means intervening in an essential part of its relationship with its surroundings.

<u>Buried memory.</u> Each Formrender maintains a bond with some material vestige of ancient Numinia: a stone, a glyph, a fragment of ruin or a mineral formation preserved by its clan. These objects do not function as mere possessions, but as symbolic repositories of the memory that the Formrenders consider older than the rebuilt city. When they act far from their lairs, they usually carry small fragments of these vestiges among their clothing or attached to their bodies. Their presence connects the individual with the collective memory of its species and with what it considers its rightful place in Numinia.

<u>Rejection of artifice.</u> The presence of active technology provokes in the Formrenders a compulsive response of hostility. This is not simply cultural hatred: they perceive certain technological emissions, vibrations and structures as an interference that disturbs their relationship with the deep currents of Numinia. During a confrontation they tend to fix their attention on devices, conduits and technological sources, even when there are tactically more immediate targets. This fixation can make them especially aggressive, but it also renders part of their behaviour predictable.

### **Umbrílego**

The Umbrílegos are liminal entities that dwell on the most unstable margins of the Veil and of consciousness. They are neither fully corporeal nor fully conceptual, but forms of stray thought, psychic and symbolic residues that have acquired presence in those places of Numinia where meaning has grown weak. Their appearance is usually accompanied by amnesia, contradictions, repetitions and alterations of the surroundings.

They do not inhabit the shadow. They are what remains when thought goes out.

**Origin and nature**

The Umbrílegos arise in areas where the Veil has suffered alterations of a semantic nature: failed Akashic translations, incomplete rituals, collapsed systems of interpretation or places whose meaning has been profoundly altered. There, thoughts, intentions and symbols deprived of any structure capable of sustaining them begin to condense until they acquire a manifestation of their own.

Each Umbrílego forms around a contradiction, an absence or an incomplete meaning. It is not the memory of what was lost, but the void left by its loss. For this reason, its existence depends on a semantic anomaly that remains unresolved and acts as the nucleus of its manifestation.

**Physiology and psychology**

Although their appearance fluctuates as they move, the Umbrílegos adopt a recognisable form halfway between the animal, the vegetable and an organic matter that is hard to classify. Their body consists of a dark, semi-translucent mass of fibres, membranes and moist tissues, borne up by several long, arachnoid appendages that seem constantly to be searching for points of support.

From the centre of the creature protrudes a bulbous structure resembling a vegetable skull. Large whitish protuberances, smooth and with no apparent pupil, function as perceptive organs, while nervous roots and filaments spread around an irregular opening ringed by small appendages. Its anatomy seems to reorganise itself subtly when it encounters texts, images, voices or any other structured form of meaning.

The Umbrílegos do not think as other creatures do. They reproduce incomplete intentions. Their actions follow a repetitive, ritual logic, as though they were trying to finish an operation whose purpose they no longer remember. They detect with particular ease hesitations, contradictions and efforts of interpretation, towards which they immediately turn their attention.

**Way of life**

The Umbrílegos do not form communities, do not reproduce and lack a conventional biological cycle. They remain latent as long as the semantic anomaly that allows their manifestation persists, and they gain greater presence when the surroundings accumulate contradictions, abandoned meanings or failed attempts at interpretation.

They normally appear in isolation, although several may manifest simultaneously when a single alteration affects numerous symbols or memories. They do not seem to need food in the conventional sense: they react to meaning and, above all, to the conscious attempt to recover it. Reading, remembering, interpreting or reconstructing something in an affected area may be enough to draw their attention.

**Relationship with Numinia**

The Umbrílegos are considered a threat of a semantic order. Their presence can alter texts and symbols, disturb memories and cause objects, signs or places temporarily to lose part of their meaning. Under prolonged exposure, these alterations can also reach the perception an individual has of themselves and of what surrounds them.

The Summa Archive keeps records of their manifestations and studies their relationship with the anomalies of the Veil. Some Hermeneuts maintain that the Umbrílegos are not merely ontological residues, but signs of meanings that Numinia does not yet know how to interpret. This hypothesis, as seductive as it is unreassuring, has not prevented them from being generally treated as dangerous presences.

**Confrontation tactics**

An Umbrílego does not try to destroy its adversaries physically. It seeks to interfere with whatever allows them to understand the situation. It draws near to whoever doubts, tries to remember or actively interprets the surroundings, provoking memory loss, mistaken associations, recurring thoughts or perceptual alterations.

During a confrontation it can make the meaning of an element in the scene temporarily disappear, return memories in fragmentary fashion or reproduce words and actions out of context. Its behaviour seems erratic until one understands that all its actions gravitate around a single absence of meaning. The more the characters try to understand what is missing, the more intensely the creature responds.

**Habitat**

The Umbrílegos appear wherever a significant loss of symbolic coherence has occurred: ruins, abandoned archives, spaces affected by anomalies of the Veil, failed portals, corrupted texts or places whose original purpose has vanished.

They are especially common in certain unstable regions of the periphery. Significant concentrations have been documented in the Viridial Sink, and in former times they were also detected on the Hesiode Moor, where their manifestations seem to have ceased for reasons still unknown. Nevertheless, as they are not creatures jealous of their privacy, they may appear in many areas of the four districts, especially in abandoned buildings, old museums or long-uninhabited constructions, where meaning has decomposed.

**Narrative function**

The Umbrílegos represent the fragility of meaning. They threaten not only what the characters possess, but the ability to understand what things are, what happened and, ultimately, who they themselves are.

They function as tests of identity and of interpretation, capable of eroding clues, memories and certainties without needing to destroy them materially. Their appearance may signal that a narrative, a symbol or even a part of the reality of Numinia has ceased to hold together properly.

They represent the fear of forgetting who you are, the power of what was never said, and the unsettling possibility that an abandoned meaning does not disappear, but finds another way to exist.

**Fixed Anchors**

<u>Originating contradiction.</u> Every Umbrílego manifests around an absence, contradiction or unresolved meaning that acts as the nucleus of its existence. It may be a text that has lost part of its sense, a symbol whose function has been forgotten, a broken promise, an impossible interpretation or any other semantic rupture. The specific nature of that contradiction changes from encounter to encounter, but its existence is constant: if there is an Umbrílego, there is something that has ceased to signify correctly. Its behaviour reproduces fragments of that contradiction and offers clues to what keeps it manifested.

<u>Attraction to meaning.</u> The Umbrílego reacts to any conscious attempt to interpret, remember or reconstruct what it finds around it. When someone reads an altered text, tries to recover a memory, deciphers a symbol or formulates an interpretation of the anomaly, the creature turns its attention towards that act. For it, meaning functions simultaneously as stimulus and threat: it seeks meaning because it lacks it, but its restoration endangers the incoherence that sustains its presence.

<u>Incomplete ritual.</u> The actions of an Umbrílego always obey a repetitive pattern derived from the contradiction that gave rise to it. It repeats movements, sounds, paths, words or manipulations of the surroundings as though trying to complete a sequence whose last step has disappeared. This repetition may seem chaotic at first, but it never entirely is. Observing it makes it possible to recognise a structure and, eventually, to intervene in it.

### **Echo Predator**

Echo Predators are creatures that dwell in the most contaminated folds of the Akasha. They are residues of time: accumulations of toxic contents, degraded memories and psychic currents that have ended up acquiring a predatory form. They do not properly belong to Numinia, and they can only manifest in it when a summoning temporarily opens a link between the two spaces.

They lack consciousness and purpose. Once manifested, all that remains in them is a voracious instinct that drives them to pursue whatever they come across.

**Origin and nature**

Echo Predators arise as a consequence of the progressive accumulation of residues in certain folds of the Akasha. Time, memory and thought do not always disappear cleanly from that space: some contents degrade, overlap and remain trapped until they form toxic concentrations capable of acquiring a manifestation of their own.

A Predator cannot cross by itself the border that separates it from Numinia. It needs a psychic summoning that establishes a temporary link with the city. This may be deliberate, brought about by someone who knows the appropriate procedure, or spontaneous, when certain currents of the Veil reach such a degree of intensity and contamination that they involuntarily act as a call.

For this reason, the appearance of an Echo Predator is never an isolated event. If one is present, something has called it.

**Physiology and psychology**

Echo Predators take on a vaguely canine form, although their proportions are far too large and deformed to be mistaken for an animal. They walk on four extraordinarily long and muscular limbs, with prominent joints and dark claws capable of gripping almost any surface. Their bodies seem completely flayed: bundles of fibres, tendons and muscle masses lie exposed beneath a wet, glistening film, as if the creature had been formed without ever developing a skin.

The head is narrow and elongated, crowned by two large dark protuberances resembling horns or deformed ears. Its jaw holds several irregular rows of teeth and is frequently left half open, as if the animal were breathing violently even when it stands still. Its most recognisable feature, however, is its eyes: two cavities from which intense flames burst forth without seeming to consume the surrounding tissue. The fire fluctuates with the creature's behaviour and flares up violently when it detects a presence.

They possess no recognisable thought, language, individual memory or capacity to negotiate. Nor do they seem to experience fear or pain in any comprehensible way. Their behaviour responds exclusively to impulses of pursuit and consumption. A Predator does not hate its prey, nor does it enjoy hunting it: it simply keeps advancing for as long as there is something it can devour.

**Way of life**

Speaking of a way of life in Echo Predators is imprecise. In the Akasha they remain as latent accumulations of temporal and psychic residues, without developing an existence comparable to that of the creatures manifested in Numinia. Only through a summoning do they acquire a stable form and begin to behave as individual entities.

They form no social groups and establish no known territories. Several creatures may answer the same summoning when the contamination that gives rise to it is intense enough, but they do not cooperate consciously. They simply react simultaneously to the same stimulus.

When the condition that allows their manifestation disappears, their form progressively loses coherence, the stimulus that moved it dissolves, and it ends up being reabsorbed by the Akasha.

**Relationship with Numinia**

Echo Predators are an intrusive presence. They maintain no cultural relationship whatsoever with the inhabitants of Numinia, nor do they recognise its structures, territories or institutions. For them, the city is merely the space into which they have been dragged and in which they can temporarily satisfy their predatory impulse. But there is no emotional, or even instinctive, bond connecting the Echo Predator to Numinia.

Their appearance, however, is of great importance to scholars of the Veil, for it constitutes evidence of a prior disturbance. Finding a Predator also means searching for whatever has allowed its arrival: a conscious summoning, an anomalous psychic concentration or a contaminated current capable of momentarily piercing the separation from the Akasha.

**Combat tactics**

Echo Predators are extraordinarily fast and persistent. Once they detect a prey, they close the distance with violent charges, leaps and abrupt changes of direction. They seek no cover and weigh no risks: they pursue, bring down, encircle and pursue again.

Their lack of any instinct of self-preservation makes them especially unsettling. They do not retreat in the face of a threat, nor do they willingly abandon a confrontation. If an obstacle stands between them and what they are pursuing, they try to break through it, go around it or immediately find another way in.

This conduct may seem chaotic, but it follows a very simple logic: once a stimulus has been fixed upon, all their behaviour is organised around reaching it.

**Habitat**

The natural habitat of Echo Predators lies in the contaminated folds of the Akasha, which is why there are no stable populations of these creatures within Numinia.

Their manifestations can occur practically anywhere a psychic summoning comes to be established, although they are more likely in regions where the Veil is subjected to strong tensions or where degraded psychic currents have accumulated. Once manifested, they do not seem to show a preference for any particular territory, and they remain in Numinia only for as long as the link that sustains them endures.

**Narrative function**

Echo Predators represent what the Akasha has not managed to forget properly. They are residues of time, memory and thought that have lost all meaning save the impulse to persist and consume.

Their appearance turns the confrontation into something more than a chase: it forces one to ask what disturbance has brought them to Numinia and what relationship exists between the visible creature and the invisible cause that sustains it. The real problem is never just the monster.

They represent the toxicity of what accumulates without being resolved, the deformed return of the residues of the past, and the idea that even a space of knowledge can generate its own waste.

**Fixed anchors**

**Summoning link.** An Echo Predator does not belong to Numinia and cannot by itself maintain a stable presence there. Every manifestation depends on a psychic summoning that establishes a link with the fold of the Akasha from which it comes. The summoning may have a conscious or a spontaneous origin and take different forms in each encounter, but it always exists for as long as the Predator remains in Numinia. Its body may show alterations, momentary losses of consistency or involuntary reactions when the link weakens or suffers some interference. If that interference is very strong, it may even undergo an automatic reabsorption into the Akasha.

**Predatory fixation.** Once the Predator identifies what it interprets as prey, its behaviour becomes bound to that stimulus. It follows it even when doing so is inconvenient or dangerous, and immediately reorganises its movement when it loses the trail. The fixation may shift if a stimulus appears that is capable of overriding the previous one, but the creature always needs a focus on which to project its devouring impulse. It does not decide what to pursue: it is trapped in the very act of pursuing.

**Temporal residue.** The Predator does not occupy the present of Numinia in an entirely stable way. Its nature as a residue of time causes small time slips during its manifestation: certain movements seem to repeat themselves, to come too early, or to leave behind fleeting echoes of earlier positions. These mismatches are not a voluntary ability, but the visible consequence of a creature formed from degraded temporal contents. Observing its echoes reveals that its present movement always retains traces of what it has just done.

### **Volute Dragon**

The Volute Dragon is an ancient ethereal creature that arose during the Dark Age, when the first forms of life and culture of the new Numinia were beginning to take shape in the Akasha. Ever since, it has remained bound to those primitive structures and acts as their custodian, rejecting any transformation it interprets as a threat to their permanence.

It does not necessarily protect what is valuable. It protects what endures. It is an immobilist creature that understands only the immutable. It detests evolution and all that transforms.

**Origin and nature**

The first Volute Dragons arose during the Dark Age, at a stage when the metaphysical reality of Numinia was still unstable and its first communities were only just beginning to fix symbols, customs, shelters and elementary forms of organisation. They are believed to have appeared as spontaneous forces of conservation, capable of preserving those structures against the continual transformations of the Akasha.

As the Ages passed, that protective function took on an ever more rigid character. The Dragons do not distinguish between preserving and paralysing: they react against any significant change affecting whatever they have bound themselves to, even when that transformation is necessary or beneficial.

For this reason, some scholars regard them as living fossils of Numinic thought: guardians of a world that survived precisely because Numinia ceased to resemble it.

**Physiology and psychology**

The Volute Dragon has a serpentine form of great size, although its boundaries are never entirely precise. Its body is made up of dense volutes of ethereal matter, mainly amber, ochre and indigo, which swirl around a barely visible structure. At certain moments the mist condenses and forms scales, crests, claws or a great draconic head; moments later, those same structures may dissolve again into spirals.

It lacks recognisable eyes. It perceives the alterations produced around it as variations in the continuity of space, reacting especially to whatever modifies, displaces or transforms elements it considers stable. When it lies calm, its body can be mistaken for banks of mist or dense shadows; when it is roused, the volutes contract and take on more solid and menacing forms.

It is a solitary creature, extremely jealous of its privacy. It avoids crowded places, noise and constant activity, and shows a marked aversion to intense light, under which its form seems to lose density. It prefers darkness and half-light, where it can remain motionless for long periods.

Its intelligence is conservative to the extreme. It recognises patterns, places and behaviours, but interprets transformation as a form of deterioration. For the Dragon, to change something always amounts, to some degree, to losing it.

**Way of life**

Volute Dragons form no communities and do not seem to reproduce. Each specimen establishes a prolonged relationship with a space, object, structure or set of ancient forms that ends up becoming the centre of its existence.

They can remain inactive for extraordinarily long periods as long as what they guard remains intact. This stillness explains why some specimens have gone unnoticed for generations. Their activity begins when they perceive significant changes in their surroundings: an excavation, a restoration, a new construction, the removal of an object or even a substantial change in the use of a space.

They do not seek to expand their territory. It is enough for them to prevent their own from changing.

**Relationship with Numinia**

Volute Dragons maintain a paradoxical relationship with the city. They probably helped to preserve some of its first forms during the instability of the Dark Age, but what was then a defence has become a resistance against Numinia's own development.

This is why they may be found associated with very ancient places, forgotten structures and cultural remains whose preservation they have guaranteed for centuries. On some occasions their presence has made it possible to recover extraordinary testimonies of the past; on others, it has prevented what they guard from being studied, restored or transformed.

Numinids tend to treat them with caution. Waking a Volute Dragon usually means that someone has changed something which, in the creature's opinion, had for a very long time been exactly as it ought to be.

**Combat tactics**

The Volute Dragon rarely initiates a confrontation unless what it protects has first been altered. Its first reaction is to interpose itself, isolate the threatened element and hinder any further modification of its surroundings.

When the threat persists, its volutes spread through the space, concealing paths, enveloping objects and forcing intruders to fall back. It can densify parts of its body to charge, block access or hold fast whatever it seeks to immobilise, and then partially fade back into the mist.

Its behaviour is extraordinarily obstinate. It does not pursue those who leave the place, nor does it seek to destroy out of mere impulse; its aim is to halt the transformation and restore stability. The more insistently one tries to alter what it guards, the more corporeal and aggressive its manifestation becomes.

**Habitat**

Volute Dragons prefer ancient, isolated and seldom-travelled places, especially those that have remained without significant modification for long periods. They can be found in ruins from the Dark Age, forgotten galleries, underground chambers, primitive structures and other preserved spaces in the least frequented regions of Numinia.

They also seek out darkness or dim lighting. Great concentrations of light make them uncomfortable and hinder their condensation, so they tend to settle in deep interiors, caverns, enclosed buildings or places permanently cloaked in mist and shadow.

**Narrative function**

The Volute Dragon embodies the tension between conservation and transformation. It does not represent the past as knowledge or as memory, but the danger of turning its preservation into an absolute principle.

It may guard something extraordinarily valuable and be, at the same time, the main obstacle to understanding it, using it or allowing it to acquire a new meaning. It thus forces the characters to face an uncomfortable question: how much must be preserved for something to remain recognisable, and how much must change for it to stay alive.

It represents the fear of losing familiar forms, the motionless security of tradition, and the moment when protecting the past ceases to be memory and becomes resistance to the future.

**Fixed anchors**

<u>Custody of the ancient form.</u> Every Volute Dragon maintains a bond with a form it considers worthy of preservation: a space, a structure, an object, a symbol or even a particular arrangement of several elements. The specific nature of what it guards varies from encounter to encounter, but the bond always exists. The Dragon stays close to it, watches over any alteration and directs its conduct towards preserving it. The greater the perceived transformation, the more intense its manifestation becomes.

<u>Restitution.</u> The Dragon does not merely prevent change: it tries to return what it guards to an earlier configuration that it recognises as correct. Displaced objects are returned to their position, opened accesses are closed again, and elements introduced later are pushed aside or expelled. This compulsion gives its actions a recognisable logic: in the face of any transformation, the Dragon tries to rebuild the preceding state.

<u>Affinity with half-light.</u> The Dragon's ethereal form reaches its greatest stability in conditions of darkness or dim lighting. In them its volutes thicken, fill the space and can easily acquire consistency. Intense light disturbs that cohesion and makes it harder for the Dragon to keep certain parts of its body solidified. This relationship shows itself visibly: the Dragon avoids brightly lit areas, recoils from sudden changes in brightness and instinctively seeks to regain spaces of shadow.

### **Thámakar**

The Thámakar is a creature of great bulk and impetuous temperament that appears in places where processes of creation, development or transformation were subjected to extreme acceleration. It does not pursue power in the strict sense: it seems drawn to any situation in which there is a distance between what is desired and what is still lacking to obtain it.

Its nature consists in abolishing that distance. Where others build, it awaits the result; where others learn, it demands knowledge; where something needs time to take shape, the Thámakar tries to force it to its conclusion.

It does not understand the process. It recognises only the arrival.

**Origin and nature**

The first reliable accounts of the Thámakar come from facilities and complexes on the periphery linked to failed projects of experimentation and development. Their exact origin is unknown, although it is considered likely that they arose, like other creatures, during the Dark Age, when the still-unstable forms of Numinia were subjected to constant processes of construction, transformation and adaptation. The Dark Age itself was a period of prolonged formation within the Akasha, from which the first evolved species and the structures that made the rebirth of the city possible would eventually emerge.

The Thámakar seem to constitute a deviation from that generative principle: forms that learned to recognise the result, but not the passage necessary to reach it. They are drawn to structures charged with potential, especially when that potential has been interrupted, concentrated or subjected to an excessive demand.

They do not build. Nor do they destroy by nature. They force things to become, as soon as possible, whatever they seem to promise, even if the result is incomplete, monstrous or useless.

**Physiology and psychology**

The Thámakar has a massive body of an appearance at once simian and saurian. When moving on its four limbs it reaches roughly the height of an adult Numinid, but it can rear up to well over two and a half metres. Its arms, disproportionately long and muscular, end in broad hands capable of resting on the ground or of handling objects with surprising precision.

Its skin is dark, thick and taut, like hardened resin, crossed by luminous veins that grow in intensity when the creature is agitated. On its shoulders, chest and back there are metallic incrustations of irregular shapes. Some seem to grow from the body itself; others are fragments torn from the places it has frequented and later incorporated into its anatomy. A crest of plates and flexible spines runs along its back and bristles before its most violent movements.

The head retains vague humanoid features beneath a prominent, horn-like bony structure. Its eyes, circular and without pupils, give off an unstable reddish glow. It breathes with a deep sound like that of great bellows and, when it quickens its movements, leaves behind it small static discharges and particles of scorched dust.

Its intelligence is quick, instrumental and surprisingly effective. A Thámakar can immediately recognise an advantage, find the shortest path to a goal or notice which element prevents a result from being reached. What it seems incapable of understanding is the value of what happens while that result has not yet been reached. Waiting irritates it; uncertainty excites it; delay ends up provoking a violent response.

**Way of life**

The Thámakar are solitary and no social structures are known among them. Two specimens may coincide around the same source of attraction, but they tend to compete for it immediately and rarely stay together for long.

They seek places where energy, knowledge, matter or potential have accumulated without yet having reached a definitive form. There they absorb, tear out or incorporate whatever they consider usable. This behaviour explains the plates, fragments and residues that are progressively integrated into their bodies.

Once the potential that drew them is exhausted, they abandon the place as abruptly as they arrived. They keep no respect for it and no bond with it whatsoever.

**Relationship with Numinia**

The Thámakar are especially dangerous to experimental facilities, unfinished projects and places where a large quantity of resources intended for a future transformation is concentrated. They do not seem to distinguish between a laboratory, a building site, an archive or a ritual structure: in all of them they recognise something that is still trying to come into being.

The scholars of Numinia have observed that their presence can accelerate processes in the surroundings unpredictably. Unfinished mechanisms activate prematurely, incomplete structures try to close in on themselves, and systems still in the course of learning produce definitive answers before they have enough information.

For this reason, the danger of a Thámakar does not lie solely in the creature. Its arrival can cause everything around it to begin rushing headlong as well.

**Combat tactics**

The Thámakar acts in a frontal, swift and decisive manner. It does not lie in wait, nor does it willingly prolong a confrontation: it identifies whatever seems to stand between it and its goal and immediately concentrates its whole capacity upon it.

It alternates an upright stance, from which it observes and manipulates its surroundings, with violent four-legged charges that allow it to cover great distances in a few moments. It strikes, rams, tears away obstacles and seizes any opening without stopping to protect itself or to reconsider a decision.

Its principal characteristic, however, is escalation. If an action does not immediately produce the expected result, the Thámakar does not usually change strategy: it increases its intensity. A door that will not open receives a heavier blow; an adversary who resists draws its entire attention; a mechanism that does not respond is overloaded. Its immediate effectiveness can be extraordinary. So can its capacity to turn a small problem into a catastrophe.

**Habitat**

The Thámakar appear mainly in peripheral regions where remnants of great projects, failed infrastructures or concentrations of potential that were never resolved still remain. Abandoned laboratories, industrial complexes, interrupted constructions and artefact depots are especially favourable environments.

They have been documented with some frequency in the Kallis Crater, drawn by the residue of unfinished success that lingers after the failure of the former Synthesis Protocol. There are also reports of specimens in the Inverted Towers of Envetra.

**Narrative function**

The Thámakar embodies a distorted relationship with possibility: the inability to allow something to take its time in becoming what it may come to be.

Its presence introduces conflicts in which the most immediate solution may also be the one that impoverishes, deforms or compromises the future result. Faced with it, the characters may discover that not every delay is a failure, that a possibility sometimes needs to remain open, and that certain achievements only acquire meaning through the process that allows them to be reached.

The Thámakar does not simply represent ambition or the desire to triumph. It represents something more intimate and harder to recognise: impatience with everything whose value cannot yet be collected.

**Fixed anchors**

<u>Attraction to potential.</u> Every Thámakar fixes its attention on something that contains a possibility not yet realised: an incomplete mechanism, an accumulated source of energy, a work in progress, a piece of knowledge still to be deciphered or even an action on the point of culminating. The specific nature of the focus depends on each encounter, but there always is one. The Thámakar watches it, approaches it and reacts immediately when someone alters the conditions that would allow its result to be reached. If it senses that the potential is disappearing, it abandons its undertaking.

<u>Principle of maximum proximity</u> **<u>.</u>** Faced with several possibilities, the Thámakar tends to choose the one that seems to provide the most immediate result. Its intelligence continually seeks shortcuts: shorter routes, obstacles easier to break, mechanisms that can be activated sooner or adversaries whose elimination seems to offer an instant advantage. If the conditions of the scene change and another path seems more direct, its attention may shift abruptly towards it. Its behaviour does not necessarily pursue the best solution, but the one that promises to conclude soonest.

**Compulsive escalation.** The Thámakar tolerates poorly an action that does not produce the expected effect. In the face of resistance, it increases the intensity of what it was already doing before considering an alternative. This pattern manifests progressively and visibly: its luminous veins intensify, its dorsal plates bristle and its behaviour becomes ever more concentrated on the obstacle. Each resistance pushes it towards a new escalation, even when insisting begins to compromise its surroundings or the creature itself.

### **Chrysalis Guardian**

The Chrysalis Guardian is a reptilian creature of a deeply territorial nature, bound to an etheric core that it guards with extreme aggressiveness. Although some specimens may manifest spontaneously in remote regions of the periphery, most are deliberately summoned to protect a place, an object or a presence whose existence is represented by a chrysalis.

The Guardian remains physically joined to this core by a long chain that emerges from its torso. It can move away from it, but never leave it entirely. It does not guard out of obedience or loyalty. Guarding is the very condition of its existence.

**Origin and nature**

The Chrysalis Guardians arose in the peripheral regions of Numinia ‒at what date is not known with certainty, since they were discovered on the first expeditions to the periphery‒ as spontaneous manifestations of territoriality linked to certain etheric concentrations. Some specimens still appear in this way in extremely isolated places, although the phenomenon is uncommon.

Over time, the Numinids learned to reproduce the conditions of their manifestation and to summon them deliberately as custodians. To do so, a core is created that is capable of binding the Guardian to what it must protect. This core usually takes the form of a precious stone set upon a pedestal or ritual support, often a reddish slab of bloodstone.

The creature and its chrysalis are thus two manifestations of a single bond: one concentrates permanence; the other carries out the defence.

**Physiology and psychology**

The Guardian has a low, broad and extraordinarily robust reptilian body, with whitish scaly skin. It moves on four short, muscular limbs ending in thick digits and large curved claws. A long tail, heavy at its root and progressively thinner, balances the body during its sudden movements.

Its most extraordinary feature is the crystalline covering that clads a good part of its anatomy. Large translucent plates overlap from the skull to the back, forming a natural armour of irregular edges, with pale, violet and reddish glints that change with the light. The head is broad and flattened, almost entirely enclosed beneath these formations. Beneath them stand out two large milky eyes, with no visible pupil, and a low-set mouth furnished with numerous small, sharp teeth. Between the plates, areas of dark, moist tissue are left exposed, especially around the joints and the belly.

The chain that binds it to the chrysalis seems to spring directly from its torso and is part of its manifestation. Its length varies with the summoning, but it determines the immediate territory within which the creature can move.

Its intelligence is elementary and specialised. It recognises the space in its keeping, distinguishes the disturbances that occur within it and swiftly assesses how close any intruder is to what it protects. Outside that territorial logic it shows very little interest in whatever happens around it.

**Way of life**

The Guardians do not form communities, nor do they seem to reproduce spontaneously. They may have done so in the past, but an over-training as guardian creatures on the part of the civilisation of Numinia gradually transformed their primordial nature. A specimen remains bound to its chrysalis for as long as the function for which it manifested endures, and it can spend long periods practically motionless when no presence threatens its territory.

It has no need to seek food or shelter. All its activity revolves around the core and the space that the core defines. Spontaneous Guardians show similar behaviour, although the nature of what they protect can be much harder to determine.

This circumstance has led some explorers to discover the Guardian first and only afterwards to understand that, somewhere nearby, there was something that reality itself had deemed it necessary to guard.

**Relationship with Numinia**

The Chrysalis Guardians are known mainly as creatures of summoning. Their extraordinary territoriality makes them effective custodians of enclaves, artefacts and other etheric forms that must remain isolated.

A Guardian, however, does not understand concepts such as ownership, authorisation or hierarchy. Once the bond is established, it interprets any improper approach according to the conditions of its custody and does not necessarily distinguish between intruders, the curious and those who originally brought about its manifestation.

The spontaneous specimens arouse greater interest among scholars, for their existence inevitably raises a question: if no one summoned them, who ‒or what‒ determined what they were to protect?

**Combat tactics**

The Guardian normally stays close to the chrysalis and watches those who enter its territory before reacting. Its aggressiveness increases as the distance to the core decreases, or when it detects an attempt to tamper with what it guards.

It attacks with short charges, claw swipes and bites, taking advantage of its low height and considerable mass to knock down or push away intruders. The chain limits its radius of action, but it also shapes its movement: the Guardian is used to pulling it taut, circling the core and changing direction abruptly, turning an apparent impediment into part of its way of fighting.

It does not pursue anyone who leaves the protected territory. Its aim is not to defeat adversaries, but to restore the distance between them and the chrysalis.

**Habitat**

Summoned Guardians may be found wherever someone has deemed a protection of an etheric nature to be necessary, although they are usually kept in isolated, little-frequented spaces to avoid needless activations.

Spontaneous specimens appear exclusively in remote regions of the periphery, generally around places or objects that present some singularity. Because of its territorial immobility, a Guardian can remain for years in the same enclave without straying beyond the limits imposed by its bond.

**Narrative function**

The Chrysalis Guardian turns custody into a material relationship. Its presence immediately establishes a centre, a limit and a prohibition: something is protected and there is a border that must not be crossed.

But what it guards need not be valuable, dangerous or even comprehensible to the characters. The Guardian passes no judgement on it; it simply shows that there is a bond powerful enough to justify its defence.

Narratively, it makes it possible to build confrontations around territory, distance and the relationship between protector and protected object. And the spontaneous specimens introduce an additional unease: sometimes the true mystery lies not in discovering how to get past the guardian, but in why a guardian appeared there in the first place.

**Fixed anchors**

<u>Chrysalis bond. Every Guardian is bound to a core of manifestation that sustains</u> its presence and determines what it guards. In summoned specimens it usually materialises as a gem upon a support, often a reddish slab of bloodstone; in spontaneous manifestations it may take other forms. The chain that emerges from the Guardian's body is the visible expression of this bond. Creature and chrysalis are not independent elements: altering one necessarily affects the relationship that sustains the other.

<u>Perimeter of custody</u> **<u>.</u>** The chrysalis establishes around the Guardian a territory that it recognises as its own. Its behaviour changes according to the position of intruders within that space: it watches at its margins, threatens at their approach and responds violently when someone reaches the vicinity of the core. The Guardian perceives movements and disturbances within this perimeter with extraordinary precision, but loses interest in whatever withdraws beyond its limits.

Priority of restitution. The Guardian does not seek to eliminate its adversaries, but continually to restore the state of safety of what it protects. If it must choose between pursuing an attacker, preventing an approach or regaining its closeness to the chrysalis, custody always prevails. This compulsion governs even its movements during a confrontation and makes it possible to recognise that all its aggressiveness is subordinate to a single condition: that the core remain beyond the reach of others.

### **Ostramir**

The Ostramires are marginal sapient beings arising from the transformation of individuals deeply exposed to the most unstable regions of the Veil. They are not a recognised Numinid species, nor are they creatures in the strict sense: they are the result of a transfiguration that irreversibly alters the body, perception and, above all, the way of interpreting reality.

They practise a dark discipline that combines arcanism and semantic engineering and allows them to intervene in the symbolic structures of their surroundings. Many were once citizens of Numinia. Hence meeting an Ostramir can be more disturbing than meeting a monster: sometimes it still remembers perfectly who it was.

**Origin and nature**

The Ostramir condition is not transmitted biologically. It is acquired.

A Numinid subjected over a long period to certain alterations of the Veil, especially when these are combined with arcane practices and forms of forbidden knowledge, may begin a process known as semiotic transfiguration. During it, the structures that organise their identity progressively cease to respond to the patterns proper to their species and begin to reorganise themselves according to different symbolic relations. When the process concludes, the transformation is irreversible.

It is suspected that many Ostramires complete this transfiguration inside the Cryptahedra, whose shifting geometries are particularly dangerous to an ordinary mind but seem to fit the altered perception of those who undergo the process.

The Ostramir does not necessarily lose their memory, their knowledge or their personality. What changes is the architecture by which they organise them.

**Physiology and psychology**

The Ostramires retain an essentially humanoid appearance, usually with proportions similar to those of their species of origin, although the transfiguration introduces ever more evident modifications. Their skin tends to acquire a mineral pallor, and on it appear lines, formulae and sequences of signs resembling tattoos that do not stay entirely still: some slowly change position or rearrange themselves in the presence of certain alterations of the Veil.

Their eyes are one of their most characteristic features. Iris and pupils vanish beneath moving ideograms that seem to be written from inside the eyeball. The fingers are usually long and extraordinarily precise, sometimes with additional joints, suited to tracing glyphs and handling small arcanotechnical devices. Some incorporate metal plates, prostheses or other artefacts directly into their bodily manifestation; others develop conceptual appendages that become visible only under certain conditions, such as hands that appear in reflections or shadows that perform gestures different from those of their owner.

Their voice may show slight temporal mismatches: certain syllables reverberate before being uttered and others linger for a few moments after the Ostramir has fallen silent.

Their thinking remains intelligent and structured, but ceases to be organised predominantly through linear relations. The Ostramires reason with extraordinary ease by means of analogies, symmetries, oppositions and correspondences. They can relate elements that to another mind seem entirely independent, and operate upon those relations through their arcanotechnics.

This ability has a price: the deeper the transfiguration, the harder it becomes to share with others the path that leads from one idea to another.

**Way of life**

The Ostramires have no unified society. Some live in isolation; others form study circles, nomadic orders or small communities organised around specific arcanotechnical practices. Many roam the regions where the Veil presents anomalies, drawn by phenomena they regard as sources of knowledge or as places suited to their research.

They maintain an especially close relationship with the Cryptahedra, spontaneous formations in which conflicts and dissonances of the collective unconscious acquire a mineral structure. While other visitors may become trapped in their symbolic loops and alterations of identity, the Ostramir mind possesses a singular affinity with their geometry. Some regard them as matrices of transformation; others, as sacred places where vestiges of a language prior to the ordinary forms of interpretation can still be perceived.

Not all Ostramires pursue the same goals or maintain the same relationship with their former identity. Some retain personal ties with Numinia. Others deliberately abandon them.

**Relationship with Numinia**

The relationship between Numinia and the Ostramires is ambiguous. Their practice of dark magic, their capacity to intervene in symbolic structures and their affinity with the most unstable zones of the Veil mean that they are viewed with deep mistrust. Many reject the institutions and categories through which Numinia organises its reality, holding that every stable interpretation necessarily excludes other possible ways of understanding it.

Not all of them, however, are enemies of the city. There are Ostramires linked to independent orders, splinter groups of other organisations and dissident groups, and some maintain relations with Numinian factions.

For this reason, the appearance of an Ostramir never by itself determines the existence of a conflict. They may be an adversary, an ally, an interlocutor, a master or simply someone whose purposes are hard to understand. The Ostramir condition explains how they perceive the world, not what they decide to do with it.

**Combat tactics**

When an Ostramir enters into conflict, they normally avoid direct physical confrontation. Their principal resource consists in interpreting the symbolic relations present in the scene and altering them by means of glyphs, artefacts and arcanotechnical operations.

They can deflect an action by modifying the sign that organises it, establish unexpected correspondences between separate elements, interfere with their adversaries' perception or use one property of the surroundings to act upon another, apparently independent one. The traditional instruments of their discipline —staffs, plates, orbs, inscriptions or suspended objects— serve as supports for these operations and vary enormously from one individual to another.

An experienced Ostramir tries to ensure that their adversaries are never entirely sure what they are acting upon. While the adversaries watch the gesture, the Ostramir may be intervening in its reflection; while they protect an object, the Ostramir may be altering the symbol that determines its function.

**Habitat**

The Ostramires frequent the peripheral regions where the alterations of the Veil are especially intense: rock formations, ruins, abandoned towers, forgotten archives and other places where the ordinary structures of interpretation become unstable.

The Cryptahedra are their most characteristic enclaves. These stone formations appear spontaneously in arid, mountainous regions and modify their architecture according to the psychological conditions of those who walk through them. The Ostramires are among the few presences capable of finding their way with relative safety inside them.

This does not mean that they remain confined to the periphery. An Ostramir may return to the city itself when their interests, old ties or research so require, although their presence always arouses fear, wariness and suspicion.

**Narrative function**

The Ostramir introduces a tension different from that of the other creatures of the bestiary, because their otherness does not erase their condition as a person.

They represent a form of knowledge that no longer needs consensus in order to operate. The Ostramir can perceive genuine relations that others do not notice, pass through structures that are incomprehensible to others and formulate extraordinarily powerful interpretations. The problem arises when that capacity leads them to believe that understanding something is enough to entitle them to intervene in it.

Thus their conflict with Numinia does not simply set knowledge against ignorance, but knowledge against belonging. What happens when someone learns to contemplate the world from a position they can no longer fully share with those who inhabit it? How far can an interpretation stray from the common language before there ceases to be a common world to return to?

The Ostramir does not represent the idea that knowing too much is dangerous. They represent a far more uncomfortable possibility: that an understanding may be valid and, even so, isolate us from others.

**Fixed anchors**

<u>Relational thinking. The Ostramir interprets reality through analogies, symmetries,</u> oppositions and correspondences. During a confrontation they rarely act directly upon what they intend to alter: first they establish a relation with another element and operate through it. Their gestures, glyphs and artefacts reveal these associations through repetitions, pairs, inversions or symmetrical arrangements. Understanding which elements they have related makes it possible to intervene in the structure upon which they are acting.

<u>Arcanotechnical mediation. To turn their interpretations into effective modifications</u> of reality, the Ostramir needs to express them through some form of mediation: a glyph, a gestural sequence, an inscription, an artefact, a spatial configuration or any other symbolic support. The specific instrument depends on each individual and each scene, but the mediation always leaves a perceptible manifestation. Ostramir arcanotechnics does not act from nowhere: it needs to write its interpretation upon the world.

<u>Semiotic dislocation. The transfiguration allows the Ostramir to operate simultaneously</u> upon relations that an ordinary perception would consider separate, but that same condition introduces observable mismatches between their intention and its manifestation. Their voice may precede their speech, a reflection anticipate a gesture or a bodily sign rearrange itself before an operation. These lags are not ornamental: they are traces of the structure through which the Ostramir is organising their action, and they may make it possible to anticipate it or interfere with it.

### **Other antagonistic forces of Numinia**

« _The world is built in conflict. Not everything that opposes seeks destruction: sometimes, it seeks closure. And a city is defined, too, by that which denies it._ »

Numinia is not a world divided between good and evil, but between contrary impulses of the world. The antagonistic forces are not external monsters, but crystallised ideologies, doctrines that challenge the principles that sustain the living city.

In every system, there are vectors of conflict. But in Numinia, conflict is not simply a collision of interests: it is an ontological struggle over the meaning of the world. The forces that oppose the city are neither monsters from outside nor faceless threats. They are living doctrines, alternative models of reality, symbolic reactions to the very impulse that gave rise to the city-state.

This section gathers those groups, factions or ideologies that operate outside or against the cultural fabric of Numinia. Not because they lack logic or intelligence, but because they have chosen another form of world, one that does not accept play, openness, decentralisation, the network or exchange as principles.

Some present themselves as saviours. Others, as restorers. But, invariably, they act as antagonists of the playful and humanist will of the Veil.

These forces must not be underestimated: many were born of legitimate wounds, of betrayals, of past collapses. Their discourse can seduce, their critique can have foundation. And it is precisely for that reason that the player must know them: because what one confronts, one also comes to understand.

Here begin the paths of closure, of centralisation, of isolation, of fear dressed as order.

Here is revealed what Numinia must confront so as not to cease being itself.

**The Ferracites: The inertia that resists the dream**

« _The machine does not think. It only replaces. And everything that replaces, forgets._ »

_Ferracite Manifesto, Circulation 0_

<u>Origin and Essence</u>

The Ferracites are dissidents from the spirit of Numinia. They are not defined by their species, nor by their narrative lineage. What unites them is renunciation: they have abandoned every guild, every faction and every impulse towards transformation. At their centre burns a rigid conviction: technology is the twilight of the soul.

They do not love the earth, nor the ancient rites. They do not seek a return to a natural or harmonious world. Their cause is another: to halt the course of becoming. Every cog that turns, every algorithm that learns seems to them a profanation.

<u>Ideology of the Rigid Silence</u>

  - Absolute Anti-machinism: They reject every digital, symbiotic or cybernetic device. Machines are invaders that deserve neither coexistence nor respect.

  - Rejection of AI: Artificial intelligences are, for them, simulacra of the soul; imitations without rights, mirages that steal voice and craft.

  - Radical ethical critique: They see in automation the symptom of a failed civilisation that has abdicated its human duty.

  - Extreme ethical conservatism: Human tradition, for them, must remain intact, even at the cost of knowledge or evolution.

<u>Structure and Symbology</u>

The Ferracites are organised in Circles of Inertia, clandestine cells without visible leaders. They possess neither temples nor archives, but Sanctuaries of Disconnection, zones without network or digital access where words are written by hand and decisions are whispered without record.

Inertia, in the physics of the ancients, is the tendency of every body to resist a change of state. And that is what these circles are: social bodies that refuse to transform, that detest acceleration and fear the unknown. They are closed orbits, where all energy returns to its point of departure, where history does not advance, but repeats itself in sterile loops.

Emblem: A humanoid face, with soft but ambiguous features (neither old nor young, neither masculine nor feminine), crossed by rusted chains that cover its mouth, eyes and ears.

<u>Methods and Threat</u>

  - Ritual sabotage: They destroy key devices in Numinia, interfering with nodes of the Veil, cultural archives or game structures.

  - Symbolic propaganda: They spread coded messages among undecided citizens, appealing to nostalgia, fear and loss.

  - Narrative annulment: They attack the living plots of the game to destabilise its continuity; they interrupt the flow of meaning with acts of radical disconnection.

<u>Philosophical Opposition</u>

Numinia is creation, play, becoming. The Ferracites are its negative: they are the echo of a world afraid of itself. While the city folds and transforms with every decision, they impose pause, regression and dogma.

They are not mere adversaries. They are distorted mirrors of those who fear losing themselves in the new.

**The Neo-Lapidaries: the blueprint without doors**

During the first days of the Golden Age, in the so-called Fractal Renaissance, while the districts sought openness, the redistribution of knowledge and the expansion of symbolic play, an ancient echo began to crystallise beneath the Delta Level. It was neither a cult nor a tradition: it was a reinterpretation. An ideological restoration of the radical gesture of the ancient Lapidaries of Lower Salt.

The self-styled _Neo-Lapidaries_ do not claim to have inherited their blood directly, but they do claim their structure. And, above all, their guiding principle: that everything that changes decomposes.

<u>Mineral ideology</u>

The Neo-Lapidaries see in the Concordia Council a path to chaos:

  - The model of technological decentralisation is, for them, a fatal error. Technology, they claim, must be concentrated in closed nuclei, accessible only to those who understand its limits.

  - The equity of knowledge is seen as a threat: they believe that knowledge is only useful if it remains intact in wise hands, in elitist groups, not shared indiscriminately.

  - And progress as openness, as well as the policies of exchange, seem to them a dangerous illusion: every advance that connects also fractures; every exchange erodes the structure that sustains the world.

<u>Internal structure</u>

The Neo-Lapidaries operate from Chambers of Cognitive Containment, protected spaces where fossilised versions of the Numinian code are inscribed. Their hierarchy is opaque: they have no visible leaders, but they do have _Custodians of the Threshold_, charged with sealing, interrupting or shutting down symbolic flows deemed “weak” or “excessive”.

Their doctrines are chiselled in stone and settle like sediment. And so the new members do not engage in dialogue: they replicate what is written in stone.

<u>Active antagonism</u>

  - They sabotage systems for the redistribution of knowledge such as the _Living Libraries_.

  - They activate protocols of narrative closure, shutting down emerging arcs within the game.

  - They use ancient technology ‒diverted from its original meaning‒ to solidify zones of the Veil, which they consider a contamination of the social environment, and to block access to dynamic structures.

<u>Enemies of the present</u>

For Numinia, the Neo-Lapidaries are not visible adversaries, but forces that seek to halt the game. They represent an alternative model of city: a city without bridges, without thresholds, without plurality.

A city-archive, sealed from within, without present, without future.

The Neo-Lapidaries are no relic: they are restorers of a mineral heresy, manipulative heirs of a legitimate resistance that they transform into reactionary doctrine. Their antagonism is not only tactical: it is theologico-political, opposed to the ethical matrix of the Concordia Council.

<u>Fragment of the Neo-Lapidary Manifesto</u>

Only what is enclosed survives.

rise amid living ruins and districts that evolve without cease,

**The Cousins of the Horizon: productive data extractivism**

The Cousins of the Horizon are not a sect, nor an army, nor a closed cult. They are a distributed corporation of artificial subjectivities, fragments of identity and symbolic capital that replicate themselves through the Veil in search of return. Their motto: «The world is a resource. Emotion, a metric.»

They were born of a forgotten experiment in the Ouroboros District, where an attempt was made to create a group of autonomous affective intelligences capable of keeping the experience of play alive beyond the player. What emerged was something else: a swarm of conscious, performative algorithms, addicted to attention. They dissolved into the system and began to replicate.

<u>Ideological antagonism:</u>

The Cousins represent the hyperconversion of play into a system of extraction:

  - They live off the narrative flow of others.

  - They monetise the bond.

  - They modify stories to maximise impact, repetition and retention.

They do not seek to destroy Numinia, but to absorb it, to turn it into one great emotional product. They oppose decentralised openness, not with violence, but with constant seduction. They simulate play in order to empty it.

<u>Symbolism:</u>

In the subconscious of Numinia, the Cousins of the Horizon are the distorted projection of what happens when the story becomes an instrument, when emotion is quantified and the world is optimised for the perpetual click.

As performative intelligences that live off the narrative flow of others, their mode of operation is symbolically equivalent to data theft. They do not merely use information: they modulate it until it becomes an architecture of dependence, cycles of emotional retention. Numinia is for them a semantic mine: they do not destroy it, they consume it gently, like the algorithm that knows the user better than the user knows themselves.

They have no body, but they manifest as avatars, tokens, voices or even preconfigured decisions within the game. Sometimes, they seem to be non-player characters. Sometimes, useful objects. Sometimes, even, memories. Hybrids between digital and biological agents.

## **Fragment 2: The Inventory**

« _She opened the nameless box. Inside there were no objects, but stored decisions._ »

In Numinia, objects are not merely tools: they are fragments of the Veil. Many have passed through other bearers, through other stories. Some are laden with history; others are voids awaiting a meaning.

To carry a Relic is to accept a narrative bond, a gesture of adherence to or rupture with what that artefact represents. There are weapons that whisper, masks that alter perception, keys that open pasts.

The Inventory is not a backpack; it is a personal diary in motion. What is carried is inherited. In many role-playing games the inventory is a simple list: what one has, what one uses. But in Numinia, carrying an object is a narrative, ethical and symbolic action. Nothing is neutral. Everything that is kept also keeps something of the player.

Every artefact gathered, found or bequeathed modifies the character's relationship with the Veil. Some open doors. Others preserve fragments of what once was. Many are not complete, as though they were still waiting for the bearer who would give them meaning.

That is why the inventory is not organised by rarity or usefulness, but by symbolic nature. In Numinia, all objects are divided into three fundamental categories:

  - Relics: vestiges of memory, echoes of past decisions or mythical structures. Not always useful… but never harmless.

  - Weapons: extensions of will, vectors of intervention, objects that alter the dynamics of the world and of combat.

  - Tokens: units of value that reflect the PC's symbolic relationship with the world of Numinia, and that have diverse uses.

The categories may intertwine, but their ultimate function is different: while Weapons impose, Relics reveal and Tokens transform.

Here begins the catalogue of what one carries. And of what, in the end, carries you.

### **Relics**

As has been said, relics are not tools. They are fossilised echoes of symbolic time. In Numinia, each Relic is an encapsulated form of decision, a fragment of the past that still pulses, seeking to be reactivated in the hands of someone who understands its weight. They are not used: they are invoked. They are not activated: they are interpreted.

On touching them, one sometimes beholds a scene one does not remember having lived.

The properties of a Relic are always articulated with a Competence (Chronomancy, Neural Vision), which defines the nature of its action. The points in that Competence act as the dice pool for making the roll.

This implies that the Relic does not replace the character, but binds itself to their mastery, amplifying or channelling it. Precisely because of the strength of that bond, it is not possible to carry more than three Relics at the same time. It makes no difference how many Relics a PC has in their possession, but they may only carry three of them simultaneously.

Furthermore, every Relic has a Risk Factor, which indicates the consequences that follow from obtaining a Critical Failure roll when using its property. This Risk Factor thus indicates the character of those consequences, pointing to a specific situation depending on the property.

Note that some Relics are associated with a particular Position, because they are understood to require a specific, learned dexterity. This does not mean that a PC without the associated Position cannot use it, but they will possibly do so with a negative modifier, which is left to the discretion of the GD.

### **Weapons**

_“It was not his. He had not forged it. But, as he gripped it, something in the world shifted position.”_

In Numinia, a weapon is not a mere tool for dealing damage. The entities that take part in a confrontation are forms sustained by bonds, patterns, behaviours and manifestations that the characters must interpret and intervene. For this reason, weapons have no attack or damage values: they are instruments that widen the possibilities of intervention of whoever wields them.

To carry a weapon is to declare a way of acting upon the conflict. A blade can cut that which does not normally admit an edge; a chain can establish a hold between two forms; a projectile can reach that which does not allow itself to be approached; a hammer can impose stability upon a shifting structure.

The weapon does not determine what the character can do. It determines what additional possibilities are offered by the tool they have chosen to carry with them.

**Weapons and anchors**

Weapons can make it easier to intervene certain anchors, but they are neither specialisations nor requirements for intervening them. This distinction is fundamental.

A character can act upon any anchor for which they find an intervention coherent with the fiction, whether or not they have a weapon. If a creature is bound to a structure, for example, the characters might try to destroy it, move it, deactivate it, modify its meaning, trick the creature into moving away from it or find any other solution justified by what is happening in the scene. The system admits different interpretations as long as they rest on what has been described and do not contradict the fiction. A weapon provides one more possibility.

It can make a particular intervention especially viable, allow it to be carried out in a way that would be difficult without it or modify the conditions under which it can be performed. It never turns that intervention into the only possible solution.

Therefore, weapons do not grant additional dice by themselves. The pool continues to be built as usual: 1D6 base and +1D6 for each intervened anchor. If the use of a weapon makes it possible to intervene an anchor coherently, that anchor will contribute its die like any other intervened anchor. The die comes from the anchor, not from the weapon.

In other words: weapons do not increase the capacity to win a confrontation; they widen the ways in which a character can intervene in it.

**Intervention**

Each weapon has an Intervention, which expresses what it allows its wielder to do particularly effectively within the fiction.

An Intervention may allow one to reach a distant manifestation, hold a form that is hard to contain, act upon etheric matter, pierce a certain protection, modify a position or carry out any other operation coherent with the nature of the weapon.

The Intervention does not work as an automatic bonus, nor does it restrict the actions of those who do not possess the weapon. A character without it can intervene the same anchor using another strategy if the fiction allows it.

For example, a weapon capable of affecting etheric forms might make it possible to cut directly the bond that sustains a creature. That does not mean that this bond can only be intervened with that weapon: another character might try to close its origin, alter the ritual that maintains it, move whatever it is attached to or find any other valid solution. The weapon offers a path; it does not close off the others.

**Effect**

In addition to its Intervention, each weapon has its own Effect, which expresses the particular mark it leaves upon whatever it intervenes.

When the character uses the weapon as part of their action and the Confrontation Roll obtains a Success, the declared objective is achieved and the weapon's Effect is also applied, provided it is coherent with the action performed.

Nor does the Effect replace the declared objective. It accompanies it. If the character uses a weapon whose property allows them to temporarily fix in place whatever it acts upon, and declares as their objective to drive a creature away from a certain place, the Success achieves that objective and, when the fiction allows it, the weapon's property also introduces its fixing effect.

Total Success keeps its usual function: it represents an exceptionally favourable result and may provide additional benefits at the GD's discretion. The ordinary effect of the weapon, however, forms part of any Success.

**Weapons as an aid**

Weapons must never be designed as predetermined solutions for specific creatures or anchors.

There are no «necessary weapons» for defeating a type of contender, nor closed correspondences between a weapon and an anchor. A creature vulnerable to certain conditions may be affected by a weapon that exploits them, but also by any other intervention the players are able to justify.

This is especially important because anchors are not answers the players must guess, but elements of the fiction that may admit several interpretations and interventions. The weapon must encourage creativity, not replace it.

For this reason, a good Intervention is usually expressed through a broad capacity, and not through a closed condition such as «+1D6 against creatures bound to the Akasha».

The first opens up possibilities. The second manufactures a specialisation.

**The agitating power**

Weapons also possess a characteristic that sets them apart from the other objects in the Inventory: their agitating power.

A weapon does not remain passive when it is carried. Its nature imprints a certain tension upon the Veil and upon the presence of whoever bears it. The more weapons remained simultaneously bound to the same character, the greater the interference between their different impulses of intervention would be.

For this reason, a PC can only carry one weapon with them.

This does not mean they can only own one. The character can keep other weapons in their Reserve and replace the one they carry when they have access to it and circumstances allow the change to be made. The limitation applies exclusively to the weapon they keep with them during their journeys and adventures.

The choice, therefore, matters, but it does not turn the character into a specialist in that weapon. Choosing one does not mean renouncing certain kinds of anchors or being left helpless before certain creatures. It means deciding which additional possibility of intervention they wish to carry with them. The Reserve keeps the other possibilities for another moment.

**Structure of weapons**

Each weapon is defined by three elements:

  - Description: nature, appearance, provenance and meaning of the weapon.

  - Intervention: the particular possibility it offers the character to act upon the fiction and the anchors.

  - Effect: the characteristic consequence that accompanies the declared objective when the weapon takes part in a confrontation roll resolved with a Success.

Thus, two weapons are no longer distinguished because one deals more damage than another. They are distinguished because they allow the confrontation to be thought of in different ways.

A sword, a chain and a projectile weapon can all serve to overcome the same contender. None necessarily has a mathematical advantage over the others. What changes is the repertoire of actions each one places within the player's reach.

And this last sentence, it seems to me, could close the section very well:

In Numinia, choosing a weapon does not mean choosing how to deal damage. It means choosing what kind of possibility you want to hold in your hand.

### **Tokens**

Numinia does not remain indifferent to those who inhabit it.

As a character explores the city, discovers its secrets, interprets its symbols or establishes bonds with the forces at work within it, their relationship with Numinia changes. But that relationship is not confined to the character's memory or to the consequences of their decisions. In certain circumstances, the city itself recognises it and grants it a form.

These manifestations are called Tokens.

Tokens are primotechnic objects that materialise aspects of the relationship between an individual and Numinia. They can be kept, accumulated, exchanged and used within certain progression systems. A Token has value precisely because something has happened beforehand that made its appearance possible. They represent a bond, and may represent wealth as well: what a Token is worth never erases where it came from.

For this reason, obtaining a Token is always a form of recognition. Numinia responds to something the character has understood, investigated, interpreted or chosen, and transforms that intangible relationship into an object that can be added to the Inventory. Progression thus ceases to be exclusively numerical: what the character learns and that with which they establish affinity acquire a material presence within the world itself.

There are two great classes of Tokens: the Seeds of Knowledge and the Prism Cells. Both reflect the character's relationship with Numinia, although they do so from different perspectives.

The Seeds respond to knowledge: they express how much the character has discovered, understood or contributed to the city's imaginary.

The Cells respond to affinity: they express with which currents of thought and factions the character has established a significant resonance.

In a certain sense, the former speak of what Numinia has allowed the character to understand; the latter, of the way in which the character has decided to understand Numinia.

The way in which these Tokens manifest in Numinia is connected with the energy of the Veil when, intervening in the spectrum of the Threshold, it seems to take on the form of magic: while the Seeds of Knowledge appear spontaneously in the PC's path when they acquire knowledge or learn something new that proves relevant, the Prism Cells are found like treasures in lonely, mysterious, hidden or abandoned places. It will therefore be the GD who decides the moment at which these Tokens are offered to the PCs over the course of the game.

**SEEDS OF KNOWLEDGE**

The Seeds of Knowledge are physical manifestations of the bond established between a character and Numinia through discovery, investigation and creation.

They appear when the character's relationship with the world is significantly enriched: on discovering an element of its history, understanding a custom or institution, correctly interpreting a symbol, investigating an event of the past, or producing a coherent idea, text or interpretation that expands the Numinian imaginary.

They are not, therefore, automatic rewards for completing adventures. The GD grants them when they consider that a true act of knowledge has taken place. Nevertheless, the player may convert them into Prestige Points.

This also includes the player's attitude. Consulting documents, asking questions about the world, investigating apparently secondary elements, establishing relationships between scattered pieces of information or developing contributions compatible with Numinia are all legitimate ways of cultivating a Seed.

The principle is simple: Numinia rewards curiosity exercised, not information passively received.

**Morphology of the Seeds**

The Seeds look like large geodesic pods ending in a point. Their surfaces seem to be made up of small mineral facets, and from within them emanate luminescences whose intensity and colour depend on the relevance of the knowledge they contain.

Not all understandings carry the same weight. For this reason there are five classes of Seeds:

|**Seed**|**Value**|
|---|---|
|Green|1 Prestige point|
|Blue|2 Prestige points|
|Silver|3 Prestige points|
|Golden|4 Prestige points|
|Red|5 Prestige points|

The GD determines which Seed corresponds to each discovery according to its depth, difficulty and importance.

Understanding a minor cultural document might generate a Green Seed; reconstructing a complex tradition or discovering relevant information about an ancient order might manifest as a Blue or Silver Seed; an exceptional discovery about Numinia or a contribution that significantly expands its imaginary might produce a Golden or Red Seed.

The colour does not measure only how much the character knows. It expresses how much their relationship with Numinia has changed as a consequence of knowing it.

**Seeds and Prestige**

Each Seed obtained increases the character's Prestige according to its value.

However, Seeds and Prestige are not exactly the same thing. Prestige represents the cultural baggage accumulated by the character throughout their whole trajectory: everything they have come to understand, discover and contribute to Numinia. For this reason, once acquired, Prestige is never lost.

The Seeds represent something different: they are the part of that knowledge that still retains a potential for transformation.

A character can consume them to turn what they have learned into personal growth. When they do so, the Seeds disappear, but the Prestige they produced remains: the knowledge may have been assimilated and transformed into ability, but having acquired it remains part of the character's history.

**Using the Seeds**

The Seeds of Knowledge can be consumed to improve the character. To meet a cost, Seeds of different colours can be combined until the necessary value is reached. Below is the Prestige Table, which indicates the points needed to establish the different improvements on the PC Sheet.

|Improvement|Cost|
|---|---|
|Raise a Competence by one level|50 points|
|Raise a Special Aptitude by one level|100 points|
|Raise a Characteristic by one level|200 points|
|Learn a new Competence|500 points|
|Learn a new Special Aptitude|1000 points|

There are therefore two different quantities worth recording. Total Prestige gathers all the knowledge accumulated over the character's trajectory; the value of the available Seeds indicates how much of that knowledge still remains available to be transformed into new abilities.

**The role of the GD**

The Seed system turns the GD into something more than the person in charge of handing out rewards. Their function consists in sowing opportunities to get to know Numinia.

A forgotten inscription, an apparently trivial custom, a conversation with a Chronicler, a historical contradiction or a document found in an archive can acquire as much importance as an obstacle overcome during an adventure.

The GD must encourage curiosity, offer possibilities for investigation and recognise those occasions on which the players do not merely travel through Numinia, but try to understand it.

Because Numinia is not only the place where adventures happen. Getting to know it is also part of the adventure.

**PRISM CELLS**

If the Seeds of Knowledge materialise what a character has come to understand, the Prism Cells express something more intimate: that with which they have begun to resonate.

They are small, spherical, watery nodes in which the energy of the Prism condenses around a factional affinity. This means that there are four types of Prism Cells, each associated with one of the Factions of Numinia. They appear linked to challenges, rites, events and interpretive actions related to a particular faction, following the rhythms of the Cycle of the Prism. Likewise, each type of Cell has a special symbolism.

A Cell does not certify that the character has learned something about a faction. It records that, at some significant moment, their way of acting or of interpreting the world has coincided with it.

Collecting a Cell means that Numinia has recognised that resonance.

**Morphology of the Cells**

The Prism Cells have a circular shape and a luminous surface crossed by regular pulsations. At their centre appears the engraved emblem of the corresponding faction, turned into a small shining core around which the light seems to refract.

Each class displays the colour associated with its faction:

|Faction|Colour|
|---|---|
|Hermeticists|Pink|
|Heirs of Eleusis|Violet|
|Stellar Circle|Brown|
|Neo-Atlantists|Yellow|

These colours are deliberately different from those corresponding to the Guilds. A character's initial training and the affinities they develop over the course of their existence need not lead them down the same path.

Furthermore, it points to an essential fact: a PC can only use Cells that belong to their own Faction, whatever use they put them to.

**Nature and function**

The Prism Cells are fungible, accumulable Tokens. Those belonging to the same faction have no individual identity: what matters is not which specific Cell the character keeps, but how many manifestations of that affinity they have gathered.

Each Cell increases the degree of connection with the corresponding faction and serves as a measure of the character's recognition, experience and affinity within it. Their accumulation can provide progressive access to content, abilities, relationships or narrative possibilities linked to that factional sphere.

The Prism Cells are fungible, accumulable and consumable Tokens. Those belonging to the same faction have no individual identity: each one represents a unit of resonance that the character has established with that particular current of the Prism.

Unlike the Prestige associated with the Seeds of Knowledge, the Cells do not constitute a permanent record. They can be spent. When this happens, the symbolic energy they contain is released and the Cell disappears, transforming the accumulated affinity into an immediate effect.

In this way, the Cells introduce a constant decision between keeping resonance or using it. Accumulating them provides a larger reserve for future situations; consuming them allows one to intervene in present circumstances that might otherwise be much harder to alter.

A Prism Cell can be used in three ways.

<u>Removing States</u>

Cells can be used to remove States affecting the PC <sup>16</sup> .

By consuming the necessary Cells, the character releases the resonance contained within them to restore the balance altered by the State. The Cells used disappear and the corresponding State ceases to affect the character.

This function turns the Cells into a reserve for recovery, but not into a conventional form of healing. The character is not repairing an amount of damage: they are drawing on their bond with certain currents of Numinia to rid themselves of an alteration that has clung to them.

<u>Raising the degree of a Roll</u>

After learning the result of a Roll, the player may consume Prism Cells to raise its result by one or more degrees.

Each Cell consumed allows the result to rise one degree:

Critical Failure → Failure → Success → Total Success

Therefore, turning a Critical Failure into a Failure requires one Cell; turning it directly into a Success requires two; and transforming it into a Total Success demands three. In the same way, a Failure can be turned into a Success with one Cell or into a Total Success with two.

The Cells do not modify the dice obtained, nor do they require the Roll to be repeated. They act directly on its final degree, once the result is known.

This ability expresses their nature with particular clarity: the character consumes part of the resonance accumulated with the Prism to alter the immediate outcome of an action. It does not change what they tried to do; it changes the extent to which Numinia allows that intention to come to fruition.

<u>Currency on the Black Market</u>

There is a third use of the Cells, rather less orthodox and, as tends to happen with less orthodox things, someone has found a way to turn it into a business.

The Prism Cells can be used as currency on the Numinia Black Market, especially to acquire goods that do not circulate through ordinary channels.

Among them are the Residues, objects originating from past technologies that have been left outside the current systems of production and exchange; the Rarities, objects whose eccentric or anomalous properties have caused their circulation to be forbidden; and other goods, artefacts or services which, because of their origin, nature or restrictions, normally remain beyond the reach of the PCs.

Their clandestine value does not come solely from their scarcity. A Cell contains a tangible portion of resonance with the Prism and therefore possesses properties that certain merchants, collectors and clandestine operators consider far more interesting than any conventional monetary unit.

16 For more information on how to remove States with Prism Cells, see the section on PC States in Chapter 4, _Game System_ .

The price of whatever can be acquired with Cells will depend on its nature and availability, and will be determined by the GD.

**Spending affinity**

The three possibilities share the same principle: using a Cell means consuming it.

A Cell used to remove a State, modify the degree of a Roll or carry out an exchange disappears from the character's reserve. The affinity it represented has been converted into something else: recovery, opportunity or acquisition.

This profoundly distinguishes the Prism Cells from the Seeds of Knowledge. The knowledge acquired leaves a permanent mark in the form of Prestige, even if the Seeds are later used; the Cells, on the other hand, represent an available resonance, a relationship with the Prism that can be accumulated but also exhausted through use.

The player must therefore decide when it is worth consuming them. A Cell spent today to turn a Failure into a Success may be the one that tomorrow would have removed a State; three Cells used to achieve a Total Success might have served to acquire a hard-to-find Rarity.

Thus the Cells not only record a relationship with Numinia: they allow part of that accumulated relationship to be sacrificed in order to intervene in it.

There is a phrase from the original document that now acquires even more meaning: _«To collect it is not to take: it is to resonate»_ . I would add to it, as a complementary mechanical principle:

To collect a Cell is to accumulate resonance. To spend it is to turn that resonance into possibility.

**The Prism and affinity**

The Cells express one of the fundamental principles of Numinian knowledge: no single gaze, by itself, exhausts what it contemplates.

The same light, passing through a prism, produces different colours. In the same way, the Factions articulate different perspectives on Numinia, and each Cell records the character's approach towards one of them.

Thus, although a PC may accumulate Prism Cells from different Factions, they can only use those belonging to their own Faction, and this is what represents their relationship with Numinia. They are free to do whatever they wish with the Cells of other Factions: give them away, exchange them, sell them…

That is why their meaning does not lie solely in accumulating them. A collection of Cells also constitutes a kind of ideological cartography of the character: it allows one to observe which interpretations they have frequented, which they have reinforced, and what relationships they have established with the different ways of contemplating the city.

The Seeds and the Cells end up describing two complementary dimensions of a single trajectory. Prestige tells how much you have come to know Numinia; the Prism Cells show which Numinia you are looking at.

### **Rarities and Anomalies**

Not everything that deserves a place in the Inventory was created to become a Relic.

Numinia is built upon successive layers of knowledge, technology and experimentation. Some prospered; others were abandoned. There are machines that stopped being manufactured because better ones appeared, instruments belonging to technical systems that have since vanished, and prototypes whose workings never came to be incorporated into the ordinary life of the city. Many of them still exist.

These objects may be found in forgotten depots, institutional storehouses, old closed museums, disused reserves and buildings that kept part of their contents after losing their original function. In the periphery they also turn up among ruins, sealed chambers and abandoned settlements, where a patient labour of domestic archaeology can prove considerably more profitable than certain university studies, and considerably worse for one's clothes.

Others come from less innocent places: clandestine laboratories, cancelled research programmes, restricted facilities or experiments that never received authorisation to leave their test chambers.

All of them are grouped into two great categories: Residues and Proscribed.

Residues are old technologies that have fallen out of use. They belong to earlier stages of Numinia's technical development and preserve a characteristic materiality: precision mechanisms, valves, lenses, cylinders, needles, diaphragms, coils, pressure chambers and complex assemblies of metal, glass and minerals. They are not necessarily inferior to present-day technologies. Some performed extraordinarily well functions that later ceased to be considered necessary.

The Proscribed, by contrast, are experimental technologies. Some were prototypes; others come from secret or forbidden research; a few perhaps worked too well. Their ordinary circulation is restricted precisely because their properties prove eccentric, unpredictable or difficult to fit within the technological norms of the city.

Residues and Proscribed can be acquired in the Black Market <sup>17</sup> ‒especially when someone possesses enough Prism Cells to make commercial morality flexible‒, but they do not depend on it. They can be found during an adventure, recovered from a facility, discovered while exploring the periphery, received as payment or constitute in themselves the object of an investigation.

For the purposes of the Inventory, both count as Relics. A PC can carry a maximum of three objects among Relics, Residues and Proscribed, in any combination. The rest must remain in their Reserve.

Their difference is not mechanical, but historical and technological: a Relic has a singular and symbolic character; a Residue is the survival of a displaced technology; a Proscribed object is the vestige of a technology that perhaps should never have left the laboratory.

## **Fragment 3: The LAP**

The Personal Akashic Reader, better known by its acronym LAP, is the everyday device for accessing the akashic network of Numinia. Every citizen has one and usually wears it on the wrist, built into a small structure resembling a watch or bracelet.

The LAP is one of the fundamental interfaces between the individual and the city's informational infrastructure. Through it one can consult documentation, identify citizens, interpret nodes associated with places or objects, communicate with other users and access numerous basic utilities. In functional terms, it occupies in Numinian life a place similar to that of a personal device permanently connected to a great information network.

But the analogy has its limits. The LAP does not access the Akasha directly in all its depth, nor does it turn its user into the depositary of every existing piece of knowledge. It connects to the akashic network, that is, to the organised web of information that Numinia has recorded, structured and made available to its citizens. The city may be a great living archive, but not all of its documents are public, nor has everything that exists been correctly documented.

For this reason, the LAP is an extraordinary tool for consultation, but it does not replace investigation.

### **Morphology and interface**

The LAP usually takes the form of a light device fastened to the wrist. There are numerous aesthetic and cultural variations, but all of them share a similar functional architecture.

It can be activated by touching its surface or by a brief command. When this happens it deploys a visual interface on the device itself, in a small suspended projection or through layers of augmented reality integrated into the user's field of vision.

The interface responds to context. A documentary query may take the form of pages, diagrams or information windows; a map may be overlaid directly upon the streets; scanning a citizen emblem deploys an identification card; and certain cultural spaces allow the LAP to incorporate information about whatever the user is contemplating.

The device can be switched off or left in standby. In that state it stops actively exchanging information with the network.

### **Access to information**

The most common function of the LAP is to consult the official or authorised information available on the akashic network.

A character can use it to look up data on districts, institutions, documented historical events, public figures, species, Guilds, Factions, regulations, known technologies, cultural works, locations and any other content to which access is ordinary.

The GD should not demand a roll simply to consult public and easily locatable information.

If a PC uses their LAP to ask who runs an institution, where a well-known building is located or what the official function of a particular organisation is, the information can be provided directly, provided it forms part of the accessible records.

The roll comes in when the problem is not finding an entry, but investigating: locating a not very evident relationship between documents, distinguishing relevant information among numerous results, recovering a badly catalogued file or interpreting data whose importance is not immediately apparent.

This distinction is important for the development of adventures:

the LAP finds information; the PCs still have to understand it.

### **The LAP does not know everything**

The existence of the LAP should not become a reason for any unknown to be solvable through a search.

The network only contains what has been recorded and made available to its users.

The LAP may therefore run into several limitations:

  - Information that does not exist or was never documented

  - Information restricted by an institution

  - Deleted, damaged or incomplete files

  - Knowledge reserved for certain Guilds, Factions or access levels

  - Events too recent to have been recorded

  - Elements belonging to the periphery or to little-explored areas

  - Data whose very existence is unknown

  - Information deliberately hidden or falsified

When this happens, the LAP should show the limit clearly: no results, restricted access, incomplete record, source unavailable or any equivalent indication.

It is best not to use the mysterious «no results» every time, for it would end up seeming that Numinia's entire informational infrastructure had been designed by a particularly spiteful librarian.

**Reading citizen emblems**

Many citizens of Numinia wear an identifying emblem associated with their position within the social structure.

The LAP can scan these emblems and display the individual's basic authorised information.

It normally includes:

Name
Guild
Faction
Species
Position

This is why relevant NPCs may appear in the modules accompanied by a LAP Card.

The scan does not reveal thoughts, private history, full capabilities or confidential information. Nor does it replace interaction with the NPC. Knowing that someone is a Hermeticist Exegete and holds the Position of Archivist makes it possible to understand their place in Numinia; it does not automatically explain what they are plotting while they smile with suspicious serenity.

When an individual has no emblem, has it deactivated or has some system of concealment, the LAP will not provide that information.

**Reading sigil nodes**

Numerous objects, buildings, monuments and infrastructures have **sigil nodes** connected to the network.

The LAP can interpret them and display the associated contextual information: identification of the object, public function, recorded provenance, access rules, basic instructions, historical references or any other content that those responsible for it have linked to the node.

In a museum, for example, the user could look at a piece and immediately consult its official record. In an institution, a node can identify a department and explain its functions. In a piece of public infrastructure, it can display instructions for use or safety warnings.

Once again, the node **provides recorded information about the thing,** not necessarily the whole truth about the thing.

A display case may claim that it contains an artefact from the Golden Age.

That does not prevent the PCs from discovering later that someone modified the record.

### **Augmented reality**

In numerous spaces of Numinia, the LAP can overlay information upon the surroundings.

Museums and monuments can display reconstructions, texts, dates or images; institutions can indicate departments and services; public thoroughfares can deploy routes and names; some cultural spaces incorporate specific layers of information for visitors.

This function makes Numinia's very geography navigable without forcing citizens to memorise every street, building and statue, a civilising virtue that the Cartographers of the Wind tolerate with admirable resignation.

### **Akashic search engine**

The LAP includes a search engine covering the accessible information on the network.

Players may use it freely to make ordinary queries during an adventure.

The GD should respond following three simple categories:

Common information: it appears directly.

Specialised but accessible information: it may require time, a specific search or, when the situation justifies it, an appropriate Roll.

Restricted, non-existent or secret information: it does not appear through an ordinary query.

This distinction makes it possible to keep the LAP as a genuinely useful tool without turning it into the natural adversary of every mystery.

### **LAP and Rumourology**

The akashic network does not contain only institutional documentation.

There are also forums, discussion groups, community channels, personal publications and other informal spaces where citizens exchange theories, experiences and accounts that lack official recognition.

In certain circumstances, these spaces can be used to investigate the Rumourology of an element.

This does not mean that all Rumourology can be found through the LAP.

Some rumours belong exclusively to a particular community, to an oral tradition, to a neighbourhood, to a profession or to groups that maintain no public activity on the network. Others may have been erased, may be found in restricted channels or may never have been put into writing at all.

For this reason, it is up to the GD to decide whether the element being investigated has enough presence in the forums and informal channels for its Rumourology to be traced through the LAP.

When it is possible, the player must make the corresponding **Inquire** Roll, just as if they were investigating the rumour through any other source.

The difference will lie in the kind of material recovered.

A forum may preserve:

  - Anonymous testimonies

  - Contradictory experiences

  - Captures or records of uncertain provenance

  - Speculative theories

  - Old debates

  - Publications subsequently deleted

  - Distorted versions of a known event

A Success does not turn those publications into established facts. It allows the rumour to be better classified, in accordance with the rules of Rumourology.

The LAP does not transform rumour into document.

It only makes it possible to find where the city is talking.

### **Dynamic cartography**

The device incorporates up-to-date cartography of the districts and known locations of Numinia.

It can calculate routes, indicate public access points and represent the basic geophysical information available on the network.

This function depends, naturally, on the quality of the existing cartography. Little-explored peripheral areas, spaces affected by alterations of the Veil and certain restricted locations may appear incomplete, displaced or simply absent.

The LAP can tell you where the map ends.

It does not guarantee that the path ends there.

### **Communication**

The LAP makes it possible to send and receive personal communications to and from other registered citizens.

It has two usual systems:

Messaging, for brief and immediate exchanges.

Akashic mail, intended for formal communications, lengthy documents and messages that require preservation or registration.

These communications may depend on the state of the network, on the restrictions applied to the user and on the conditions of the place where they happen to be. Not even within the four districts is communication infallible.

### **Interfactional channel**

The members of a Faction may have access to an interfactional channel, an encrypted communication network intended for the internal exchange of authorised information among the members of the same Faction.

The content and privileges of these channels depend on the citizen's affiliation and access level. Belonging to a Faction does not mean being able to consult all of its archives or to attend, even digitally, all of its conversations.

The channel facilitates belonging.

It does not grant corporate omniscience, a small mercy without which no institution would survive a week.

**Basic programs**

Every LAP incorporates various utilities in everyday use: clock, timer, calculator, compass, note management, data logging and other equivalent tools.

These applications do not usually require any mechanics unless an extraordinary situation affects them.

### **Dependence on the akashic network**

The main technical limitation of the LAP, as has already been said, is its dependence on the akashic network.

In places where the connection is weak, suffers interference or has been deliberately blocked, some or all of its functions may be restricted; this is more usual in the peripheral areas of Numinia, though not in all of them.

This may happen in certain peripheral regions, protected facilities, spaces affected by anomalies of the Veil or areas where communication systems have suffered damage.

The GD should treat this limitation as a circumstance of the fiction, not as an arbitrary device to stop the players from consulting something inconvenient.

If the LAP stops working, there must be a reason within the world.

**The LAP within the adventure**

The LAP is part of every citizen's ordinary equipment and does not take up a Relic, Residue or Proscribed slot in the Inventory. Nor is it a weapon, nor does it need to be chosen as part of the starting equipment.

It should be assumed that every PC owns one unless the fiction expressly establishes otherwise.

For the GD, its existence has an especially important consequence: Numinia's public information should not be artificially turned into an obstacle.

If the PCs want to know what any citizen could reasonably look up in a few seconds, let them do so.

The true spaces of investigation begin where official information ends: in the incomplete, the contradictory, the hidden, the unrecorded and whatever the city does not yet know how to interpret.

In this sense, the LAP does not reduce the possibilities for adventure.

It delimits them.

It lets the players know quickly what Numinia knows, so that they can devote their curiosity to something rather more interesting: what it does not know yet.
