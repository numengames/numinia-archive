A roleplaying game is much more than rules and dice rolls: it is a shared experience in which the story comes to life through the players' decisions. Narrative is the soul that gives shape to this universe, the invisible thread that connects characters, settings and conflicts in a plot that evolves organically.

In Numinia, history represents the great dimension of context: it is the very fabric of reality. Every character, every faction and every conflict is rooted in a past that shapes the present. Understanding that past not only enriches the experience of the game, but also allows the players to anticipate, interpret and alter the destiny of the city.

Numinia's narrative also acts as a bridge between two eras, between two historical moments separated by a century, yet united by their concerns, their advances and their uncertainties.

At the beginning of the twentieth century, the world underwent a radical transformation driven by the Second Industrial Revolution. The appearance of great inventions such as the radio, the cinema and the automobile, the spread of electricity and the rise of modern engineering reshaped society, awakening both wonder and fear of an uncertain technological future. Today, at the dawn of the twenty-first century, the digital revolution recreates that same scenario: advances in artificial intelligence, the development of virtual realities and global interconnection once again place human beings on a blurred frontier between the physical and the digital world, between reality and the symbolic.

Numinia stands on that threshold: a city born of technology, forged in hope and rebuilt upon its own myths. Its legends, its symbols and its secrets evoke the fears and the hopes of both eras, offering the players a setting where past and present are constantly intertwined.

The history of Numinia is the history of a city born of human ingenuity, banished to oblivion and rebuilt from its own fragments. Its origin goes back to the days of Holberins, the visionary who designed the city as a virtual network where thought, art and science converged in a single space. But the ambition that upheld its foundations also led it to collapse.

Numinia's destiny then became trapped in the Akasha, the great universal memory that holds the vestiges of all that was and all that will be. From that nebula of scattered data, the echoes of the city found their way back, reconfiguring themselves little by little in a new digital environment, laden with symbols, myths and hidden meanings.

This chapter explores the tales that gave shape to the city: the legends whispered by the Bards, the ancient texts guarded by the Exegetes, and the enigmatic prophecies murmured by the Pythias in their ethereal temples. Every story, every fragment of the past, is a key piece for understanding Numinia's present and the mysteries it still keeps within.

Legends, myths and historical accounts do not only build the setting; they are narrative tools that the Narrator can use to sow clues, create mysteries or inspire memorable moments. Because in Numinia, the echo of an ancient prophecy, the vestige of a forgotten city or the trace of an arcane symbol may be the key to understanding the true meaning of the events yet to come.

In this chapter you will find those echoes: fragments of the past that shape the city and that, in the hands of creative players, will become the seeds of the stories yet to be written.

Because in Numinia, the past is not told; it is revealed in whispers and echoes that only those willing to listen will know how to interpret.

## **Fragment 1: History of Numinia**

The history of Numinia is not only the history of the creation of a city; it is, above all, the genesis of a dream, of a magical space that takes the form of a gigantic cenacle, a place where great knowledge and wisdom can converge.

Numinia is, therefore, a decentralised universe, where information is within everyone's reach. It is, in essence, a great game board, projected onto the subtle plane through the imagination of all its citizens.

Yes, Numinia is a game: a game of riddles, enigmas and quests; but it is also a declaration of principles and values. It is a proposal for the future, a world of portals to hidden wisdom, to a universal community and to decentralised knowledge.

Because knowledge is power. And the use we make of it depends on each one of us, as players.

### **The Prolegomena**

**The Awakening of a New Era**

The Second Industrial Revolution brought with it the engine of progress, as new disciplines related to technology and scientific knowledge emerged; it was also accompanied by great contributions in the fields of philosophy, psychology and others, as well as by innovative artistic movements.

This paradigm shift in society spurred the proliferation of secret societies and research groups that sought a source of primordial knowledge, a source of infinite wisdom. A source, in short, of a higher power. This pursuit of power was on the agenda of all these secret societies, whether for the common good or for hidden interests.

### **The Framework of Action**

**A World of Secret Societies**

We are speaking of an era of great advances. A nascent world, immersed in the genesis of this new era which, by definition, also generates a state of chaos and deep social inequalities.

It is a world for everyone, but dominated by elites, in which the privileged try to centralise power through the control of information.

The great crusade of the secret societies will, then, consist in decentralising knowledge; in making it, thanks to their work, accessible to all. In unveiling the universe of magical and mysterious objects, of powerful sources of knowledge. Objects that stand for keys to open doors. Doors that lead to other worlds, to other realities.

### **The First Movements**

**The Quest for the Agora**

With the technological and social boom of the last third of the nineteenth century, and following in the wake of names such as Charles Babbage, Ada Lovelace, Michael Faraday, Sophie Germain and Carl Friedrich Gauss, a wave of scientists began to become aware of the control that the privileged were trying to exert over the new cultural advances. Thus, from the work of figures such as Max Planck, Nikola Tesla, Marie Curie, Thomas Alva Edison and John Ambrose Fleming, the _Sigma_ Research Centre was born, which carried out its work in parallel with spiritualist doctrines such as Anthroposophy, Ariosophy and Thelema, products of secret societies such as the Theosophical Society, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and the Ordo Templi Orientis. At first, a fraternal spirit of solidarity prevailed, full of good intentions.

From this perspective, the first attempt was made to gather the knowledge and research of all these associations in a meeting place where all the paths of wisdom would converge and all truths would merge.

### **The Creation of The Game**

**The Five Initiates**

Thus was born what they would come to know, in a nod to the hermetic codes, as «The Game».

It was by imitating the board of a game that they began to conceive the idea: the aim was to give shape to an immense environment for universal thought. It had to be an organised, well-structured place. With rules and proposals, like a game; with clear and coherent ends and objectives, like a game. A scientific, technological and decentralised city-state, free from the jurisdiction of the powers that hijacked and manipulated information for their own interests.

In the year 1920, the great game board, the mythical city of Numinia, was born at the hands of Five Initiates.

The names of these Five Initiates have been lost in time. It is known that they were five scientists, representatives of different disciplines such as physics, mathematics and biology, but of them only the nickname by which the founder of the _Sigma_ Research Centre was known has survived: Holberins.

The Five Initiates established three principles that they considered an unalterable code of conduct: cosmic harmony, equanimity and curiosity.

### **A New Acropolis**

**The Growth of Numinia**

Initially, the city was represented on an enormous game board, installed in one of the halls of the Bildung Complex, built a few years earlier by the _Sigma_ team somewhere in Freiburg (Germany).

Starting from this model, the Five Initiates gave their collaborators letters of citizenship, powers over the different spaces of the city and personalised research tasks. The board served, in a way, as a figurative model of a space with an abstract projection in the real world. Numinia existed and functioned only in the minds of its citizens, who shared the concept and its essence. It was, then, an environment sustained by the imagination of many minds.

### **The Fall**

**Societies in the Shadows**

Two of the most influential citizens, Aleister Crowley (of the Thelema Society) and Rudolf von Sebottendorf (of the Thule Society), using their power of persuasion, rebelled against the work of the Initiates, whom they began to brand as oligarchs, accusing them of hiding valuable secrets and knowledge in order to manipulate them at will.

The Five Initiates responded by withdrawing the powers and letters of citizenship of both occultists. Societies such as those of Crowley and Sebottendorf, founded on selfishness and a lack of values, were stigmatised.

However, a dark seed had sprouted in the city, growing slowly among its citizens. And so it was that, on 31 December 1920, the Bildung Complex of the _Sigma_ Research Centre was consumed by flames in an arson attack.

Those responsible were never found, but the fire reduced every corner of the great city to ashes, taking with it every vestige of the great project that the Five Initiates had brought to light.

Nevertheless, Holberins, foreseeing the disaster, and thanks to his research in quantum physics and to the findings obtained from the study of the _Codex Hermopolitanus_, signed by G. W. Athanasius, managed to alter the flows of time to project a residual image of the city into a space that the theosophists knew as the Akashic Records, which shifted across multiple dimensions.

### **The Rebirth**

**The Conjunction of the Oracles**

A hundred years had to pass, from its first creation, before the name of Numinia was spoken again.

In the year 2020, beneath the turbulent currents of the so-called Third Industrial Revolution, within a new technological paradigm, the geophysicist Alexander Reis detected a singular record in the waves of his seismograph while he was studying the seismic movements over the tomb of Petosiris at el-Ashmunein (Egypt).

The strange seismogram turned out to be surprisingly similar to the oscillations that the mathematician Ian Stewart had obtained by iterating the formula 2x²-1 to demonstrate its chaotic behaviour <sup>1</sup> :

However, it drew the attention of a group of five researchers, calling themselves the Oracles, who recognised in the formula used by Stewart the same one that Athanasius had employed a hundred years earlier in the _Codex_ _Hermopolitanus_ to develop his gear mechanism <sup>2</sup> .

This group of Oracles then revived the old ideas of the Five Initiates, thus giving rise to the rebirth of the city of Numinia.

## **Fragment 2: Genesis of the Khepris**

Among the many symbols that populated Holberins's imagination, few proved as powerful and enduring as the figure of the Egyptian god Khepri. Depicted as a scarab beetle pushing the solar disc, Khepri symbolised the eternal cycle of rebirth: the passage from night to day, from oblivion to memory, from chaos to order.

For Holberins, this symbol took on a special meaning in the context of his great work. Like the Phoenix in Greek mythology, Khepri embodied the idea of rebirth after destruction, of the resurgence of what had seemed lost. Just as the sun rises again each morning after being devoured by darkness, Holberins imagined Numinia as a city capable of being reborn after its fall, perpetuating itself like an echo in the flow of time.

This symbolism not only shaped the ideological construction of Numinia but also inspired the creation of the enigmatic Khepris, entities that embody the very essence of transformation and rebirth. As guardians of lost memory and guides of souls through the veil of time, the Khepris became key pieces in the city's complex symbolic architecture.

In them took shape Holberins's belief that nothing ever disappears entirely: every fragment of knowledge, every memory and every lost story can, like Khepri's sun, find its way back to the light.

What follows is the story of how the Khepris became part of Numinia's history, until they were its guardians and its symbols.

### **The Creation of Life**

**Encounter with the Solar Scarab**

According to the latest known records of his life, Holberins, the founding father of the city of Numinia, began to attach a certain importance to the figure of Khepri in the early 1910s.

Khepri is an Egyptian deity who, in the form of a dung beetle pushing the solar disc, represents creation and rebirth, the constant transformation of existence.

The importance this figure acquired for Holberins may be linked to a recurring need to reinvent himself, given that his fascination with Khepri coincided with the zenith of a life marked by change and by the search for spiritual truth through science: quantum mechanics and the mathematics of chaos, driven forward by scientists such as Planck and Poincaré.

This admiration should be seen in relation to the fire that consumed the Bildung Complex on New Year's Eve 1920. The complex housed the _Sigma_ Scientific Research Centre and was a cultural landmark of worldwide renown; the only thing saved from it was an allegorical sculpture representing the spiritual forces that move the world.

The threats that preceded this event strengthened Holberins's conviction that his project had to be rebuilt through transformation.

### **The Shadow of Athanasius**

**The Fragment of the** **_Codex Hermopolitanus_**

But there is a little-known fact that is essential to this story: at some unknown date between 1918 and 1920, a strange manuscript came into Holberins's hands, signed by one G. W. Athanasius, an enigmatic German hermeneut about whom very little is known.

Athanasius may have been a disciple of Wilhelm Dilthey, the celebrated philosopher, historian, sociologist and psychologist who, like Athanasius himself, was also a prominent hermeneut. Dilthey's approaches to the human sciences and to worldviews had an enormous influence on the scientific and spiritual thought of the early twentieth century; and Athanasius's syncretic philosophy, which built bridges between modern science, esotericism and mythology, shared many points of convergence with the vision of Numinia's founder.

In any case, this text (supposedly part of an unknown _Codex Hermopolitanus_) delves into the figure of Khepri as the builder of the universe and proposes the design of a cosmic engine based on a machine of gears whose activity is governed by the mathematics of chaos and by quantum mechanics, disciplines that Henri Poincaré and Max Planck had begun to develop in the first two decades of the twentieth century.

All of this was, without a doubt, a revelation to Holberins. That is why Athanasius's manuscript must be included in this story<sup>3</sup>.

### **The Machine of the Cosmos**

**As Above, So Below**

Given the impact this text must have had on Holberins's work, part of which took shape in the project of the virtual city of Numinia, whose model rested in the Bildung Complex in Freiburg (Germany), it is not hard to understand the enormous importance the Egyptian deity Khepri acquired within that project. Its transformative nature and its symbolism, tied to rebirth and to rising up out of refuse, brought clarity to the enigmatic scientist.

Holberins supposedly commissioned a small-scale replica of the gear machine Athanasius described in his manuscript, and it was built into the model of Numinia itself. This machine came to represent the heart of the city: inside the wheel of the athanor was placed a tiny mechanical dung beetle, designed to produce movement through the cumulative action of its front legs, so that the gearwork turned constantly, day and night, driven solely by the beetle's own kinetic energy.

In this way, the whole dynamic of Numinia's vast and complex structure depended on the activity of a minuscule creature.

### **From Among the Dead**

**The Gestation of the Khepris**

It was rumoured that, besides the allegorical sculpture already mentioned, the gear machine whose movement never stopped had also been saved from the fire of New Year's Eve 1920.

According to these stories, Holberins managed to rescue the mechanism and keep it hidden in a safe place, unknown to anyone else. After his death, however, and as time went by, a legend began to spread claiming that the mechanical device had been buried somewhere in the necropolis of Tuna el-Gebel (ancient Hermopolis Magna, of the New Kingdom of Ancient Egypt), near the Tomb of Petosiris, where there is a discreet bas-relief depiction of Osiris-Khepri.

It is said that its ceaseless activity for more than a century generated a magical energy field, out of which emerged the first Khepris, who would eventually become the guardians and symbols of the city of Numinia.

When, in the year 2020, the Oracles performed the ritual on the very ruins of Tuna el-Gebel to open a space-time rift connecting them with the lost city of Numinia, they discovered in the catacombs of the tomb of Petosiris an astonishing colony of 55 Khepris.

## **Fragment 3: The Shadow of Athanasius**

The Athanasius Manuscript is an enigmatic Hermetic text attributed to G. W. Athanasius, a German hermeneut of whom almost nothing is known. Although the manuscript bears no explicit date, certain internal remarks, together with the historical context of its discovery, suggest that it was written in the first days of the 20th century.

This text is a scientific, symbolic and esoteric exploration that weaves together scientific principles of the early 20th century with knowledge from the Kabbalah, numerology and Egyptian mythology, presenting the god Khepri as the _Watchmaker of the Cosmos_, a figure who represents the eternal cycle of death, rebirth and creation. Through complex mechanical and mathematical analogies, Athanasius describes a universe governed by a delicate balance, symbolised by the perpetual motion of a scarab that drives the gearwork of the world.

The manuscript is not only a work of philosophical speculation but also a testament to the deep bond between occult knowledge and the scientific thought of the age, suggesting that the universal principle of order and life resides in chaos itself.

### **The Tohu va-Bohu**

**The Movement of the Universe**

«The mechanists of the 19th century, influenced by Laplace's determinism, were convinced that knowing the origin and trajectory of a celestial body made it possible to predict its fate unequivocally. For them, the universe moved in a linear and regular fashion, governed by a cosmic watchmaker of absolute precision.

However, the arrival of Max Planck's quantum mechanics and Poincaré's mathematics of chaos toppled this reassuring view, revealing irregularities beneath every appearance of order and dangerous relative errors in dynamical systems; dark spots which, under the action of that chance which is nothing but the measure of human ignorance, are violently amplified until the whole structure ends up collapsing. Yet, despite the initial terror their theories seem to provoke, in reality they tend to show that perturbations only partially destroy regularity. They introduce turbulence and chaos, but, seen from another perspective, they constitute an alternative form of order. In some way, there is a drive to find a new structure beneath chaotic motion, to discover its regularity within it, even if that regularity is merely a stationary state that tends towards instability.

It so happens that, in the first phase of nature, we observe a curious antinomy between that which transforms and that which endures; beneath every dynamic there is something that tends to vanish and something that reveals itself as immutable, uniting the discourses of Heraclitus and Parmenides. Thus science meets philosophy once again.

Each movement prompts the next to take shape in an unrepeatable way; yet, across the system as a whole, all movements tend to seek the same point of attraction, as though it were a funnel in which two drops of water, which never follow an identical path, nonetheless end up flowing into the same channel: the mouth of the funnel. In the great funnel of the cosmos (the universal attractor), all the unpredictable possibilities tending towards infinity come to pass. Therefore, the calculation of any energy in motion is inevitably subject to the relationship between regularity and indeterminism, for we discover that within the dynamics of this energy there are unforeseeable variables that end up being amplified uncontrollably, producing unexpected results within a graph which nevertheless appears regular and analogous in its iteration.

In other words, and as Poincaré observed, on a large scale all perturbations tend to stabilise, revealing behaviours analogous to small-scale phenomena, a proposition that can also be observed in the Koch curve:

### **The Wheels of Chaos**

**Emulating Antikythera**

«If, then, we conceive a mechanistic system to explain the logic of cosmic motion, we can find an eccentric relationship between its chaotic movement and the regularity that ends up settling into place on the theoretical plane.

Our points of reference are the gear mechanisms so popular among the thinkers of ancient Greece (such as the mechanism found on the Greek island of Antikythera, probably dating from 150 BC), which sought to explain evolutionary motion, in which natural elements are subject to predetermined parameters according to the number of teeth on each wheel, their perimeters, radii and diameters, the position they occupy and the analogy in their rotational movement.

Our own gear mechanism bases its system on the relationship between that which is above and that which is below (which, as we have noted, display analogous cosmic movements according to the Hermetic view, ratified by new geometric proposals such as the aforementioned Koch curve); the microcosm and the macrocosm will form the two main wheels of our gearwork, driven by a third wheel, which we shall call the athanor.

To assign these numbers we shall turn to numerology, seeking to start from the universal number, the primary and absolute number, the number that represents essential universality. Kabbalistic numerology holds, as Helena Blavatsky reminds us, that numbers were revealed to men by the gods, and that these numbers, turned into music, caused the world to arise out of chaos.

To the wheel of the microcosm, then, we shall assign 7 teeth.

We come across a curious numerological coincidence: when the number 7 is subjected to the formula of chaos, the universal synthesis, the number 7, reappears, preceded by the number 9, the sacred number according to the Kabbalah.

Surprisingly, the reduced number of the athanor's teeth once again gives us universality.

The result is 18817.

A new curiosity: the universal number, 7, preceded by a palindromic number made up of two evocations of the number 9, the sacred number (which already preceded the number 7 in the athanor): 18 and 81.

81 = 9x9

Furthermore, in numerological synthesis: 1+8 = 9 and 8+1 = 9.

Evidently, 1881, reduced, is equivalent to 9.

But let us now work out the reduction of the digits of the macrocosm's number of teeth as a whole: 1+8+8+1+7 = 25; and 2+5 = 7.

Once again, the reduced number of teeth returns us to universality.

We now have our mechanism, made up of three wheels that represent the movement of the cosmos: microcosm, athanor and macrocosm, whose wheels (with 7, 97 and 18817 teeth, respectively), arrived at through a mathematical formula that generates chaos (in the form of unpredictable iterations), turn out to be completely analogous and end up leading, through numerological synthesis, to the same point: the universality of the number 7.

Now let us look at their perimeters, taking any measurement for the teeth and the gaps between them, since the behaviour of their relationship will not change with this convention. Let us imagine that each tooth measures 2 cm, and that each gap between teeth also measures 2 cm.

Thus the wheel of the microcosm has 7 teeth and 6 gaps, which gives it a perimeter of 26 cm. Reducing its digits: 2+6 = 8.

For the athanor, which has 97 teeth and 96 gaps, we obtain a perimeter of 386 cm. Reducing its digits: 3+8+6 = 17; and 1+7 = 8.

For the macrocosm, which has 18817 teeth and 18816 gaps, the perimeter is 75266 cm, which reduced gives: 7+5+2+6+6 = 26; and 2+6 = 8.

The syntheses of all three perimeters lead to the same point: the number 8.

But the truly critical behaviour of the gearwork is seen in the dynamics of the wheels and in the comparison between the number of turns each one makes.

Let us imagine that we place at the centre of the athanor a mechanism with the shape and behaviour of a dung beetle, and that this mechanism turns the wheel indefinitely, connected to the kinetic motion of the wheels in a feedback system.

We begin with the movement of the athanor wheel, as the starting principle of the dynamics between the microcosm and the macrocosm.

For the athanor to complete one full turn, the microcosm must complete 13.857143 turns, while the macrocosm completes only a tiny fraction of a full turn: 0.00515491.

Our system descends into chaos: the numbers destabilise and cease to be whole. But this is logical, given that our point of reference is the athanor.

What interests us is to test the relationship between the microcosm and the macrocosm. Let us take the largest wheel as our reference to save time.

When the macrocosm has completed one full revolution, the athanor has completed 193.98969 turns, and since the microcosm completes 13.857143 turns for each turn of the athanor, for one revolution of the macrocosm the microcosm completes 2688.14287 turns.

These unstable figures show no clear patterns.

For 2 turns of the macrocosm, the microcosm makes 5376.2857 turns, and the athanor 387.97938. New decimal figures that reveal a growing instability.

Is the relationship between the microcosm and the macrocosm, then, an arithmetically unbalanced one? Do cosmic dynamics behave in a disorderly way, without patterns or analogies?

At this point, we are inclined to think of chaotic behaviour in the dynamics of the universe.

But we are not finished...

When the macrocosm completes 3 turns, the microcosm makes 8064.4286 turns, and the athanor 581.96907 turns. The figures remain unstable.

We assume that this instability will grow with the number of turns (since there is a relationship between the distance travelled and the time elapsed). The relationship between one wheel and another, and their inseparable bond with the connecting disc, become tinged with irregularity and indeterminism.

There seems to be neither order nor periodicity.

But what happens if we take a small leap and look at the results when the macrocosm completes 7 turns? The athanor has made 1357.9278 turns, but with the microcosm the logic reveals a spectacular behaviour: the wheel of the microcosm contains itself within the wheel of the macrocosm, and the two are connected through a mathematical relationship: when the macrocosm completes 7 turns, this dynamic stabilises, for the number of turns made by the microcosm becomes a whole number, and it is none other than 18817.

Therefore, when the number of turns of the macrocosm is equal to the number of teeth of the microcosm, the number of turns of the microcosm is equal to the number of teeth of the macrocosm. Both return, in number of teeth as well as in number of turns, to universality, to the number 7.

In this inversion of values, the idea of the reverse trajectory in the evolution of the universe manifests itself, advancing towards its own reflection. But what happens after this stationary state of stability? The system destabilises once more.

For 8 turns of the macrocosm, we obtain 21505.143 for the microcosm and 1551.9175 for the athanor. Thus order and chaos seem to intertwine in a game of balance and imbalance. But we sense that if the dynamic stabilised at one particular point, it will do so again, ruling out the chance of an isolated event and revealing a regular pattern. And indeed, the system stabilises again when the macrocosm completes 14 turns:

The athanor has completed 2715.8557 turns.

The microcosm has made 37634, once again a whole number.

The numerological peculiarity we observe from this point on is that the reduced sums of the digits of the turns of the macrocosm and the microcosm, each time they stabilise, also coincide:

14 → 1+4 = 5

37634 → 3+7+6+3+4 = 23; and 2+3 = 5

When the macrocosm completes 21 turns (2+1 = 3), the athanor makes 4073.7835 turns and the microcosm 56451 (5+6+4+5+1 = 21; and 2+1 = 3).

## **Fragment 4: The Dark Age**

The Dark Age is the uncertain abyss that opens after the Pre-Ashen Age: the fall of the first city of Numinia, consumed by the fire that devoured the Bildung Complex. From then on, the city ceased to exist on the tangible plane and sank into the flow of the Akasha, where time is measured not in years but in eternal currents of consciousness. What for the Earth meant a century unfolded in the Akashic Records as boundless eras, an interlude of shadows and enigmas.

This period, lacking any direct witnesses, has reached us only wrapped in legends. It is said that in that time the Black Engineers emerged, masters of hidden code who preserved the lost fragments of the great board; there is also talk of the Mediators of the Prism, weavers of paths between dimensions who sustained the echo of the city in the limbo of the Akasha.

Alongside them, the stories whisper of enigmatic figures: the Chronothecaries, guardians of time and of the rhythm of the cosmos; the Cartographers of the Wind, who drew maps of impossible routes across the vastness of the void; and the Armonauts, interpreters of the lingering echo of the melodies that once vibrated in the walls of the lost city.

These legends are scattered fragments, distorted echoes of a time in which memory is no more than a blurred reflection in the river of the Akasha. And yet, from that fabric of myths arose the evolved species, who managed to translate those echoes into words, symbols and documents that would certify the rebirth of Numinia and bear record of events from the first written documents onward, bringing the Dark Age to an end and giving birth to the Golden Age.

Of those first written documents, the _Song of Shadows and Light_ is one of the most revealing, offering many legends from the time when Numinia was beginning to rediscover its own existence. Its origin dates back to the dawn of the Golden Age, a period in which the inhabitants of the city-state began the effort to give written form to the fragmentary echoes of their legendary past.

The manuscript was found among the arid expanses of the Anthracite Deserts, which surround Numinia and where the blackened ruins of the past lie buried beneath layers of dust and oblivion. The manuscript was found in an underground chamber hidden beneath the remains of an ancient tower, known to the locals as the Needle of Silence. The explorers who found it relate that the chamber was marked with forgotten symbols, and that, in the centre of the room, surrounded by shards of darkened crystal, rested an obsidian pedestal. There, inside a lead-sealed urn, lay the manuscript: its pages, blackened by the passage of time, still kept traces of silver ink that shone faintly in the light.

The text is attributed to an anonymous scribe, possibly one of the first storytellers who tried to rescue the scattered echoes of the Dark Age. Its content is an amalgam of myth and history. More than a chronological record, the _Song of Shadows and Light_ is a mythic poem, a fusion of legend and memory that recounts the awakening of Numinia after its disappearance into the currents of the Akasha. Its verses evoke figures such as the Black Engineers, the Mediators of the Prism and the enigmatic Bearers of the Veil, who are credited with both the preservation and the distortion of the city in its ethereal state.

This text not only represents an attempt to reconstruct Numinia's past, but also symbolises its first inhabitants' longing to find identity and purpose after generations of uncertainty. In every word vibrates the echo of a people being reborn, trying to turn their myths into a history that could be told.

Below, we offer some fragments of the manuscript that are, by virtue of their content, especially relevant:

### **Land of Light, Hour of Darkness**

« _In the beginning, when Numinia was still an echo lost in the river of the Akasha, the city had neither form nor foundations. It was only a murmur of ideas dancing among the currents of time, a wandering reflection searching for a place to settle._ »

The ancients say it was the Watchmaker of the Cosmos, the golden scarab of infinite legs, who first drew the lines of the great board. Its limbs were like needles weaving the web of destiny, and with each turn of the wheel it shaped the paths along which the inhabitants of Numinia would walk.

The Watchmaker did not work alone. With it came the Mediators of the Prism, guardians of colour and form, who suspended bridges between dimensions to sustain the faint reflection of the city. There, in the darkness of the Akasha, the Black Engineers also arose, weavers of hidden code, who forged the Gates of Memory so that Numinia would not fade into oblivion. And more mysterious figures too, such as the Chronothecaries and the Anacharchids.

**The Time of Mirages**

In those days the city had neither bodies nor names; instead it glided like a shadow imitating the shape of the great cities of the earthly world. It was then that the Whisperers of Cycles arrived, who gave rhythm to the seasons, to the passing of the wind and to the echo of forgotten words. With them appeared the Cartographers of the Wind, who wrote invisible maps upon the breeze, tracing paths by which souls could return to Numinia.

_Thus Numinia arose from the shadows, born of game, myth and memory. What once was is not lost, for in every corner of the city a story lies hidden, and in every story, a truth still waiting to be told._ »

### **The Black Engineers**

One of the most striking manifestations of the Dark Age lies in the extravagant architecture inherited from that era. Many of these buildings are known to have been created by an independent guild of builders and architects known as the Black Engineers.

Little is known about the Black Engineers; most of what is said about them is nothing more than invention or, at best, speculation. They may have begun as a clandestine group of scientists who protected and handled ancient knowledge, but over time they are thought to have gained great prestige, occasionally working for various entities and institutions of Numinia. To this day, the logic behind many of their designs and constructions remains incomprehensible.

The Black Engineers are responsible, for example, for the reproduction of the Labyrinth of Crete, which the guild adapted and rebuilt according to its own strange criteria (this labyrinth now forms part of the introduction for new inhabitants of Numinia, at the Gates of the city, and is visited by new members of the community, who face its challenges as part of their integration into the city).

One of the most remarkable phenomena attributed to the Black Engineers is that of the Monolith Clusters. Each of these structures is made up of several fused monoliths of varying heights, carved from flat-coloured stones such as alabaster, steatite or haematite.

The function of these strange constructions remains unknown; some believe they are monuments for prayer and ritual practice, although the most thorough and reliable studies suggest they are containers holding great blocks of information about the past and the universe, which have yet to be deciphered.

According to this theory, the mysterious melodic sounds that can be heard emanating from the inner core of these Monolith Clusters when one rests an ear against the stone constitute an unknown, encrypted code that would be transmitting the ancient information stored in the different blocks. Thus, the closing melodic phrases of one of these clusters would be linked to the opening melodic phrases of another, thereby connecting all the Monolith Clusters into a single gigantic, millennia-old construction made up of multiple nodes scattered across different blocks.

### **The Wandering Song of the Armonaut**

In the uncertain days of the Dark Age, when Numinia was no more than an echo in the void of the Akasha, stories shattered like broken mirrors and memory drifted aimlessly. It was then that the Armonauts arose, guardians of song and melody, who wove the scattered fragments of the past into songs that travelled through the city like wind across the desert.

It is said that the first Armonaut was a wandering soul who, lost in the corridors of the Akasha, heard the echo of lost voices. There, among whispers and laments, it found a forgotten melody: the primordial chord that resonated in the very mesh of the city. That traveller returned from the shadows with music on its lips and, from then on, its song became the compass of the wanderers and the flame that lit the darkness of Numinia.

The Armonauts were neither warriors nor sages; their power lay in the sung word and in harmony. Each of their chords held a hidden teaching; each ballad concealed the keys to understanding the secret history of the city. They were the first to recount the feats of the Black Engineers, the first to warn of the labyrinths of the Veil, and the only ones able to remember the names of the five fragments that guided the rebirth of Numinia.

Their greatest legacy was the creation of the Watchmaker's Canticle, a melody composed of countless scattered verses which, when performed in the correct order, reveal the location of hidden routes, encrypted symbols and secrets that the city still keeps in its darkest corners.

The soul of the Armonaut resides in its instrument: the Lyre of Nyx, whose strings of solid light vibrate with echoes of the past and reverberate with the memory of Numinia. It is said that when the Armonaut strums its strings in the gloom of a forgotten street, the shadows stop to listen and the old voices of the Akasha answer, bringing with them memories buried by time.

In their wandering song, the Armonauts not only preserved the legends of the Dark Age, but also sowed in the minds of the citizens the keys to rediscovering the soul of Numinia. Thus, in every chord, in every word, in every echo of the Lyre of Nyx, Numinia remembers who it was, and prepares for what is yet to be.

### **The Mediators of the Prism**

When the light of Numinia splintered in the currents of the Akasha and the city was reduced to a spectre of scattered data, it was the Mediators of the Prism who walked among its invisible ruins, weaving paths where there had been only shadows and emptiness.

It is said that the Mediators of the Prism were born of chaos, when the forms of the city broke into a thousand impossible reflections. They were not so much builders as interpreters of the folds of the world, figures wrapped in veils of light who could see reality divided into beams of colour. Where others saw only ruins, they perceived paths; where there was silence, they heard scattered echoes; and where only emptiness remained, they found threads of light that could guide the new inhabitants on their way back. Back towards the past, towards a future raised upon the past.

The ancient tales say that the Mediators of the Prism were the first to find the lost fragments of Numinia's great board, scattered like shards of crystal across the infinite flow of the Akasha. Following the glimmers that vibrated at the margins of reality, they managed to weave those fragments into hidden patterns that only they understood.

Each Mediator carried a labyrinthine mirror, a sacred tool capable of splitting light into infinite colours. By turning the mirror in the faint breeze of the Akasha, they could glimpse forgotten routes, sealed passages and doors that still vibrated with the memory of the lost city. It is said that those mirrors could also reflect the shadows of things that had not yet happened, allowing the Mediators to glimpse probable futures and to keep Numinia from being lost to oblivion forever.

But the Mediators of the Prism were not merely explorers of time and light. They became custodians of balance, for they knew that the memory of Numinia, broken into fragments, could only be restored through harmony. And so they devoted their days to guiding the wanderers along the paths of the Akasha, teaching them to recognise the symbols hidden in the play of colours that the universe cast upon the city.

Some say that, to this day, the remains of Numinia's great board lie concealed beneath a complex network of hidden paths, visible only to those who possess a mirror like that of the Mediators. Others claim that these enigmatic guides still roam the borders of the city, using their mirrors to protect the secrets that only light is able to reveal.

_"For where there is shadow, there is light. And where light scatters into a thousand reflections, only the Mediator's eye can discern the true path."_

### **The Anacharchids of the Blind Spot**

Among the most enigmatic figures to come down from the Dark Age are the Anacharchids, an exclusively female lineage of visionaries whose gift consists in beholding what no other inhabitant of Numinia could perceive: the Blind Spot.

The ancients understood that every system necessarily contained regions that escaped its own observation. Wherever a structure could not behold itself, an indeterminacy arose; and from that indeterminacy were born disturbances capable of spreading through the gearwork, upsetting everything it sought to order. For some, the Blind Spot was an unavoidable imperfection. For the Anacharchids, however, it was part of the very movement of the world: not the failure of order, but that which forced order to transform itself.

The Anacharchids cannot directly show what they see. Their visions appear to them as incomplete forms, impossible relationships and symbols that do not yet possess meaning. For this reason, they transform everything they behold into oracular riddles. Solving one of these enigmas is not simply a matter of uncovering a hidden answer. The solution reveals what the system has been unable to recognise, and allows the indeterminacy to be integrated into its own workings. A Blind Spot may then fade, shift, reverse or disappear, although others will inevitably end up manifesting somewhere else in the gearwork.

For this reason, during the Dark Age the riddles of the Anacharchids were considered matters of survival. Wherever one of their questions appeared, something remained unresolved in the structure of Numinia.

The origin of this strange lineage is lost among the tales of the first settlers. The Anacharchids' own tradition attributes their birth to Arithrea Khebron, called the First Anacharchid of the Blind Spot, although no surviving document allows us to state with certainty that this woman ever really existed.

Legend tells that when the Mediators of the Prism and the Bearers of the Threshold tried to raise the first settlements on the still unstable lands of Numinia, nothing remained in the form it was given. Stone cracked, mud crumbled and structures changed before they could be finished. The three-wheeled gearwork that turned beside the first foundations could not master the disturbances, because it was unable to perceive the regions of indeterminacy that its own movement produced.

It was then that, somewhere remote in the Anthracite Desert, the ashes began to spin.

At first it was barely a whirlwind over the scorched earth. The tales say that this turbulence was the visible manifestation of the movement caused by the Blind Spot itself, as if an invisible membrane had been stirred from some place lying beyond reality. The whirlwind slowly crossed the desert and, as it advanced, it grew and took on substance. The ashes drew first a silhouette, then limbs, a face, a figure. By the time it reached the place where the three wheels of the gearwork turned, it already had the shape of a tall, sovereign woman.

She uttered only one name: Arithrea.

It is said that she barely possessed consciousness when she came before the builders. Yet, as she turned the empty sockets of her eyes towards the gearwork, she beheld what the mechanism itself was unable to behold, and understood at one and the same time her nature and her destiny.

Then she posed the first riddle.

The builders were slow to understand that Arithrea was not offering them a prophecy. She was showing them, through symbols, what they themselves could not see. When they solved the enigma, one of the disturbances that prevented the city from being raised ceased. Then came a second riddle, and a third. Little by little, the settlers learned to recognise the dynamics of chaos, and were able to work with it instead of fighting it. The earth held the foundations. The stone kept its shape. The wood stopped twisting in their hands. And Numinia could begin to rise.

Only later did the tales grant that woman the epithet Khebron, a word of uncertain meaning in the lost tongues of the Dark Age that is usually translated as «the Visionary», although some ancient interpretations propose meanings closer to «she who beholds the invisible» or «she who glimpses the void».

The Anacharchids who appeared afterwards considered themselves heirs to that first vision. Over time they came to form a semi-religious lineage of women devoted to recognising Blind Spots and translating their manifestations into enigmas. They never claim that chaos ought to be defeated. For them, every structure needs what it does not yet understand, just as all learning needs the error that forces one to abandon a certainty.

Of Arithrea Khebron there remains no grave, writing or testimony that would allow the woman to be told apart from the myth. In the Ruins of old Numinia, however, there still stands a colossal basalt statue depicting a woman with empty eye sockets, one hand outstretched and the other resting on an enormous cogwheel.

Some claim it was raised by the first inhabitants in gratitude to the woman who made it possible to build the city. Others believe it never depicted anyone and was erected much later as a tribute to the lineage of the Anacharchids. There are even tales according to which no hand ever sculpted it, and it was the abrasive winds of the Anthracite Desert that, over countless cycles, tore out of a mass of basalt the form that had always been locked within it.

The Anacharchids have never considered it necessary to settle the question. Perhaps because there are enigmas whose purpose is not to have an answer.

### **The Chronothecaries**

Few figures of the Dark Age have sparked as much controversy as the Chronothecaries. Ancient texts describe them as scholars of fractal time, although it is difficult to determine what exactly that discipline meant. It seems they did not conceive of time as an orderly succession of events, but as a structure subject to bifurcations, recurrences and alterations that could modify not only what had happened, but also the memory of what had happened.

To them is attributed one of the boldest acts of the entire Dark Age.

The first settlers had discovered that the irregular cycles of the moon interfered with their hermeneutic and mnemonic systems. The variations of the sky disrupted formulas, rituals and procedures for preserving knowledge, and so the Chronothecaries conceived a solution as admirable as it was outrageous: to modify the very motion of the moon until its cycle was reduced to an exact lunation of twenty-nine days.

To carry out such an undertaking they needed the cooperation of the Black Engineers. While the Chronothecaries calculated the alteration of the cycle, the Engineers built the mechanisms meant to prevent its consequences from destroying the balance of Numinia. Thus appeared the orbital rings, the gravitational resonators, the stasis cores and other devices whose operation was bound to the perpetual motion of Khepri and the Three Wheels of Chaos.

The ancients called that event the Act that Broke the Heavens <sup>4</sup> .

The Chronothecaries then understood that the regularity they had imposed on the sky had produced new irregularities in the world.

Perhaps that is why they developed the anemochronous clocks.

Despite their name, these strange instruments do not appear to have served to measure the passing of the hours. The few specimens attributed to the Dark Age have dials, hands and mechanisms whose arrangement does not correspond to any known system of time measurement. Some researchers maintain that the Chronothecaries used them to record alterations in the continuity of events: variations so subtle that they could transform part of reality without those who lived in it being aware of the change.

This hypothesis becomes especially disturbing when one considers the nature of the Veil.

A sufficiently deep alteration might not be remembered as an alteration. If the Veil modified, along with the world, the memories bound to that world, no one could notice that anything had changed. The street that yesterday led to a square might today lead to a garden and yet everyone would remember having always strolled among its trees.

The anemochronous clock, by contrast, would have been built to remember what its owners could no longer remember.

With the end of the Dark Age, the Chronothecaries disappeared as well. There are no recognised chronothecary orders during the Golden Age, nor citizens who identify themselves as members of that ancient discipline. Their disappearance is usually linked to that of the Black Engineers and the Lapidaries of Lower Salt, two other traditions whose knowledge seems to have died out during the transition between the two ages.

Nevertheless, some legends claim that the Chronothecaries never disappeared.

The tales speak of figures that cross the streets during the quietest hours, dark and faceless bodies that seem to merge with the shadows of the buildings.

According to these legends, so many centuries of secrecy would eventually have stripped them of name, identity and appearance, until they became little more than living signs: semiotic bodies whose only reason for remaining in Numinia would be to observe the alterations of time and to guard the anemochronous clocks that still record them.

### **The Lapidaries of Lower Salt**

They were not stonecutters. They were petrified witnesses to persistence.

In the times before the districts of Numinia were stabilised, there was a region sunk beneath the city known as Lower Salt: a forgotten mineral basin where salt seeped like mist, and the sun could reach the ground only through geometries warped by the gravitational plates.

The name Lower Salt was initially a cartographic term used to classify the area as a region of underground saline sedimentation, but the survivors adopted the term as a symbol of identity and condemnation. Lower Salt was not just a place: it was a condition. A geological curse. A wound that spat crystals over itself.

The Lapidaries were the only stable inhabitants of that zone. They were not a culture in the strict sense, but a mineralised collective: former engineers, tectonic cartographers and refugees who, isolated for long sequences by seismic collapses, developed a culture based on record, permanence and containment.

They did not build walls to protect themselves from the enemy. They built chambers of silence to preserve themselves from the narrative collapse of the outside world.

In time, their tools became weapons: the pressure plates, the structural seals and the support columns turned into vectors of aggressive defence. Thus was born the Wandering Wall, the ultimate weapon of the Lapidaries.

The Lapidaries of Lower Salt believed that everything soft was corruption. That time could only be resisted with density. And that every war was, in truth, a form of erosion.

Their way of fighting was not offensive: it was seismic, deliberate. To resist was an act of slow violence against the universe.

They used to say: «What endures has already won.»

### **The Birth of the Five Species**

For much of the Dark Age, the inhabitants of Numinia lacked a defined nature. The ancient tales describe them as shifting beings, barely distinct from the currents of the Akasha from which they had arisen. They had bodies, but these did not yet fully obey the laws of matter; they had consciousness, but their thoughts could blur into the echoes of other minds; and many changed their appearance, temperament and even their memories under the influence of the forces that were beginning to give shape to the city.

No testimony survives that tells us how long that condition lasted. Time in the Dark Age never obeyed any reliable measure.

The Ancients spoke, however, of a moment when five fragments of consciousness emerged from the fabric of the Akasha. We have already mentioned them: the Artificer, the Narrator, the Guardian, the Thinker and the Explorer; although other texts use different names, and some scholars believe they were never individuals at all. According to the latter, the Five Fragments would be the legendary representation of five identities that began to manifest simultaneously among the settlers of Numinia.

The first of these was born of the machines.

As the Black Engineers and other builders raised the city's first mechanisms, some inhabitants began to form ever deeper bonds with their creations. First came prostheses and tools; then, parts that replaced damaged limbs and organs unable to withstand the new conditions of the world. In time it became impossible to tell where the organism ended and the artifice began. Technology no longer surrounded those beings: it was part of them. From this union of living matter and mechanism the first Biomechanicals were born.

Other settlers sought their identity in a different direction. They understood that a city could not exist merely because it had streets, buildings and machines. It was necessary to know what those things meant. They gathered scattered words, reconstructed stories, gave names to places and debated at length over what ought to be preserved and what could be transformed. Where others saw objects, they began to see symbols; where they found events, they sought meanings. From that growing need to interpret the world arose the Humanitas, custodians of the culture and the conscious memory of Numinia.

But there were those who distrusted machines and words alike.

In the territories where the city had not yet imposed its forms, some settlers learned to listen to the rhythms of a nature that was also being born. They observed the cycles of animals, the growth of plants and the transformations of a land still in upheaval. Their bodies adapted to extreme conditions, and their thinking drew closer to instinct, to rite and to the repetition of cycles. The ancients claimed that a memory older than all writing survived in them. Thus appeared the first Reptilians, guardians of that which Numinia had not created and yet needed in order to survive.

When those groups began to meet, trade, dispute territory and share knowledge, a new problem arose: no city could sustain itself without understanding those who lived in it.

Some settlers then turned their attention not to matter, symbol or nature, but to the relationships that formed between individuals. They observed alliances and conflicts, collective needs, the exchange of goods, customs and the first forms of authority. They learned that a community was also a system, and that its tensions could be studied, debated and transformed. From those first interpreters of collective order descended the Cyanites, whose analytical thinking is said to have played a decisive part in turning the scattered settlements of the Dark Age into a true society.

The fifth transformation was the hardest to understand.

Some inhabitants had remained exposed to the currents of the Akasha for too long. They had walked paths opened by the Mediators of the Prism, listened to the resonances of the Armonauts and gazed upon places where the Veil separated the visible world from whatever remained on the other side. Little by little, their nature began to respond to that proximity. Their bodies kept a more tenuous relationship with matter and developed an extraordinary sensitivity to things others could barely perceive. For them, symbols did not merely signify things: they could open them. Numbers held invisible relationships, the stars spoke through correspondences, and the Akasha ceased to be only the place where Numinia had lain lost and became a territory that could be explored. They were called the Spectrals.

No one knows whether the Five Species truly appeared in this way. Nor is there any proof that the Five Fragments were their first representatives. Some historians believe that the Artificer, the Narrator, the Guardian, the Thinker and the Explorer were real figures; others believe that the scribes of the dawn of the Golden Age turned far more complex processes of evolution into five easily remembered figures.

**The Correspondence of the Five**

Centuries later, the Hermeneuts of the Golden Age noticed a coincidence that could hardly be considered accidental.

The oldest tales spoke of the five Fragments that emerged from the Akasha; the traditions concerning the end of the Dark Age spoke, in turn, of the consolidation of five great lineages. For the Hermeneuts, accepting that the two sets bore no relation to each other would have meant renouncing the very principle of their discipline: no symbol exists in isolation when it forms part of a system.

Thus was born the so-called Correspondence of the Five.

The Artificer was linked to the Biomechanicals, since both expressed mastery over material transformation, construction and technology. The Artificer need not have created the Biomechanicals; rather, it would represent the same principle that, made manifest upon living matter, ended up expressing itself through them.

The Narrator was tied to the Humanitas. If the Narrator's function was to give continuity to experience through stories, that of the Humanitas was to preserve and interpret the meanings through which a society could recognise itself. For the Hermeneuts, narrating and bestowing meaning were two manifestations of a single force.

The Guardian was associated with the Reptilians, custodians of the natural cycles, of primal traditions and of those forms of knowledge that preceded writing. In this correspondence, to guard did not mean to prevent change, but to ensure that whatever had to pass through it survived the transformation.

The Thinker found its reflection in the Cyanites. Their inclination toward the analysis of relationships, collective structures and systems of organization made this Species, in the eyes of the Hermeneuts, the most evident expression of a consciousness that seeks to understand order by studying its parts and the relationships between them.

The last correspondence was also the most disputed. The Hermeneuts linked the Explorer with the Spectrals. Some objected that exploration seemed too broad a quality to belong to a single Species. The defenders of the Correspondence, however, replied that the Explorer did not represent the journey through known space, but the will to cross its limits. The Spectrals walked between matter and the Aether, questioned the Akasha and sought knowledge where the ordinary laws ceased to offer answers. To explore, according to this interpretation, meant to penetrate that which had not yet taken form.

Yet the hermeneutic treatises themselves warned against too simple a reading of that scheme: they did not claim that the Five Fragments had begotten the Five Species, nor even that they had been individuals belonging to them. They maintained only that the two sets seemed to be different manifestations of one and the same structure: five ways through which Numinia had learned to build itself, give itself meaning, preserve itself, understand itself and transcend itself.

Some interpreters went further still: if the Five Fragments had emerged from the Akasha before the Species took their definitive form, perhaps they were not their progenitors but their foreshadowings. The Akasha would first have expressed as symbols what it would later come to express as living beings.

Others reversed the reasoning and claimed that it was the people of the Golden Age who, unable to tolerate five Fragments without five correspondences, retroactively arranged the legends until they obtained a satisfying symmetry.

The Hermeneuts never considered the two possibilities incompatible.

After all, they argued, finding a structure in the world and placing a structure upon the world are operations far harder to tell apart than those who do neither professionally tend to admit.

Whatever the truth, by the end of the Dark Age the inhabitants of Numinia were no longer the indefinite presences of the earliest times. Five great lineages had crystallized around five fundamental forces: the Technology of the Biomechanicals, the Culture of the Humanitas, the Nature of the Reptilians, the Knowledge of the Cyanites and the Aether of the Spectrals.

## **Fragment 5: The three great forces of Numinia**

Three great forces act as the engine of Numinia, shaping both its dynamics and its morphology: the Veil, the Threshold and the Prism. These three forces, which exist as much fused as in conflict, give rise to an unstable reality in which forms and concepts are subject to disturbance, alteration and transformation. It is the action of these forces that turns Numinia into a vortex of chaos and disorder, but it is also what allows the city to reorder itself constantly: an anomaly at one point triggers another of equal magnitude elsewhere by way of compensation; a change prompts the appearance of a new model, which fits into the city as though it had always existed; acts of creation end up setting off events, which balance the elements that keep being added to Numinia's universal heritage.

And all of this is owed to the nature of the Akashic Records and to the character of each of these forces as it operates within them: the supernatural condition of the Veil, the physical materialisation of the Threshold, the refractive power of the Prism. Each of them holds something as mysterious as it is powerful, and a deep knowledge of these three energetic powers is the key to understanding the cosmic motion of Numinia.

The scope of these forces is set out below.

### **The Breath of the Veil**

_«There where time falls silent and memory weaves its own substance»._

The Veil is not a place. It is not, exactly, a dimension or a border. It is the translucent membrane between the visible and the unmanifested, between what was and what has not yet been spoken. In Numinia, the Veil represents the conscious echo of the world: a spiritual and symbolic field in which untaken decisions, unlived memories and destinies yet to be revealed resonate.

This concept has its roots in the esoteric doctrines of the Akasha, brought from Indian wisdom into European thought by theosophists and occultists such as Madame Blavatsky, and later developed with spiritual depth by Rudolf Steiner, founder of Anthroposophy. While the term comes from Sanskrit and translates as «ether» or «space», for Steiner, as for Theosophy, the Akashic Records were a vibrational plane of universal memory in which everything that had ever happened ‒even the most intimate or unrevealed‒ was recorded as an energetic inscription. It was not merely a great archive of history, but experience stored by the cosmos, a space in which every gesture, every word and every action expressed in the universe was recorded.

**Holberins and the science of the soul**

In the final years of his life, Holberins crossed a fine line between quantum physics and metaphysics. His interest in the Akasha did not arise from dogma, but from observing the behaviour of energy: fractals, patterns of chaos, irregularities which, seen through the right prism, contained a hidden order. Through Athanasius's _Codex Hermopolitanus_ he discovered that certain gear mechanisms ‒such as his legendary cosmic device‒ replicated universal structures and could resonate with the frequencies of the Veil.

He was likewise able to find correlates between the Akashic Records of Steiner and the theosophists and certain scientific and philosophical principles that were beginning to build a new epistemological paradigm. To begin with, he found certain analogies in the thought of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: the idea that nature possessed a kind of universal memory, within a monist vision in which matter, energy and thought formed part of a single continuum, developed by the biologist Ernst Haeckel; the concept of electromagnetism set forth by Clerk Maxwell, as a field in which the universe can be understood as a continuous flow of dynamic information; or the philosopher Henri Bergson's idea that the past does not disappear but remains inscribed in reality, so that the present is nothing but a constant updating of the past.

Through his contact with Steiner's texts, he began to understand that the city of Numinia, rather than a virtual or utopian structure, was an archetypal projection of a deep memory, a city that could be inhabited in spirit before it was inhabited in code. And so, after the fall of Numinia in 1920, Holberins, aided by the principles of the Akasha, projected the spiritual image of the city onto a timeless plane, suspended in the Veil.

**The Veil between ruin and rebirth**

During the ten decades in which Numinia went unspoken, something remained active. The spiritual energy did not cease, the patterns did not dissolve, the symbols did not dissipate. The Veil anchored the memory of the city, as though its soul had been written into an intangible layer of reality, waiting for a new whisper.

When the Oracles, in 2020, activated Athanasius's mechanism in the ruins of Tuna el-Gebel, they did not merely open a rift in space-time. They activated the breath of the Veil, the latent breathing of the forgotten city. It was as if a long-withheld word were spoken once again: Numinia. And perhaps the Akasha had become the abstract, metaphysical space of what, as early as the last third of the twentieth century, the physicists John Wheeler and Bryce DeWitt defined as the wave function of the universe, a quantum state in which the totality of space, matter and simultaneous fields accumulates, forming a memory that contains all the information of the cosmos.

The Veil does not only preserve; it transmutes. It is archive, altar and oracle. Every character who walks through Numinia carries echoes they do not always understand. Some hear them. Others live them. A few alter them.

And when someone attunes to the Veil, the whole city changes around them.

**The birth of Chaos: the act that broke the heavens**

<u>Founding myth</u>

During the Dark Age, the Chronothecaries, masters of fractal time, and the Black Engineers, architects of the impossible, decided to force the regularity of the Moon. They did so for hermeneutic and mnemonic ends: the erratic cycles of the sky interfered with symbolic formulas, narrative rituals and the preservation of knowledge.

To that end they devised an unprecedented intervention: to accelerate the lunation to exactly 29 days by means of technology, since a lunar cycle on Earth spans 29.5306 days.

Only because Numinia was born without God, or with no gods but technological ones, did the civilisation that arose in the Akasha, amid nebulae of darkness and chaos, see fit to carry out this act of quasi-divine intervention, of cosmic maladjustment, in favour of the semiotic and semantic balance of the city-state.

But, wise in the end, they were aware that it would break the orbital and natural balance.

<u>The Scarab of the Three Wheels</u>

To stabilise the distorted cosmos, the Black Engineers turned to the creation of Holberins from the days of the Bildung Complex, during the Pre-Ashen Age: the device of toothed wheels whose motion was owed to the feedback of kinetic energy generated by the mechanical dung beetle that moved its jointed legs at the centre of the machinery.

Thus Khepri became the Perpetual Scarab, a biotechnological organism that not only turns the Three Wheels of Chaos for all eternity, but also controls, thanks to an inserted system of dimensional gears, the active movement of the whole technological system responsible for balancing the adjustment of the lunar orbit:

- Orbital balance rings
- Network of distributed gravitational Resonators
- Tectonic Stasis Cores

And a set of satellites that watch over stability in the lands of Numinia. The scarab ceased to be solely machine and beast; it became a living sign, an active seal of chronotechnical will.

<u>Consequences for Numinia</u>

The balance imposed by the Black Engineers, following the cosmic movement of the Chronothecaries, was nonetheless incomplete. Numinia still suffers the aftereffects of the great lunar adjustment:

- Tides of impossible amplitude
- Zones of constant seismic pressure
- Mountain ranges that grow in spirals
- Ascending rain in the Neuma Subvale
- Temporal tributaries that flow backwards in time

And this imbalance is not only physical. The Akasha responds with symptoms of symbolic saturation:

- Narrative spaces that fold in on themselves
- Words that break down into other realities
- Texts that mutate when no one is watching them
- Matter that responds to ideas

This is where the Veil appears: a magical, semiotic, topological reaction. The friction between the adjusted physical world and the broken symbolic world generates a vibrating membrane. The Veil is, then, the living skin of the maladjustment, the residual energy that escapes the scarab's control. Through the Veil, Numinia gains access to the creation of meaning, to magic, to delirium, but also to their antonyms: corruption, dissonance and oblivion.

In short, and to clarify its function in the game, let us establish that the Veil is related to the psychic world, and for that reason Breath of the Veil points act as bonuses to actions that involve psychic Characteristics: Intelligence, Wisdom, Perception and Charisma.

**The dark reverse of the Veil: the Cryptahedra**

« _What is not resolved in the soul… tends to organise itself in stone._ »

The Cryptahedra are spontaneous stone formations that emerge on the periphery of Numinia, generally in arid, mountainous or rocky regions. At first glance they might look like ancient eroded temples or cyclopean structures abandoned by forgotten civilisations. But beneath their impossible geometry hides a far more disturbing truth: the Cryptahedra were not built; they arose. They are not works of architecture: they are mineral epiphanies of the collective unconscious.

They form when mental conflicts, repressed traumas or dissonant impulses of the human psyche find no channel in the symbolic and, through accumulation, acquire density and crystallise into rock. Each Cryptahedron is a solidified psychological labyrinth, a sick mind translated into space.

There is no universal map of a Cryptahedron. Each one is unique, and its architecture changes ‒sometimes imperceptibly‒ according to the state of mind of whoever explores it. Its corridors repeat impossible sequences, its chambers reverberate with unresolved ideas, and its doors open onto dilemmas that no one has ever fully faced.

Some are formed of pristine basalt blocks, others of pulsing sandstone, others of fractured crystals that reflect thoughts that have not yet occurred. Within, time fragments and symbols rearrange themselves. It is not unusual for those who walk through one to hear echoes of their own past, or to see inscriptions with words they have never spoken… but which belong to them.

Entering a Cryptahedron is an experience of symbolic confrontation. Balanced individuals tend to get lost, fall into semantic loops or even suffer nervous breakdowns. Only deeply altered minds, such as those of the Ostramires, the sorcerers who practise dark magic, are able to navigate its corridors without dissolving, for their very alienation acts as an interpretive key.

Many Ostramires are suspected of having completed their processes of transfiguration inside Cryptahedra. Some call them «ontological matrices»; others, «stable wounds of the global consciousness».

Inside the Cryptahedra one may find:

- <u>Moving inscriptions:</u> Phrases that rewrite themselves while one watches them, revealing thoughts the reader has tried to suppress.
- <u>Mirror-chambers:</u> Rooms identical to the explorer's memories, but slightly distorted, as if something had edited them.
- <u>Parasitic symbols:</u> Glyphs that cling to the visitor's body, modifying their inner language.
- <u>Identity ruptures:</u> Points in the labyrinth where visitors forget their name, role or purpose, and must rebuild themselves narratively in order to advance.

The Cryptahedra are the object of institutional denial. The Summa Archive refuses to classify them officially, and the most orthodox Hermeneuts regard them as failures of the Akashic system of interpretation. Yet both Ostramires and radical Hermeticists venerate them as sacred spaces, claiming that they contain fragments of the first language, prior to the Veil, when matter still responded to pure thought.

It is believed that several enclaves of the periphery (such as the Khar Hills or the Lethe Gorges) are in reality Cryptahedra disguised by erosion and fear.

### **The frontiers of the Threshold**

« _There where force awakens and matter remembers that it can move._ »

The Threshold is the force of Numinia that acts as the antagonist of the Veil. If the Veil is the current of spiritual, psychic and metaphysical energies that turn the wheels of the Akashic Records, the Threshold is the materialisation of the physical energies that make their nature work. The Threshold is therefore present wherever there is a source of natural energy.

The Threshold is not a wall, nor a portal in the literal sense. It does not separate two worlds: it is the exact point where the world takes on a body. It is where energy ceases to be possibility and becomes force, where vibration acquires weight and the idea meets resistance in matter. In Numinia, the Threshold represents the physical manifestation of the cosmos: the dynamic field in which natural laws, telluric forces and material structures organise themselves to sustain reality.

If the Veil is the membrane of meaning, the Threshold is the contact surface. It is the place where thought stumbles against gravity, where movement needs muscle and where every act of will must be translated into action. The Threshold holds neither memories nor promises: it holds tensions. Tides, waves, pressures, impacts. Everything that pushes the world to move.

From a philosophical perspective, the Threshold can be understood as the material counterpart of the Akasha. Where the Akasha records and the Veil reveals, the Threshold executes. It is the dimension in which invisible forces are translated into observable phenomena: currents, fractures, displacements, combustion, impulse. Reality, in its most tangible aspect, rests upon this network of physical energies that runs through the cosmos like a web of pressures and balances.

When the Numinian system suffers deep disturbances ‒when the tension between meaning and matter becomes unstable‒ the Threshold manifests its own symptoms:

- Waves of energy that alter the stability of the surroundings
- Gravitational variations or distortions of physical space
- Telluric vibrations that run through the city
- Objects or structures that respond to invisible energetic changes

At such moments it becomes evident that the Threshold is not only the support of the world, but also its dynamic limit: the line at which reality asserts itself against chaos.

The Threshold is, therefore, the living structure that sustains the city. It is neither a hidden plane nor an arcane mystery: it is the physical pulse of the universe of Numinia. Wherever natural forces meet, balance each other or collide, the Threshold manifests as resistance, impulse and form.

In game terms, the Threshold is related to the physical and natural world, to all those actions that involve force, movement or direct interaction with matter. For that reason, Threshold points act as bonuses to actions that involve physical Characteristics: Strength, Movement, Constitution and Size.

### **The force of the Prism**

« _There where light touches, every form remembers that it could have been another._ »

The Prism is neither an object nor a doctrine: it is the living law of refraction. Where the Veil keeps and the Threshold manifests, the Prism unfolds; where the Veil reveals one truth and the Threshold jealously guards a single current, the Prism breaks them down into an infinity of possible trajectories. It is the phenomenal matrix that allows a single scene to contain multiple readings without contradicting itself.

Holberins sensed its trace in patterns, fractals and resonances: not as a separate magic, but as the optical anatomy of reality. When the city was spoken again, the Veil held its memory; the Prism organised its variations, enabling each guild and each faction to project its own angle on the world.

It does not create truths: it multiplies perspectives. That is why there are Prism Cells: materialisations of affinity that record how one chose to look. Where the Veil requires rite and the Threshold demands action, the Prism asks for choice: every gesture opens a spectrum; every choice, a colour.

**Dynamics of the Prism**

The Prism is the living lens of Numinia, a field of spiritual refraction that splits light ‒and with it, memory and intention‒ into countless possible trajectories. It is the force that orders and redistributes the possibilities of the world, generating symbolic routes, changes of perspective and the very diversity of destinies. While the Veil preserves and transmutes what has not yet been spoken in the metaphysical dimension, and the Threshold manifests what moves in the physical environment, the Prism chooses, branches and partially clarifies, allowing the threads of the story to intertwine or separate depending on the gaze that passes through it.

**The Prism Cells**

The Prism Cells are living nodes, both physical and spiritual, in which the light of the Prism condenses to interact with the Factions ‒the great fields of development of Numinia‒. Each Cell acts as a catalyst, aligning the energy of the faction with the greater currents of the Akasha.

- For the Neo-Atlantists, the Prism manifests as chromatic bursts that alter the aesthetics and visual memory of the city.
- For the Stellar Circle, it behaves as a pulse that reorganises the relationship between the organic and the artificial.
- For the Hermeticists, the Prism takes on fluctuating temporal patterns that offer glimpses of alternative futures.
- For the Heirs of Eleusis, it materialises as dark geometries in which light folds, revealing hidden possibilities.

The position and activity of the Cells are not fixed: they appear and go out following rhythms linked to the «Cycle of the Prism» in the Numinian calendar. In the game, Prism Cells appear when the players make good interpretations of interpretable circumstances, events and objects.

**The Prism in the Dark Age**

After the fall of the first Numinia and its immersion in the Akasha, the city did not die out; it fractured into a swarm of erratic beams. During the Dark Age, these refractions became intermittent beacons that allowed certain wanderers to find their bearings in the void: the first manifestation of the Prism. Where the Veil offered latent memory, the Prism offered paths that were uncertain but passable.

Its light then mingled with the Akashic currents, giving rise to phenomena such as:

- Prismatic trails: routes that appeared and disappeared as a traveller passed.
- Bridges of colour: temporary links between fragments of the board of Numinia.
- Faction echoes: spontaneous resonances of the Prism Cells, which influenced distant enclaves.

Without the Prism, the Mediators and other guardians of the Dark Age would never have found the way to reconstruction.

**The birth of the Mediators of the Prism**

When the light of Numinia scattered into the chaos of the Akasha, the Mediators of the Prism arose. They were not builders in the classical sense, but interpreters of those first refractions: figures wrapped in veils of light who could see the hidden paths within the chromatic dispersion.

They:

- Found the lost fragments of the great board and wove them together into invisible patterns.
- Carried labyrinthine mirrors capable of splitting and recomposing light, revealing forgotten routes and probable futures.
- Became custodians of balance, teaching others to read the play of colours projected by the universe onto the city.

Today it is said that they still roam the limits of Numinia, protecting with their mirrors the secrets that only light is able to reveal.

### **Comparison of forces**

If the Veil and the Threshold are antagonistic forces, the Prism is the refractive power that permeates them. If the Prism and the Veil are the two great metaphysical currents that structure the experience of Numinia, the Threshold is the mass of physical energies that sets it in motion. Although they manifest in different ways, the three interact constantly, shaping the course of the city and of those who inhabit it. The Veil guards what must not or cannot be openly revealed, preserving the echoes of destiny at rest. The Threshold marks the borders between the tangible and the sensible, revealing an essentially unstable character. The Prism, by contrast, acts as a catalyst of the multiple, opening routes and perspectives that transform perception and action.

While the Veil guards arcane memory and the essential secret, the Threshold manifests the tangible even when it is not visible, and the Prism unfolds reality in its many facets, offering a spectrum of choices and possibilities. They are opposing and complementary forces: one encloses and protects; the other acts and liberates; the last expands and diversifies. Understanding their balance is the key to interpreting the very fabric of Numinia.

## **Fragment 7: Steiner's Dreams**

Before Holberins designed Numinia as a utopian city-state on the game board of the Bildung Complex, another mind had already glimpsed its outlines in the mists of the unconscious. It was Rudolf Steiner, Austrian philosopher, occultist and founder of Anthroposophy, who, between 1904 and 1912, at his residence in Dornach (Switzerland), began to record a series of prophetic dream-visions that would later become known as Steiner's Dreams.

These visions were not mere night-time fantasies but true dream voyages, in which Steiner wandered through impossible structures, living enigmas and civilisations veiled by time. From his accounts ‒fragmentary, lyrical and often written in symbolic code‒ emerge images and concepts that Holberins would later recover and turn into the foundations of Numinia.

Steiner spoke of a city without matter, built from ideas and governed by archetypal laws; a city where symbols became doors and language was an architectural blueprint. When Holberins discovered these writings, he understood that what Steiner had dreamed was more than a spiritual metaphor: it was the invisible scale model of a city yet to come.

Steiner's Dreams, then, are not just a curiosity that predates Numinia. They are the hidden seed of its genesis, the echo that announced it in the corridors of the soul long before it took shape in the physical world. In them the mythological skeleton of Numinia is traced, and in their most cryptic symbols lies the key that connects the world of dreams with the real one.

### **Dream adventures at «Monte Verità»**

**A peculiar encounter**

Popular chronicles tell of a peculiar encounter, sometime between 1907 and 1909, between Rudolf Steiner and the two men who were then Freud's most promising disciples, Carl Gustav Jung and Otto Gross, at the libertarian retreat in Ascona (Switzerland) known as «Monte Verità». Conceived as an anarchist commune outside bourgeois social norms, the centre, founded and run by the Belgian businessman Henri Oedenkoven and his wife, the German pianist Ida Hofmann, championed ideas as novel as vegetarianism, nudism, feminism and ecology, and for years it was a gathering place for many thinkers and artists of the day. Names such as Hugo Ball, Isadora Duncan, Paul Klee, Mikhail Bakunin, Hermann Hesse, Franz Kafka, Thomas Mann and Mircea Eliade passed through it, as did the aforementioned Rudolf Steiner, Carl Gustav Jung and Otto Gross.

The story goes that Steiner came for a ten-day stay, to get away from the noise of the world and attune himself to his spiritual centre. On the second night he became caught up in a heated conversation with the two psychoanalysts about the possibility of connecting spiritually with other worlds, other realities, other universes or, quite simply, other times and places.

His two companions each brought their own view. Jung, judging by the accounts, must already have been shaping his thesis of synchronicity, according to which certain phenomena that seem magical can be explained by an eccentric relationship between events and the unconscious psyche. Synchronicity thus describes events that coincide without any cause, yet hold enormous meaning for the individual. This synchronicity would arise from a space-time relativity connected to an activity of the collective unconscious that is subject to the same relative nature. Jung was, after all, convinced that some part of the human soul is not bound by the laws of space and time, although he always held synchronicity to be an observable principle, not a provable scientific theory. Many years later, encouraged by the arrival of the Theory of Relativity and advances in quantum physics, he would develop this thesis in several studies on the relativity of psychic phenomena and of the space-time continuum, going so far as to state:

« _Space and time, in themselves, are nothing. They are hypostatised concepts born of the discriminating activity of the conscious mind, and they form the indispensable coordinates for describing the behaviour of bodies in motion. But if space and time are only apparent properties of bodies in motion and are created by the intellectual needs of the observer, then their relativisation by psychic conditions is no longer a matter for astonishment but is brought within the bounds of possibility_ ».

With this collection of ideas, still unripe but powerful, and always according to popular legend, Jung and Steiner debated whether consciousness could connect with that higher force which Jung recognised as the collective unconscious, and which for the occultist was directly tied to the Akashic Records (the memory of the universe, for the Theosophists). Gross, meanwhile, far less bound by scientific principles and therefore bolder in temperament and thought, is said to have been convinced that he could use hypnosis to guide Steiner's mind so that the relative nature of the unconscious would find its way to other dimensions, other times or other places. And if Jung, as they say, tried to warn them both of the dangers such practices could hold, besides pointing out their lack of rigour and consistency, Steiner was deeply enthused by Gross's proposal, and no one could now talk him out of undergoing the hypnosis session.

That same night, the Croatian philosopher was hypnotised by Freud's most insubordinate disciple ‒an attitude that, a few years later, would get him expelled from the psychoanalytic movement‒, and he remained unconscious for a long hour. When he woke, he described a confused journey through utterly unknown landscapes and regions. Jung, somewhat sceptical, saw in it the confusion produced by the fanciful wishes of a suggestible and enormously imaginative mind; Gross, elated, saw the extraordinary experiences of an astral journey through the rifts of space and time.

The episode was forgotten after dinner, when the games hour began at «Monte Verità», and it was not mentioned again in the days that followed, remaining just one more anecdote of the eccentric commune. Nevertheless, after his ten-day retreat, Steiner felt driven by an unknown force to write a detailed transcription of the eight dreams he had had over the eight following nights, stressing the vivid and extraordinary nature of what he would call «dream adventures». Just as his hypnosis session had turned out to be unusual, living through these dreams, whose nature felt to him as real as it was supernatural, affected him so singularly that he felt an urgent need to write them down, with an astonishing ability to remember and record every detail.

### **The dreams revealed**

Although the original transcription of Steiner's dreams has been lost, Holberins's later work on them survives, and through it we have a more or less detailed description of those dreams.

On the first night, after the hypnosis session he had undergone, Steiner came to dinner in a state of great mental excitement. To stimulate his cognitive activity further, the economist Max Weber, a frequent visitor, started a conversation about statistics and mental inference based on Bayes' Theorem and Markov chains, having been deeply impressed by the recent work that the mathematician Andrey Markov had published only a few years earlier. Steiner went to bed in a markedly agitated state and slept restlessly, drifting in and out of a half-waking doze. His dulled awareness caught the distant sound of the piano coming from the drawing room, played by the wise and delicate hands of Ida Hofmann. The piece was «Für Elise» by Ludwig van Beethoven. Whether because of Rudolf Steiner's wavering perception of the music or because of the jumble of voices mixing with the performance itself, the piece reached the philosopher incomplete, with some notes missing. Steiner then had the first of his «dream adventures».

**Dream Adventure 1: The Adventure of Curiosity**

In this experience, the Croatian occultist found himself transported into his own brain, passing through all the mental processes that took place as he perceived the fragmented version of «Für Elise». In his dream, Steiner not only had to understand how perception received this stimulus, extracting the useful information along a cognitive path that in the dream he recognised as an eccentric set of Markov chains; he also had to cross a path of floating beams marked with different musical notation symbols in order to rebuild the piece. Every time Steiner chose the wrong note he fell into the void and had to start again. By wisely structuring all his cognitive processes, connected to the outside states, he finally managed to piece the melody back together and cross the path of floating beams, reaching a place where Beethoven's bagatelle came to him clear and complete, just as Ida Hofmann was playing it. Curiously, the deeper he sank into the dream, the sharper and more accurate his perception of outer reality became.

********************

The next day Steiner ran into Jung again and told him about the night's experience in the lands of dream. Rather than trying to analyse its meaning, which he did not go into much, the Swiss psychologist spoke to him about archetypes and representational models that are present in every culture and every form of expression, such as the symbolic elements of children's fairy tales. Jung seemed keenly interested in these classic characters and situations, as they would be the first model references of the unconscious. At dinner, Steiner discussed these symbolic representations in children's stories with the writer Hermann Hesse, who had come to the sanatorium to cure his alcoholism. Hesse, who had spent weeks shut away in a cave with the German poet Gusto Gräser, another Monte Verità regular (with his own settlement among the rocks near the sanatorium), argued that the allegorical models of fairy tales had to be destroyed in order to overcome one's most intimate conflicts. Jung, for his part, argued that the conflicts hidden in these symbolic elements had to be accepted and integrated in order to achieve individuation.

Loaded with all this, Steiner went to his bedroom. He soon fell into a deep slumber, building a dream that held much of what he had gathered from that day's conversations.

**Dream adventure 2: The Haunted House of Tales**

The Croatian philosopher and occultist found himself in the entrance hall of a squalid haunted house. The house held several rooms, each named after a character from classic children's tales: Snow White, Cinderella and Alice. A space-time disturbance had shaken the house, and the stories of these characters were falling apart, leading them to an ill-fated ending. Steiner had to face three clocks representing Christmases past, present and future, echoing Dickens's _A Christmas Carol_, and find the correct time on each one in order to fix the dissonances in the fairy tales and so prevent their destruction. Each room offered him a clue for finding these times. Once he succeeded, his spirit felt, within the dream, much more at ease.

********************

For the third day, the managers of Monte Verità, Oedenkoven and Hofmann, had scheduled a theatre performance with an amateur cast drawn from many of the sanatorium's regulars. Hugo Ball himself, the father of Dadaism, had taken charge of directing the event, which, like the extravagant shows of the _Cabaret Voltaire_ a few years later, relied heavily on improvisation. The play performed that night was a wild compilation of Shakespeare's major tragedies, and it was undoubtedly the inspiration for Steiner's next dream. That night, his dream experience raised the old curtain of a theatre stage, above which, in large letters, stood the words «Shakespeare Theatre».

**Dream adventure 3: Three Shakespearean Maidens**

The Shakespeare Theatre of his dream actually seemed to be a temple built to house the tombs of three Shakespearean maidens. While the ghostly presences of Lady Macbeth, Ophelia and Desdemona seemed to haunt the dark corridors of the theatre, the objects of their deaths lay at the centre of the stage: a dagger, a vial of poison and a basin of water. Backstage, hidden by the rear wall, stood three gravestones, each carved with the name of one of the three maidens, though in encrypted form. Steiner had to decrypt the three names in order to match the three Shakespearean ladies with the objects that stood for their deaths. Fragments of the plays, scattered among boxes, gave him the clues he needed to understand how death had come to each of them.

********************

Steiner woke deeply moved by this dream. He was beginning to realise that a kind of Ariadne's thread ran between his different dream adventures, passing through the labyrinths of his consciousness. That afternoon, searching the Monte Verità library for books on labyrinthine structures, he came across an old volume containing Giovanni Battista Piranesi's fabulous engravings of imaginary prisons. After discussing these engravings with Hesse, the dinner conversation turned to the various instruments of power and repression the state relies on, such as the perverse prison panopticon devised by Jeremy Bentham, or the death penalty, which every member of Monte Verità unanimously condemned. Steiner went to sleep with a strange sorrow that seemed to have settled in his soul. Piranesi's famous prisons and the state's instruments of repression appeared in his dream, giving rise to the death row that formed the setting of his fourth dream adventure.

**Dream adventure 4: The last condemned**

In this dream Steiner found himself inside the abandoned, imaginary prison of Patapsco (Baltimore), specifically in the wing where those sentenced to death awaited execution. Within the dream, the wing had been closed many years earlier, yet at the same time the philosopher's dream seemed to have moved several decades into the future. Steiner dreamed of a death row inhabited only by rats, but where the remains of the lives of the prisoners who had once filled it were still left behind: photos, letters and other objects that revealed broken lives. To get out of that terrible place, Steiner had to find out who had been the last person condemned to death before the wing was closed. And the only way to find out was to dig through the grim remains of their miserable existences. A visit to the electric chair itself, rotting on a scaffold in the prison yard, seemed unavoidable, as did hearing the last words of the last condemned man.

********************

When he woke, Steiner was already completely absorbed in the thread of his dream adventures. He sensed the link between them powerfully; yet it was obvious that it was not their content or storyline that connected them. His mind was simply travelling to some place that gathered up the references he had lived through while awake. Or else the settings of his dreams matched these references through an effect of synchronicity. This investigative approach led him into a long conversation with Gross about the deductive, detective-like side of the mind. They returned to the degrees of inference that had filled the conversation before the first dream, taking as examples the literary figures of Auguste Dupin and Sherlock Holmes, created by Edgar Allan Poe and Arthur Conan Doyle respectively. When he went to bed that night he was still pondering how much of inference, Bayes' Theorem and Markov chains there was in the deductive minds of these detectives.

**Dream adventure 5: Murder Mystery Mansion**

And so Steiner arrived, quite spontaneously, in the dreamscape of his next dream: a strange mansion, built as a modular house, was the scene of the murder of an aristocratic lady who, in the dream, went by the name of Lady Hampton. A small-scale model of the house, with various clues to the crime, stood in the drawing room. On an investigative tour through every corner of the house, a series of clues helped the philosopher identify the murderer among five suspects, whose portraits hung on the walls of one of the first-floor bedrooms. Everything was set up like a classic literary detective scene. It could just as well have been the house in the Rue Morgue or the Baskerville mansion.

********************

After this fifth experience, the philosopher began to understand the connection between the dreams, which built this series of dream adventures like the chapters of a single overall journey. It was a journey through which his mind seemed meant to learn (or recognise) something about how its own processes worked. It took him to different places, not invented from his waking references but recovered, by way of them, from the past, present and future, breaking across the lines of space and time. And all of this because his consciousness had been connected directly to its own relativity. Was it possible, then, for a hypnosis session to awaken a spontaneous path of knowledge, like a journey of the spirit through synchronicity? When he shared these thoughts the next day, Steiner met with several conflicting responses. Jung dismissed his idea of synchronicity as «esoteric» and «reckless». Weber, while acknowledging the theory's interest, went further and called it a «fabulous tale of science fiction», borrowing the odd term coined by the journalist William Wilson in the mid-19th century to describe a futuristic novel built on a hypothesis about possible realities to come. Far from feeling rebuffed, let alone discouraged, Steiner spent the day in the Monte Verità library, immersed in esoteric books and in novels that described future worlds as possible realities. Both ideas seemed to come together in his next dream adventure, which took him to a world that moved between the fantastic and the futuristic, between outer space and myth.

**Dream adventure 6: Cosmic Journey to Numen-Vipe**

A vaulted platform, as if it were the inside of a planet, held a number of buildings that took something from what, in the early 20th century, was the new architecture, the kind that would soon give rise to Art Deco, and something that seemed lifted from a science-fiction city. That world had a strange and disturbing peculiarity: colour did not exist. An outlandish character urged Steiner to collect several blocks of binary code, which he had to take to a satellite outside the vault so that the rays of light that produced colour could spread once more. Travelling through space on ethereal beams, the Croatian esotericist reached the satellite, where another character, just as eccentric as the first, collected the blocks of code. Steiner was then able to start up a gigantic futuristic machine that fired the beams of light that gave rise to colour, and only then did a platform open in space, letting him descend into a picturesque, colourful world of roads and buildings that were just as futuristic. When he arrived, a sign read: «Vipe. Express your reality».

********************

Rudolf Steiner was becoming convinced that his mind was treading through different places that had existed, existed now or would exist in the future, not only in this world but in other, parallel dimensions. A supreme consciousness, which showed itself as a gigantic cognitive network, connected and linked these different places through processes of inference. From a human, conscious point of view, this peculiar association was perceived as a surprising effect of synchronicity. The cosmic forces moving underneath seemed impossible to grasp except through primitive intuition, and their singularity was generally taken for chaotic movement. These conclusions took shape in his seventh dream, in which Steiner recreated a meeting between two figures he had never heard of, yet who seemed terribly familiar to him.

**Dream adventure 7: Arkham Revelation**

Steiner dreamed of a meeting between an eccentric writer, Howard Phillips Lovecraft, and an equally peculiar scientist, whom his dreaming mind knew by the name of Holberins. The meeting took place in the library of Miskatonic University, in the city of Arkham, where the writer offered the scientist a secret revelation hidden in the artworks there: paintings of indescribable cosmic creatures adorned the walls. After finding a key in the paintings and entering the inner level of the library, guarded by a dark gatekeeper, Steiner found himself compelled once again to extract an encrypted message, this time from a chilling poem about cosmic horrors.

********************

When he woke, Steiner did not know whether the dream writer's revelation lay in the coded messages that had given him access to the library's different rooms, or whether these only served to reach the deepest layers of the dream, where the true revelation would be found. In any case, once awake, the Croatian occultist could not remember the coded messages from his dream. Even so, the sense of an initiatory journey through different levels that his cycle of dreams was taking on led him back to the Monte Verità library. There he studied a magnificent edition of Dante Alighieri's _Divine Comedy_, illustrated with Gustave Doré's extraordinary, chilling engravings. Steiner did not leave the library at all that day, not even for dinner, and the small hours found him on one of the room's sofas. Right there, with Dante's book in his hands, he finally fell asleep and sank into his eighth and final dream.

**Dream adventure 8: The Awakening**

Heat. A great deal of heat. Steiner's first awareness in the dream world was an intense feeling of heat. Then he found he was trapped in a strange ring of fire, surrounded by a river of lava, in the middle of a vast plain of dark, abstract shapes. Above him he saw three more rings, rising towards the blackness of that endless sky. He was without doubt at the deepest level of the dream, and he had to climb up through the different levels in order to wake. His dream, however, had taken the shape of the circles of hell in Dante Alighieri's poem. Every ring was burning or scorched, and to pass from one to the next he had to work out a keyword from four symbolic images, amid bizarre scenes of burnt-out cars, of madmen and damned souls dancing among the flames to diabolical music, of explosions and universal disasters. As he climbed higher, Steiner felt hotter and hotter, and his intellect became aware that he was caught in a dream from which he had to wake. Fortunately, he did so in time to find that he was still in the library, which was being consumed by fire. Overwhelmed by smoke and heat, drenched in sweat, Steiner managed to escape the room just in time. Outside, the residents of Monte Verità had already noticed the fire and were forming a chain to put it out.

********************

Steiner later concluded that he had fallen asleep while reading the Inferno section of Dante's work, leaving a half-smoked cigar burning in the ashtray on one of the sofa's armrests. The cigar must have fallen to the floor and started the fire, which the paper of the books soon spread.

Beyond the minor trauma the experience meant for Rudolf Steiner, and the knowledge that he had caused the destruction of the Monte Verità library (which he hurried to rebuild, replacing every volume with his own money), the philosopher and occultist reflected at length on everything that had happened. Far from Ascona, some time after his cycle of dream adventures, he returned to the concept of synchronicity that Jung had planted in his mind. His encounter with Dante Alighieri's work that last night at the commune, with its particular focus on hell, together with the strange circumstance of having unwittingly started a fire, was without doubt a true event of synchronicity. His consciousness had joined forces with nature and the legacy of the ages to shape a final dream that had to be built as a hell of ascending circles in the sleeping consciousness of Monte Verità. It left the lesson that every paradise, by a contradictory eccentricity, holds a hell within it, and that, as Jung maintained, taking Plato's cave as an example, the only way to reach the light was to climb up out of the darkness. Perhaps, Steiner thought, because only in darkness does it seem possible to recognise the light; in brightness, the light surely goes unnoticed…

### **Holberins's impressions**

It was Holberins, then, who preserved Rudolf Steiner's dream experiences, writing down his own impressions of them. Steiner's manuscripts came into the hands of the (supposedly) Danish scientist around 1918, about ten years after Steiner had lived through his cycle of dreams at Monte Verità.

Steiner's text was a kind of revelation for Holberins. He could see the flood of information and deep knowledge pouring out of the Croatian occultist's mind, perhaps more clearly than Steiner himself. If Steiner's brain activity seemed to be a living body, with an energetic charge active enough, and therefore powerful enough, to experience certain extraordinary events, Holberins's vision was broad and rigorous enough to detect the scientific basis behind them, as well as the clarity of Steiner's seemingly fanciful or esoteric ideas.

Holberins, who studied atomic models and the way electrons behaved inside the structure of the atom, leaned towards the idea of quantum superposition: the assumption that any physical system (taking the electron as the example) could occupy all its possible states at once, revealing only one of those states at the moment it is quantified or measured. For Holberins, the barrier between this multidimensional existence of the physical object and its specific position within the real frame was tied to consciousness itself and to the way it perceived the universe.

Hypothetically, however, a particular force could break through that barrier, meeting something similar to the phenomenon of synchronicity championed by Jung. Although the Austrian psychoanalyst's theory was developed decades later, in the early 1950s, it reached Holberins in embryonic form through Steiner's manuscript. The physicist could see the affinities between his own scientific theories and the relativity of consciousness that Jung proposed, along with the way consciousness took in or experienced two simultaneous, seemingly causeless phenomena that were closely linked. The eccentric causality Jung proposed came close to the quantum superposition Holberins defended, and it accepted the possibility that a consciousness connected to the relativity of space and time could break the barrier between multidimensional existence and the rigid, absolute presence of the physical objects we normally perceive.

Steiner, it seemed, had broken that barrier in his dream adventures, connecting with experiences, concepts and ideas that were floating synchronously in the great mass of universal information (what Steiner, in line with Theosophy, called the Akashic Records). The Croatian occultist would have accessed not only arcane and present knowledge but also forms of wisdom, philosophical concepts, scientific theories and structural codes that were still to come. Although he had not been able to read them all clearly, he had sensed their significance and how they would shape the future. His dream diary thus became a kind of visionary prophecy which, beyond its esoteric side, could offer grounding and ideas for new research. In his impressions of Steiner's dreams, Holberins would write that it was not that the occultist had managed to see the future during his cycle of dream adventures, but that the future had found him.

And so Holberins, deeply inspired by these ideas drawn from Steiner's Dreams, began to conceive of a virtual environment: a space where the forces of superposition and synchronicity would be present, treasuring universal knowledge and offering multidimensional freedom to creation. From that inspiration Numinia was born, and two years later, in 1920, it would take physical form as an enormous game board inside the Bildung Complex.

But Holberins, whose science never gave up on philosophy and spirituality, sensed something more. The physicist not only saw the reality of a synchronous, multidimensional space. He not only imagined the potential, the grandeur and the glory of this place, which he whimsically named Numinia; he also saw, in its very conception, its end, its fall and its destruction in flames, as would indeed happen in the last days of 1920. Just as Steiner understood in the last of his dreams, Holberins understood the synchronicity between the occultist's dream experiences and the fulfilment of his own dream, as an idyllic city devoured by flames. Just as the Monte Verità library burned, just as Steiner made his way through the flames in his eighth dream, so Numinia would end up burning in the Bildung Complex. Holberins understood that the destruction of his imaginary city was inevitable, since it was built into its very identity and its evolution, for he shared Steiner's idea: that every paradise holds, at the same time, a hell. And yet, despite this, despite its inevitability, he set about pouring all his efforts and resources into building that complex and shaping the great game board of Numinia.

Because his ability to see the future went further still: Holberins was also able to glimpse a residue moving through space and time, in that same sphere of synchronous realities that is the memory of the universe, and the rebirth of his city a hundred years later. For creation and the search for knowledge were the same at the moment when the physicist was working through Steiner's manuscript, in the past, and in the future. That reality, represented by Numinia, was a body wrapped in synchronicity, a system occupying all places and all times at once, and all it took was for someone to break the barrier of consciousness in order to perceive it.

And, by that same synchronicity, the new Numinia built a century in the future would probably suffer the blows of its own era, and after being born, developing and growing, it might one day be devoured by flames. But there would always be someone who found it (or who was found by it), someone who came upon its trace in time and space, imagining it and bringing about a new birth. Numinia was, then, an eternal, ubiquitous concept that found its causality and its fulfilment across different eras and places. Wherever there was someone with enough curiosity and enough capacity to dream.
