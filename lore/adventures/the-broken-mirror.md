# **THE BROKEN MIRROR**

# **Module for the Numinia roleplaying game**
**Approximate duration:** 2–3 sessions.

**Difficulty:** medium.

**Main setting:** Ouroboros District, with journeys to Solomon and Vitruvian.

**Starting Conflict:** the collective memory of Numinia is suffering ever deeper alterations. The researcher Drusa Malter found a possible link between these anomalies and a Splintered Mirror kept in a forgotten storehouse; afterwards she disappeared without any record of her leaving.
# **The Root**

In recent weeks, the Ouroboros District has been the scene of alterations increasingly difficult to explain as mere anomalies of the Veil. Some residents remember streets that the maps deny ever existed; certain libraries show empty spaces where their inventories insist books ought to be kept; old popular songs lose verses that everyone recognises as missing, although no one manages to remember what they said.

Then greater contradictions appear. Marriages of which only one of the spouses retains any memory. Laws cited in official documents that do not appear in any legislative register. Buildings whose name changes according to the person looking at them. It does not seem that Numinia is simply forgetting its past: it seems to be remembering it in several incompatible ways.

The researcher Drusa Malter began to study these anomalies and detected a common resonance among several of their focal points. Her investigation ended up leading her to an old storehouse of the Museum of Arts and Relics, connected to its premises by the so-called Möbius Corridor.

There she found a Splintered Mirror.

Its surface does not only give back the reflection of whoever contemplates it, but incomplete scenes, impossible memories and alternative versions of known events.

Drusa disappeared shortly afterwards.

The records certify only her entry. No camera, document or system attests that she left the storehouse. Her last communications contain interference, fragments of observations and a word that does not appear in any official source accessible through the LAP: trakmósis.

The Concordia Council then summons the PCs.

# **Founding facts**

Drusa investigated the memory anomalies and located the Splintered Mirror. She entered the storehouse and there is no record of her leaving. _Trakmósis_ was the last intelligible word of her communications. The alterations continue to spread and the Mirror bears some relation to them. To reach the storehouse one must pass through the Möbius Corridor.

These facts must not be modified.
# **Spaces of Indeterminacy**

The GD must not decide before play whether the Mirror causes the anomalies or concentrates them; who fractured it; whether it was ever whole; what _trakmósis_ means; what exactly Drusa's state is; whether any faction or institution bears responsibility; nor what ontological relationship the Mirror has with akashic memory.

Nor must they decide what should finally be done with it.
# **Potential proposals**

The Mirror might redistribute memory instead of destroying it. The fracture might have been deliberate. _Trakmósis_ might designate an inversion between form, subject and memory. Drusa might have become integrated into the phenomenon, accidentally or willingly. The Mirror might contain futures as well as the past. It might even turn out that Numinian memory is stable precisely because it was always fragmented.

All of them are possibilities. None yet deserves the privilege of being true.
# **The Pieces of Conflict**

All the Pregenerated Pieces of this adventure are physical objects that the PCs can pick up and carry. Some may appear in different places if the fiction demands it, and new Pieces may arise from the players' actions.

**THE REPEATED TESTIMONY**

Five small acoustic capsules. Each contains the recording of a different person uttering, in separate circumstances:

_«The reflection learns the name before the face.»_

None of the witnesses remembers having learnt the phrase.

**THE APHASIC SEED**

A black seed the size of a finger. On its surface there ought to be a natural botanical glyph, but there is a perfectly defined hollow instead. When someone tries to name it repeatedly, a common word becomes momentarily inaccessible.

**THE ACCESS PASS**

A rigid card from the Summa Archive. It bears Drusa Malter's identification and a fragment that is still recoverable:

**«—ned by akashic fracture. Level: restricted.»**

It grants access to certain rooms of Aleph.

**THE COUNTERMANUAL OF MORPHOLOGY**

A small handmade book, with no recognised author. A handwritten note asserts:

«The prefix trak- indicates possession of the spirit by the form.»

Nothing guarantees that the assertion is true.

**THE FLAYED TONGUE**

A sculptural fragment made of petrified phonemes. Part of the piece has been torn away, leaving perceptible voids where there ought to be sounds.

It has a note attached:

«If it cannot be pronounced, does it exist?»

**THE MNEMONIC SHARD**

A fragment of crystal. When looked at against a light source, for a few seconds it reflects a place other than the one where it is.

Which place it shows can be determined through Dialogic Resonance.

**THE SPIRAL ARCHIVE — DIRECTION PIECE**

A physical sheet of paper, yellowed and folded several times, which appears in the Chamber of Phonosemantic Dissociation. On it there is a hand-drawn spiral diagram and notes by Drusa.

Once examined, it provides an unmistakable direction: it identifies the old Museum of Arts and Relics, the access to the Möbius Corridor and a concrete sequence that allows one to pass through its first fold without immediately returning to the beginning.

The document does not explain what they will find there or what the Corridor means, but it removes all doubt about where the Action must continue and how to begin to penetrate it.

This is the Direction Piece. Its appearance signals that the Investigation can conclude, although the players may go on gathering Pieces if they wish.
# **Presentation Phase**

The Presentation should work as an extended narration, with little dialogued roleplaying of the NPCs. The Resonances periodically interrupt the reading and allow the players to begin to populate the adventure.

In the inner levels of the Prytaneum there is a room that barely appears on the ordinary routes of the LAPs. The Veiled Chamber is not exactly hidden; it features in certain records and its entrances satisfy every bureaucratic regulation imaginable. Simply put, most citizens lack sufficient motive, authorisation or foolishness to enter.

On crossing the threshold, the air acquires a peculiar density. The room rises beneath a vault of gentle arches, supported by columns of aged bronze and decorated with dull gold. The walls are covered with shelves full of treatises, minutes and parchments whose classification seems to answer to neither date, author nor subject. The small plaques on the shelves mention categories such as _responsibility_, _moral debt_, _impossible precedent_ or _agreement necessary despite its evident bad taste_.

A round table occupies the centre. Four people wait around it. Their emblems allow the LAP to identify them immediately.

**Dialogic Resonance: the Veiled Chamber**

_What shows that the meeting had begun long before your arrival?_

_What element is especially striking in such a solemn space?_

_What indicates that someone or something is watching from the upper galleries?_

_What object or image reminds you of something important in your life?_

_What detail lets you anticipate that the meeting will end worse than it begins?_

The answers are woven in without being explained yet.

The Council then sets out the problem. The four delegates accept that the anomalies are real, that Drusa disappeared during her investigation and that the Mirror must be reached. Their agreement, however, ends exactly there.

Thalia considers that a structure capable of deforming collective memory poses a threat to the cultural continuity of Numinia, and she argues for its destruction or definitive closure.

Severin demands that it be studied: to destroy a manifestation that might offer knowledge about akashic memory would be, in his eyes, an intellectual barbarity perfectly unworthy of a civilised society.

Ibera maintains that, if the Mirror proves that memory can be recomposed, perhaps limiting oneself to restoring the past would be a premature renunciation of its possibilities.

Varro demands that they first stop the anomalies, stabilise the object and argue afterwards about metaphysics, politics or any other activity that proves more comfortable once the city has stopped forgetting its own streets.

**Dialogic Resonance: the delegates**

Which councillor arouses your distrust, and why?

Who makes a significant gesture when Drusa is mentioned?

What small object does one of the delegates handle and put away on noticing that they are being watched?

What discreet message does one of the delegates whisper to a PC?

What indicates that the hostility among these four did not begin with the Mirror?

Do not automatically turn any answer into a conspiracy. They are proposals.

The Council then presents Drusa Malter's file.

#### **Drusa Malter**

**Name:** Drusa Malter
**Guild:** Alchemists
**Faction:** Neo-Atlantists

**Species:** of mixed heritage, Humanite/Spectral
**Position:** Morphologist of Artefacts and Relics

The Morphologist studies how physical, linguistic and symbolic forms modify whatever they contain. Drusa specialised in artefacts whose structure alters memory or interpretation.

Her record still responds to the LAP.

Her status appears as ACTIVE.

Her location: NOT AVAILABLE.

The recovered records show that Drusa first investigated apparently unconnected anomalies. She discovered language phenomena in the Hyperglottic Gardens, documentary variations in the Summa Archive and emotional dissociations in the Sensory Cubicle. Later she concluded that all of them seemed to resonate with a common focal point located in the old Museum of Arts and Relics.

Her last coherent message points out that the Mirror does not seem to be merely losing memory, but might be redistributing it. Then comes static. Finally, a word:

trakmósis.

An ordinary query through the LAP produces no recognised entry.

If the PCs deliberately search in informal spaces, the GD may enable Rumourology.

**Rumourology of** **_trakmósis_**

«Trak- appears in some apocryphal texts associated with possession.»
Classification: Unknown origin.
Comment: The references point to one another and none preserves an earlier verifiable source.

«Mósis was an ancient root related to ritual transformation.»
Classification: Probably false.
Comment: The known examples appear only in modern glossaries of dubious provenance.

«The complete word appeared years ago on a forum devoted to errors of the Veil and was deleted shortly afterwards.»
Classification: As hard to prove as to refute.
Comment: Only partial screenshots survive, and their metadata do not match one another.

The Presentation ends when the Prytaneum itself manifests an anomaly. Some text in the Chamber changes for a few seconds; one of the LAPs receives an impossible notification; a shadow reproduces a gesture before its owner makes it; or some word spoken at the meeting momentarily loses its meaning.

Use several Resonance questions to pin it down.

Finally, the official register of those attending the meeting is updated.

To the names of the four councillors and the PCs another is added:

DRUSA MALTER — PRESENT.

Drusa is not there.
# **Deliberation Phase**

The PCs have a few minutes alone before leaving the Prytaneum.

The GD now addresses the players directly. Each of them must formulate a Starting Hypothesis about what is happening, what function they believe the Mirror has, what may have happened to Drusa and what relationship they imagine between the anomalies. The Hypothesis may refer to just one of these questions, or encompass them all.

They do not need to agree. The GD notes down the most fertile proposals and will later use them as narrative material, without automatically confirming them.

When all the hypotheses have been set out, ask where their PCs begin to investigate.
# **Investigation Phase**

The three main spaces are available from the start and can be visited in any order. Not all of them are compulsory, and the GD should accept other reasonable routes proposed by the players.

The appearance of the Spiral Archive will finally provide the clear direction towards the Action Phase.
## **The Hyperglottic Gardens**

The Gardens stretch out like damp terraces over an old part of Ouroboros. Their botany combines plant forms and phonetic structures: there are flowers whose movements produce soundless words, roots capable of sustaining sequences of prayers and plants whose colour modifies the emotional charge of whatever seems to be uttered around them.

The recent alteration has not turned the plants into talking creatures. It has done something more unsettling: some of them are producing words that do not belong to their repertoire, future expressions or memories of individuals who never touched them.

That meaning will affect how the reflections behave later on.

#### **Gestation Room**

The ancient coral creeper reproduces, in fragments:

«...the reflection does not sleep...» And then: «...the reflection is not one...»

At a certain moment the plant undergoes a violent expansion. The branches invade the space while phonemes and words seem to acquire material consistency. The PCs can escape, contain it, intervene in its structure or use any appropriate Competence.

A Failure may cause Confused or Destabilised; an especially deep Critical Failure may produce Unanchored if a PC temporarily loses their semantic relationship with the surroundings. The current States replace the old losses of Energy or Threshold as the consequence of this kind of conflict.

After the crisis, an unknown figure, like a faceless shadow, flees among the paths.

## **Direction Piece: the Spiral Archive**

When it is unfolded, it reveals handwritten notes by Drusa, a depiction of the old Museum of Arts and Relics and the diagram of the Möbius Corridor.

The sheet includes a clear instruction:

«Lower access. Closed heritage gallery. Do not follow the apparent geometry. At the first return, pass through the third frame before it repeats its image.»

The PCs now know where they must go and how to begin crossing the Corridor.

They do not know what they will find.

That difference is exactly what we need.
# **Reflection Phase**

Before heading to the Museum, try to create a place of rest. It may be the Hyperborean Tavern, a safe room in the Prytaneum, a room in the Cubicle itself or some space chosen by the players.

The players gather all their physical Pieces and relate them to one another.

Now they must agree on a theory.

What does the Mirror do? What happened to Drusa? What does _trakmósis_ mean? Why are language, memory, perception and documentation failing in different ways? What do they intend to achieve when they reach the depository?

The theory must end in a concrete objective.

## **Entrance to the Corridor**

The gate opens with a metallic groan and the corridor appears beyond it as an architecture that has forgotten the elementary courtesy of occupying a single position.

The floor runs straight for a few metres and then seems to curve without really changing direction. Some paintings reappear on the opposite wall before they have been left behind. The lights breathe with a slow cadence, and each dimming slightly alters the images hanging there.

A city hangs upside down from an empty sky. A reddish sun burns in the middle of a star-filled night. A river of incandescent sand runs through a town built beside a riverbed that never carried water. Further on, a crowd parades with the same face repeated; two shadows confront each other while their owners remain motionless; a banquet awaits diners represented only by dark hollows.

The paintings do not seem mistaken.

They seem to be arguing about what they remember.

The Spiral Archive makes it possible to get past the first fold. After that, the Corridor is open to interpretation.

**THE PAINTINGS AND HERMENEUTICS**

The paintings are Symbolic Figures. There is no longer any intended «correct» answer.

The GD can select the images that prove most fertile according to the PCs' Positions.

The interpretation must produce spatial consequences. A reading by a Runner of the Veil may reveal a way of passing through; an Ambassador's pragmatic interpretation may turn an image into a rule that governs that stretch; a Legionary may find an opposition of forces that explains the architecture; an Anacharchid may formulate a paradox that becomes literally true.

In this way, the Hermeneutic Action builds the Corridor while the PCs cross it.

**CONTAINMENT SITUATIONS**

The Corridor must keep a fast pace. Use Competence or Characteristic Rolls when the actions require them.

In the Paint Swell, one of the paintings spills over into the passage and the brushstrokes try to alter the PCs' faces.

In the Echoic Chamber, the characters hear their movements a few seconds before making them and may end up Desynchronised.

In The door that did not know it was a door, a stretch of wall has no function until the players find a way to establish what condition can truly turn it into an entrance.

There is no independent Distortion Clock. Failures directly alter the conditions of the scene.

**OPTIONAL CONFRONTATION**

**The Umbrílego of the absent painting**

Read or paraphrase the following narration before the players declare anchors.

The Corridor ends in a narrower gallery, lit only by the small lamps placed above each painting. The paintings are spaced at regular intervals, and they all have different frames: blackened wood, bronze, stone, dark glass. Halfway along the wall there is one that contains nothing.

It is not a blank canvas. Nor does it seem that the painting has been removed.

Inside the frame there is a grey surface that the eye cannot manage to recognise as wall. After looking at it for several seconds it becomes difficult to remember what one expected to find there. The absence seems to occupy a space of its own, as if the corridor had perfectly preserved the place of something after losing whatever was meant to fill it.

The painting immediately to the left shows a procession. The one on the right depicts a crowned figure before an empty throne.

When one of the PCs tries to wonder which image ought to exist between the two, the sounds of the corridor go out.

They do not diminish. They vanish.

Then a black fibre appears on the lower edge of the empty frame. It slides slowly across the wood and divides into several moist filaments. Others emerge from the top and begin to feel the neighbouring paintings with delicate, almost tactile movements. The mass that follows does not seem to pass through the wall. It seems to unfold from a depth that the frame had concealed until then.

First come four long, soft limbs that fold in an arachnoid manner without showing clear joints. Then a semi-translucent body made of taut membranes and fibrous tissues, glistening with an oily moisture that never quite comes away from its surface. At several points small protuberances form, swell and disappear like organs that had not yet decided on their function.

The head occupies the centre of the mass. It is a dark vegetal bulb, covered with nervous roots and large, whitish, pupilless eyes. None of them seems really to be looking at the PCs.

The creature moves towards the painting of the procession. It rests one of its limbs on the frame. Then it shifts towards the empty space. It extends the limb. It stops. It draws it back. It moves towards the coronation. It touches it. And it begins again.

Procession. Void. Coronation. Procession. Void. Coronation.

The third repetition produces a change. In the painting of the procession a face disappears. In the coronation the function of the crown ceases to be recognisable: it remains visible, but for a few seconds it looks like a completely unknown object.

When any of the PCs tries to name what is missing, reconstruct the painting or explain the sequence, the whitish eyes turn towards them all at once. The roots of the skull give off bluish flashes and the creature abandons the ritual to approach with a speed that bears no relation to the clumsiness of its earlier movements.

If the PC stops interpreting, the creature halts. It returns to the frame. Procession. Void. Coronation.

Around it other meanings begin to degrade. A sword is still painted in a nearby picture, but one of the characters does not remember what it is used for. A bridge keeps its shape and material, but for an instant no one can manage to think of it as something meant for crossing. A human figure loses the name that accompanied it on its label, and a few seconds later it is hard to remember that it ever had one.

The creature does not seem to be guarding the non-existent painting.

It seems to need it to go on not existing.

The present nature of the Umbrílego fits this behaviour exactly: it manifests around a contradiction, reacts to attempts to recover meaning and repeats an incomplete pattern derived from that absence.

**Pregenerated anchors for the GD**

Original contradiction: the frame keeps the position of a painting whose meaning has disappeared.

Attraction to meaning: any attempt to reconstruct what is absent awakens the immediate reaction of the Umbrílego.

Incomplete ritual: procession → void → coronation.

These are only the anticipated anchors. The players may infer others if they are supported by the scene.

If the roll fails, do not repeat the combat. Make the contradiction change: the Umbrílego may move the absence to another painting; one of the PCs may temporarily be depicted within the frame; the frame may begin to erase a Piece of Conflict from the group's memory; or the creature may induce a State. A failure transforms the conflict and forces it to be reinterpreted.

**CROSSROADS OF THE CORRIDOR AND THE DEPOSITORY**

The following Crossroads are deliberately designed so that no option is harmless. They are not three shades of the same problem; they are three realities capable of changing the course of the adventure.

It is not necessary to use them all.

**Crossroads I — The absent painting**

If a failure turns the empty frame into a central element, ask what its absence really represents:

1. The painting contained a fundamental event in the history of Numinia that was deliberately erased.

2. The painting depicts a future event whose disappearance is preventing it from ever happening.

3. The painting depicted one of the people present, and that person is also beginning to be erased from the records of Numinia.

All three consequences must begin to manifest immediately.

**Crossroads II — Drusa's voice**

When Drusa seems to communicate from the Corridor:

1. The voice really belongs to Drusa, but she is trying to prevent the PCs from reaching her.

2. The voice does not belong to Drusa: something inside the Mirror has learned to reproduce it and is deliberately leading the group.

3. The voice is a future memory of one of the PCs, who at some point will utter exactly the words they now attribute to Drusa.

Any of the three redefines the relationship with whatever awaits at the end.

**Crossroads III — Drusa Malter**

This one should be activated only when Drusa's nature has taken on enough importance.

1. Drusa is an artificial intelligence that had for years sustained a fully recognised civic identity without almost anyone knowing.

2. Drusa is an impostor, a member of the Commune of the Invisible Light sent to create chaos and uncover secrets of the Council of Delegates.

3. Drusa is in an irreversible coma, and what seems to be interacting with the PCs is an autonomous structure formed by her memories.

**Crossroads IV — The Mirror**

In the face of a failure during the Action:

1. The Mirror is fragmenting the memory of Numinia to prevent a single false memory from replacing all the others.

2. The Mirror is trying to put itself back together and uses the citizens' memories as material to complete the parts it is missing.

3. The Mirror is not bound to the memory of Numinia: Numinia is bound to the Mirror, and destroying it could eliminate part of the city's historical continuity.

From that moment on, the GD must make the consequences of the chosen option visible.

**Crossroads V —** **_Trakmósis_**

When the word becomes an operative element:

1. Trakmósis means that a form can completely appropriate the identity of whatever it contains.

2. Trakmósis designates the process by which two identities exchange their memories until neither can prove which was the original.

3. Trakmósis does not describe a process: it is the name of an entity or intelligence whose presence is expressed through the fractures of the Mirror.

Do not afterwards turn the choice into a mere linguistic fact. It must alter the fiction.

**Crossroads VI — Adaptable to a Piece created by the players**

Choose an especially important emergent Piece and present three severe consequences:

1. The Piece was deliberately introduced by Drusa to manipulate the PCs' investigation.

2. The Piece did not exist before the adventure began: the PCs' own contributions made it manifest physically and it is now altering the Conflict.

3. The Piece belongs to another version of the history of Numinia, and its presence is progressively replacing the object that occupied its place in this version.

These crossroads may be used freely whenever, with regard to the Containment Situations or the interpretations of the paintings, the players obtain a failure in any of the rolls.

## **The Forgotten Repository**

After getting through the Corridor, the PCs reach a large heritage chamber where covered display cases, ancient sculptures, uncatalogued artefacts and exhibition furniture pile up, left there by institutions that have long since disappeared.

In the centre stands the Mirror.

Its surface is dark and crossed by numerous cracks that give off pale glints. None seems to have exactly the same depth. Some are material fractures; others seem to open onto spaces that continue far beyond the glass.

The reflections do not match.

One of the PCs appears older. Another carries an object they do not possess. A third is not reflected in one of the shards. Some surfaces show the repository itself, but with the display cases in different positions.

**Dialogic Resonance: the reflections**

Ask what the reflection of a PC possesses that the original does not; what scar another one bears; what appears in the place of a PC missing from a shard; what action a reflection performs that its original would never perform; and what known location appears transformed in the background of one of the fragments.

Bring these answers back later.

At some distance from the Mirror stands a translucent structure of superimposed sentences.

Inside it remains Drusa.

Her emblem still responds to the LAP.

Status: ACTIVE.

Location: UNCLASSIFIABLE.

**THE SCARS OF THE MIRROR**

Keep the Triads of the original module, because they are one of its best ideas.

The Mirror reflects impossible historical scenes. Each one comes with three words.

There is no longer a score of 2 to 6 dice awarded for creativity. The players produce a collective interpretation and must then actually intervene in the scene, using their competences through their Relics, Weapons, Proscribed, Residues or any other resource that occurs to them (Special aptitudes, for example).

They must stabilise three Scars. Some of the available triads are:

**Void–Oath–Ash**

Scene: An army appears marching towards a river that flows not with water but with burning ash. The soldiers advance, but as they cross it they dissolve into smoke, as if their existence had not been confirmed.

**Spectre–Silence–Shadows**

Scene: A coronation is held in an empty hall, with no audience, a king kneeling before an unoccupied throne. The crown floats in the air, surrounded by shadows that seem to whisper, though no one makes a single sound.

**Fire–Pact–Star**

Scene: In a night camp, leaders of several factions meet beneath an incandescent star burning too close to the horizon. They greet one another, but the fire consumes the sky, leaving the pact incomplete.

**Door–Blood–Mirror**

Scene: A citadel shows its walls open, as if a gigantic door had collapsed. On the ground, a pool of blood reflects a different landscape, as if the liquid mirror led to another version of the place.

**Light-Vengeance-Pact**

Scene: A hero perched on a wall receives a torch from the hands of an ally. On raising it, he discovers that all those who follow him are watching him with hatred. The light darkens like a spilled liquid.

**Betrayal–Snow–Awakening**

Scene: In a village, the inhabitants ring soundless bells while the snow falls backwards, towards the sky. The peasants sleep standing up, frozen, and when they open their eyes they do not seem to recognise the place.

**Return–Shadows–Heavens**

Scene: A sacred temple is packed, but the faithful have no faces and weep black tears. The altars shine with contradictory symbols, as if they worshipped several gods at once.

**Jewel-Failure-Sword**

Scene: A king is crowned with a diadem of rusted iron, but when he touches it, it crumbles to dust. The wind carries the metallic ash until it forms a crown in the air, which spins like a storm halo.

**Exodus-Rain-Night**

Scene: Warriors sunk to the waist in a quagmire join hands beneath a gigantic moon that fills almost the whole sky. They swear loyalty, but the moon shatters into pieces like broken glass, falling upon them.

**Past–Mission–Ambush**

Scene: A stone bridge stretches over an infinite void. A knight brandishes a gleaming sword, but his shadow separates from his body and wields another weapon, trying to run through his own reflection.

**Flight-Flame-Silence**

Scene: A queen burns in flames, but does not scream. The fire lights up a hall where all the nobles keep silent, motionless as statues. The crown melts and drips incandescent metal onto the steps of the throne.

**War–Lie–Choice**

Scene: Two generals seal the peace beneath torrential rain. The water falls so hard that the ground becomes a mirror reflecting another scene: the same generals, but cutting each other's throats.

For example, in Void–Oath–Ash, an army crosses a river of burning ash and its soldiers disappear one by one. The players may interpret it as an oath that demands renouncing one's identity, as a collective memory of a war that never existed, or in any other coherent way. They must then perform an action that makes that reading material.

The interpretation opens a reality. The action tries to sustain it.

A failed roll: with each failure a new breach opens, and the PCs keep advancing towards another scene, until they manage to piece three scenes back together.

If 12 breaches open, the Mirror collapses and traps the PCs in a false memory.

**THE TYPOGRAPHIC SPHINX**

Each time one of the rolls to stabilise a scar yields a Critical Failure, a Typographic Sphinx comes to life. A creature with a face of glyphs that come undone. A paper body that crackles as it moves. A voice that sounds like a page being torn.

It poses a riddle.

  - Correct answer: it nods with an approving whisper.

  - Failure:

`o` A PC temporarily loses a memory

`o` They receive a State.

Possible riddles for the Typographic Sphinx:

  - What goes up and down but never moves?
Answer: A staircase

  - What is always coming but never arrives?
Answer: The future / Tomorrow

  - What would you fill a wineskin with to make it weigh less?
Answer: Holes

  - What cannot be named without being destroyed?
Answer: Silence

**FREEING DRUSA**

After the last Scars of the Mirror, the space contracts until it once again takes on the shape of the Forgotten Repository. Yet something has changed. The display cases seem farther away and the Mirror now fills almost the entire back wall, as if the chamber had been built around it and not the other way round.

A few metres from its surface, Drusa Malter remains suspended.

She is not, strictly speaking, shut inside a sarcophagus. At first glance she seems surrounded by a translucent structure of smoked glass, but when the PCs draw near they discover that the surface has no continuous matter. It is made of superimposed sentences, hundreds of lines of writing that cross in different directions and, seen from afar, produce the appearance of a solid casing.

The sentences do not stay still.

Some rise from the floor and pass through the structure from bottom to top; others are born directly in the cracks of the Mirror and advance towards Drusa like long written filaments. There are sequences that circle around her body, others that slip beneath her clothes and reappear on the opposite side, as if they did not recognise the difference between surface and depth.

Not all of them can be read from left to right.

One says:

DRUSA MALTER IS THE SUBJECT CONTAINED IN THIS FILE.

But as the eye runs along it, the words begin to shift until they read:

THIS FILE IS THE SUBJECT CONTAINED IN DRUSA MALTER.

A few seconds later it returns to its previous state.

Another sentence seems to begin with its last word. If someone tries to follow it in reverse, the text turns again and places the end out of reach once more. It never allows anyone to establish with certainty where it begins.

In others, the problem is not the order but the reference.

SHE MUST REMAIN HERE FOR AS LONG AS SHE GOES ON BEING HER.

When it is spoken aloud, one of the pronouns darkens. Then another. Finally, each _she_ seems to point to something different: first Drusa, then the Mirror, then the sentence itself.

The LAP continues to detect Drusa's emblem.

When scanned, it shows:

NAME: DRUSA MALTER
STATUS: ACTIVE
LOCATION: UNCLASSIFIABLE

But the result does not remain stable.

If a PC repeats the scan, the field for the Position changes for a few seconds to FILE OBJECT. In another reading it shows WITNESS. In another, HOST.

Then it goes back to showing her ordinary record.

When someone says _Drusa Malter_ aloud, several lines of the prison react at once. The name disappears from the text and is replaced by different expressions:

SUBJECT. FILE. WITNESS. OBJECT. HOST.

Each substitution produces a slight physical alteration in Drusa. When _subject_ appears, she opens her eyes for an instant. With _file_, her skin takes on a faint transparency and small lines of writing can be made out beneath it. With _object_, her body becomes completely motionless. With _host_, something dark seems to move behind her reflection in the Mirror, though not inside her body.

The sequence never occurs exactly the same way twice.

There is, however, one regularity: the more the PCs insist on naming her as a person, the stronger the prison's reaction.

They may also notice that the sentences do not surround the whole body with the same density. Around the chest, the writing forms a kind of knot made of several intertwining clauses. Each comes from a different crack in the Mirror. When one of those cracks gives off a glint, the corresponding sentence tightens and Drusa's body shifts several centimetres towards the glass.

The others put her back in her position.

It is as if several forces were correcting one another to keep her in exactly the same place.

On the floor beneath her there is a circle made of repeated words:

RECORD — IDENTITY — CONTAINMENT — RECORD — IDENTITY — CONTAINMENT

One of the words always appears incomplete.

It is never the same one.

When someone tries to complete the missing word, the others immediately rearrange themselves to open another gap.

The prison seems to need a lack.

Each time the PCs try to approach Drusa physically, another reaction arises. The outer lines shift towards the intruder and begin to write over whatever they touch. They cause no wound, but they do momentarily alter its classification. A bracelet may appear on the LAP as a _document_. A Relic may become, for several seconds, a _testimony_. One of the weapons may be registered as a _pending incident_.

If a line reaches a PC's skin, their name disappears for an instant from their own LAP interface.

Then it returns.

This does not look like an attack.

It looks like an administrative procedure carried out by a diseased grammar.

At some point, Drusa moves her lips.

She makes no sound.

Yet a new sentence appears on the surface of the prison:

DO NOT READ WHAT IT SAYS ABOUT ME.

The line lasts barely a few seconds.

Then another sentence crosses it and replaces _me_ with:

HER.

Next, a third adds:

SHE IS NOT A PERSON.

And a fourth corrects:

A PERSON IS NOT HER.

The Mirror answers with a pulse of light.

The lines tighten again.

Drusa rises a few centimetres.

Then the PCs may notice something else: all the sentences seem to try to describe her, classify her or define her relationship with the place, but none describes what Drusa was doing when she became trapped.

There is a subject. There is a status. There is a function. There is belonging. But there is no action.

It is as if the prison could preserve what Drusa is, or what it considers her to be, but had completely lost the verb that connected her to the world.

And even so, at the bottom of the structure, almost hidden between two superimposed lines, a fragment appears for an instant that seems different from the rest.

It is not a definition.

It is an incomplete action:

DRUSA ______ THE MIRROR.

The space between the two words remains empty.

When one of the PCs tries to complete it, the whole prison reacts.

The sentences stop moving for a second.

The LAP loses its connection.

The Mirror darkens.

And Drusa opens her eyes.

Only for an instant.

Then the entire structure violently activates again.

Now there is no longer any doubt that this is not a passive prison.

It is defending an interpretation of Drusa.

And any attempt to free her demands first disputing that interpretation.

**Objective of the confrontation**

The players must declare what they intend to achieve. The most obvious aim will be to free Drusa without destroying her identity, but they may attempt other things: recovering her consciousness even if the body remains trapped, breaking the link with the Mirror, modifying her classification, or extracting only some part of her memory.

The objective changes the way in which they will have to intervene on the anchors.

**Possible pregenerated anchors**

Identity reclassification. The prison holds Drusa by continually changing her category between subject, file, object, host and witness. Her inability to keep a single condition seems to prevent her from fully recovering her status as a person.

Syntactic inversion. The sentences swap subject and object, beginning and end, container and contents. The structure seems to sustain itself through these inversions and reacts when someone tries to fix a stable reading.

Specular dependence. Several lines are born directly from the cracks of the Mirror and tighten when it emits pulses. Drusa seems physically held up by different bonds that come from those fractures.

Verbal void. The structure can define who Drusa is but not what she was doing. The sentence DRUSA ______ THE MIRROR constitutes a persistent absence that produces an extraordinary reaction when someone tries to complete it.

Necessary defect. The circle on the floor always keeps one incomplete word; when one is restored, another loses part of itself. The prison seems to require a permanent lack in order to hold together.

Contagious classification. The sentences can extend the same process to the PCs and their objects, temporarily changing their status. The prison does not only contain: it tries to integrate everything that intervenes on it into its own system of categories.

These anchors are possible readings, not mandatory solutions. The players might interpret, for example, that the true anchor is not the reclassification itself but the prison's inability to recognise an action; that the cracks of the Mirror compete with one another and that opposition keeps Drusa suspended; or that the circle does not need a missing word, but the continuous act of losing one. If the interpretation starts from something described, explains an observable behaviour and does not contradict the scene, it can be brought into play.

**Transformations in the face of Critical Failure**

A Critical Failure should not merely prevent the liberation. The confrontation changes.

The prison may complete DRUSA ______ THE MIRROR with an unfavourable word and turn it into a provisional truth: _belongs to_, _contains_, _obeys_, _replaces_. It may erase Drusa's name from every LAP and turn the recovery of her identity into the new objective. It may classify one of the PCs as HOST, partially incorporating them into the structure. It may also separate one of Drusa's memories and materialise it as an independent figure claiming to be the real researcher.

If the Critical Failure directly compromises the PC's coherence, Unanchored or Fragmented would be especially fitting consequences, depending on the nature of the intervention; the current system provides precisely these two States for losses of contextual fixation or ruptures of internal continuity.

**IMBALANCE OF THE GEARWORK**

If the Plot Clock reaches four during Investigation or Action, suspend the Phase and use a brief mini-adventure.

The PCs appear in a version of Numinia where they remember having accepted another mission related to the same conflict.

Use Resonance to establish what mission it was, who occupied Drusa's place, what different relationship they had with one of the councillors, what important Piece never existed in this version, and what event shows that this alternative memory contains something that could be true.

When a significant recontextualisation occurs, the Veil heals and they return.

Do not hand them a solution. Hand them a new way of fearing the ones they already had.

## **Resolution Phase**

**THE RETURN TO THE COUNCIL**

After the Action, the PCs return to the Veiled Chamber with whatever is left of the Mirror, with Drusa or her resulting condition, and with the theory they have built.

The four Prism Rolls replace the old Balance of the Council. Each one poses a specific question and, when necessary, alters the next.

**Alethic Roll: the existence of the Mirror**

_Can the Mirror go on existing as a stable structure without keeping the memory fracture active?_

On a Success, the Mirror acquires enough stability to remain as an object or enclave. On a Critical Failure, it cannot keep a single form: it splits, dematerialises or begins to transfer its properties to other supports.

If it succeeds, the Deontic Roll will decide what must be done with it. If it fails, it will decide what must be saved from that which is ceasing to exist.

**Deontic Roll: the norm**

If the Mirror remains, the PCs must propose:

What rule should govern it: closure, shared study, institutional custody, limited access or conditional future destruction?

On a Success, the norm comes close to what the PCs intended. On a Critical Failure, a serious restriction appears, an institutional appropriation, or a condition that contradicts part of their objectives.

If the Mirror is disappearing, the PCs must decide what should be preserved: a fragment, a memory, a Piece, Drusa, a record or any relevant element. On a Success they preserve it; on a Critical Failure they can only keep it by sacrificing something else of similar importance.

**Epistemic Roll: what can be known**

_What will Numinia retain as knowledge of what happened?_

On a Success, the PCs' memories remain coherent and there is at least one piece of external evidence supporting their account. On a Critical Failure, knowledge fragments: the players determine one thing that remains undoubtedly true and the GD establishes another that is left open again, contradictory or impossible to prove.

The result alters the final assessment. If there was a Success, Numinia will be able to judge on relatively firm facts. If there was a Critical Failure, it will judge on accounts, testimonies and rumours.

**Axiological Roll: the meaning of the Mirror**

_What value does the Broken Mirror acquire for Numinia?_

If the Epistemic Roll was a Success and this Roll is also a Success, the interpretation defended by the PCs becomes the predominant cultural framework: historical wound, exceptional archive, warning, dangerous technology, heritage, plural memory, or whatever meaning has been born from the adventure.

If the Epistemic was a Success but the Axiological fails, the facts are known but their assessment deeply divides the city.

If the Epistemic failed and the Axiological is a Success, the PCs' version becomes the most influential account even though it cannot be fully proven.

If both fail, the Mirror definitively enters the Rumourology of Numinia.

## **Epilogue**

The Veiled Chamber apparently keeps the same appearance as at the beginning.

Now bring back several contributions from the initial Resonance. The object one of the councillors was toying with, the presence in the upper gallery, the detail that foreshadowed a disaster, or whatever reminded a PC of an episode from their life should reappear, transformed in some way.

If Drusa is there, her LAP record must reflect the consequences of the adventure.

If she is not, it may be far more unsettling for her to keep appearing as:

DRUSA MALTER — PRESENT.

Before the end, one of the PCs may search for _trakmósis_ on their LAP again.

This time there is an entry.

Do not write it.

Ask the players:

«After everything that has happened, what definition does Numinia now show for the word _trakmósis_?»

The answer becomes part of the world.

#### **Seeds of Knowledge**

In this adventure, some moments especially favourable for the appearance of Seeds of Knowledge are:

  - The study of the Möbius Corridor, its spatial alterations and the rules that govern its behaviour may produce Green Seeds, of lesser value.

  - Understanding the Mirror, its Scars, or the relationships between identity, memory and representation may bring about Golden Seeds, of considerable value.

  - Discoveries concerning Drusa, her history and what truly happened to her may generate Silver Seeds, when they broaden knowledge about individuals, societies and human relationships.

  - The interpretation of documents, testimonies, remains of the past or events that make it possible to reconstruct forgotten facts may provide Blue Seeds.

#### **Prism Cells**

The appearance of Prism Cells should be far more exceptional. Cells are not an abstract reward that appears automatically after a good Roll: they are real treasures of Numinia, found by chance along the PCs' path.

The GD may introduce one in recognition of an especially creative intervention, an extraordinarily well-executed solution, or an exploration that has led the characters to an unexpected place. The reward must always be woven into the fiction: perhaps the action reveals a hidden cavity, opens an old compartment, gives access to an area that had been sealed for decades, or catches the prismatic gleam among the remains of a structure.

Decode Surroundings Rolls are particularly suited to this. When they are made in places where it is plausible to find vestiges, abandoned repositories, forgotten chambers or ancient accumulations of objects, the GD may include a Prism Cell among the discoveries. An especially fertile Success may reveal what no one was looking for.

It is best to keep them scarce. Finding a Cell should retain something of a fortunate accident, almost a small archaeology of chance. The Prism, after all, has the dreadful habit of hiding its treasures precisely where no one had the courtesy to look for them.
